## 7. Verdict

- BROKER PATH WORKS FROM THE VM. One live batch (observe + click + type) ran end to end over the broker: start, observe, act (ok, 2/2, text visible in the field), finish/cleanup. The S1 proof-first gate is MET for the desktop batch. Answer to the probe question: yes, the VM can drive a live batch through the broker.
- Decomposition of the 16.59s delta: almost all of it is TRANSPORT, not the broker. Fresh SSH is 4.46-5.45s per call vs mux 0.98-1.36s (about 3.5s saved per call, 4 calls in the core batch). Fresh scp is 8.1-9.4s per file vs 4.6-4.9s over mux. The per-batch session spawn itself is small: the one-shot driver invocation (connect + spawn + 3 actions + teardown) took 5.375s total, of which the fresh connect is about 4.5s, leaving about 0.9s for spawn plus act plus teardown. The adjudication range for the spawn tax (0.4-2.1s) is confirmed in shape, but it is the smallest of the three taxes. The broker's own per-command cost is near zero (round trips equal plain mux ssh; laptop work 0.08-0.26s per command). The adjudication expectation "per-batch mechanism about 0.5s" holds for laptop-side work (act: 71+109+78 = 258ms) but the VM-visible per-batch cost is about 4.3s (3 mux round trips) until the channel carries more per round trip or the image stops traveling.
- SCP IS THE HIDDEN TAX (measured 4 samples over a live master, 53-57KB files): scp costs 4.57-4.93s EVEN OVER A LIVE MULTIPLEXED MASTER (4.927, 4.566, 4.684, 4.570). Fresh scp costs 8.10-9.38s (8.499, 8.104, 9.378, 8.498). So scp is roughly half connect, half transfer: S0 multiplexing alone does NOT fix scp. Only deleting scp as transport (S1/A6/D2: JSON over the channel) removes the about 4.6s transfer tax. This strengthens the S1 case beyond the adjudication framing, which priced scp as "a fresh about 5s SSH".
- ControlMaster liveness (measured): masters WITHOUT keepalive die after about 1-2 minutes idle and unlink their socket (master 1 pid 11815, master 2 pid 13600, both confirmed dead with socket gone). Master 3 (pid 14865, ServerAliveInterval=15) survived the whole broker arm, about 4 minutes with gaps up to about 60s, and was stopped deliberately at the end. Keepalive looks effective over a 4-minute window, but the 1-day soak the adjudication requires has not run. The S1 path needs the per-use liveness check plus restart-on-death as the normal path (my mux.sh guard; it never fired during the arm).
- BLOCKERS AND GATING CONDITIONS for deleting the per-batch spawn (all measured, none papered over):
  1. SYNC THE LAPTOP TREE FIRST. Laptop HEAD 56f5af4 vs VM HEAD 49081aa. Measured consequences: no act-now/first_action.py on the laptop (the specified one-shot comparison path does not exist there); laptop cu.py and session_driver.py default the browser endpoint to 9222, so `browser observe` through the broker 404s and fatally ends the session. The broker loads code from the laptop checkout. Run sync-installed.py (or equivalent) and re-verify `browser observe` through the broker before any deletion.
  2. KEEP FRESH-SSH ONE-SHOT AS FALLBACK AND RECOVERY. The broker dies, the master dies, the socket can go stale. The one-shot driver path (O5r, 5.375s for a whole batch) is the recovery path and must stay documented.
  3. MASTER WATCHDOG with restart-on-death plus the 1-day soak for ServerAliveInterval=15.
  4. DECIDE THE FATAL-ON-READ-FAILURE BEHAVIOR. A failed read-only `browser observe` ended the whole broker session. Either make read-only failures non-fatal (transient) or keep browser commands out of the broker until the endpoint is fixed.
  5. SINGLE-OWNER SERIALIZATION under concurrent VM workers is still unexamined (the broker is single-task; a second start gets `busy`). VM-side queueing is not built. Do not run two broker batches concurrently until that is decided.
