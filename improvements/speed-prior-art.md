## How to read the verdicts

- APPLIES / YES / PROTOTYPE: directly testable in this setup with the current executor and hardware.
- APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS / MAYBE: testable, but only after a named check (a usage record, a VRAM fit test, a held-out run).
- DOES NOT APPLY / NO / DEFER: blocked by the setup (needs an API key that is out of credit, needs training, needs a GPU this laptop does not have, or only helps throughput at batch sizes this loop never reaches).
- NEGATIVE / DO NOT REDO / DO NOT DO: prior work already proved this fails or backfires for speed. All of them are consolidated in section 15.
- UNVERIFIED: a claimed number with no primary source found, or a single-source claim far above all peers. Listed in section 16 and never used as ranking evidence.
- MEASURED means the number comes from a cited source on that source's benchmark, or from a laptop or VM timing in a source report. ESTIMATE means a calculation from the baseline above, labelled as such. UNMEASURED means the source did not report it and no number was invented.
- A note on benchmark numbers: OSWorld, WebArena, Mind2Web and WebShop success rates are NOT think-time numbers. A method can raise success a lot while costing MORE think time (more calls, more tokens, more rollouts). This survey keeps success and speed separate in every entry, because the track goal is speed, not success.

## 1. Reasoning effort and test-time compute (the direct lever on think time)

### 1.1 OpenAI reasoning_effort dial (o3-mini, Jan 2025; GPT-6.1 Sol measurements, Oct 2026)

- Source: OpenAI o3-mini launch, 2025-01-31 (reasoning_effort low/medium/high); SeedRouter blog, "GPT-6.1 Sol Reasoning Effort: Low vs Medium vs High vs Max", tests run 2026-10-05. SECONDARY for the SeedRouter numbers (blog, not a paper).
- Tried: the same short prompts sent at each effort level (low, medium, high, xhigh, max), measuring seconds to complete answer, reasoning tokens, output tokens, cost, correctness, and time to first visible text.
- Measured: on short tasks every effort level gave the right answer, but max took about 10x as long to show its first word as low and used 2.5x to 5x the tokens of low. One streamed test: first text after 3.0s at low, 13.6s at medium, 30.6s at max; total time 20.5s (low), 24.7s (medium), 43.5s (max); reasoning tokens 56 (low), 824 (medium), 2,517 (max). A second task: low 17.5s and 473 reasoning tokens, medium 27.6s and 1,031, max 42.6s and 2,070. The author warns these are single runs on short tasks, not a benchmark. OpenAI's own numbers: at medium effort o3-mini already matches the larger o1 on hard math and science.
- Applies: YES, highest priority. This is the single most direct lever on our 94 percent think share. Our executor runs at max reasoning effort today. The dial exists in the Codex CLI as model_reasoning_effort. Test per step type on the same held-out tasks: low or medium for windows, observe, and verify steps; medium or high only for the act-composition step, and only if low fails. Measure think gap per step, not just total wall.
- Open question (from the prototype plan): does Codex CLI accept a per-call effort override, or only a session-level config? If session-level only, run level 2 and level 3 as separate Codex invocations with different flags. The prototype must answer this before claiming the saving.

### 1.2 s1: Simple test-time scaling, budget forcing (Muennighoff et al., arXiv 2501.19393, Jan 2025)

- Source: Muennighoff, Yang, et al., "s1: Simple test-time scaling", arXiv 2501.19393, 2025-01-31.
- Tried: fine-tune Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on only 1,000 curated reasoning traces (s1K, 26 minutes of training on 16 H100 GPUs), then control test-time compute with budget forcing: forcefully end thinking by appending the end-of-thinking delimiter when the token budget is exceeded, or lengthen thinking by appending "Wait" so the model double-checks.
- Measured: s1-32B exceeds o1-preview on AIME24 and MATH by up to 27 percent (paper abstract). Test-time scaling curves show accuracy rising with thinking tokens up to the tested budgets on AIME24, MATH500, and GPQA Diamond.
- Applies: PARTLY. The budget-forcing trick (cap thinking tokens, append "Wait" to extend) is a decode-time control idea that maps onto the reasoning_effort dial and max-output-tokens caps we already have, so no new machinery is needed to test the cheap version. The fine-tune itself DOES NOT APPLY: it needs 16 H100s and a 32B model, and our executor is a closed Codex model we cannot fine-tune. Do not chase the fine-tune; test the cap.

### 1.3 DeepSeek-R1 and test-time compute scaling (DeepSeek, arXiv 2501.12948, Jan 2025)

- Source: DeepSeek-AI, "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning", arXiv 2501.12948, Jan 2025.
- Tried: pure reinforcement learning (GRPO, no supervised fine-tune) to make the model spend more tokens thinking (R1-Zero), then a distilled pipeline for R1.
- Measured: R1-Zero went from 15.6 percent to 71.0 percent pass@1 on AIME 2024 with zero architecture change, just more thinking tokens; majority voting pushed it to 86.7 percent. R1 reached 79.8 percent on AIME 2024, comparable to o1-1217.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY as an implementation (we cannot retrain the Codex executor), but the measurement is the key evidence for the track pivot: thinking tokens are the dominant cost driver, and they scale accuracy only up to a point (see 1.4). Use it as justification for capping think, not for training.

### 1.4 When More Thinking Hurts (Zhou et al., arXiv 2604.10739, Apr 2026) - NEGATIVE for uncapped thinking

- Source: Shu Zhou, Rui Ling, Junan Chen, Xin Wang, Tao Fan, Hao Wang, "When More Thinking Hurts: Overthinking in LLM Test-Time Compute Scaling", arXiv 2604.10739, 2026-04-12.
- Tried: vary the reasoning-token budget on R1-32B and s1-32B across math benchmarks and count correct-to-wrong flips as the budget grows.
- Measured: accuracy peaks around 1,024 tokens on several benchmarks, then flattens or reverses. Marginal utility fell from +1.8 percent per 500 tokens in the 2,000 to 4,000 range to +0.1 percent between 8,000 and 12,000, then turned negative. Past about 7,000 tokens the model flipped more previously correct answers to wrong than wrong to right. Flip ratio (wrong over right): 1.09 at 7,000 tokens, 1.42 at 8,000, 3.29 at 12,000, 7.55 at 16,000. The 32B run peaked at 55.8 percent accuracy at 12,000 tokens and fell to 54.9 percent at 16,000. Easy MATH-500 Level 1-2 items peaked near 1,500 to 2,000 tokens; hard Level 5 items kept improving to roughly 7,500 to 8,000 tokens.
- Applies: YES, as a guardrail. This is the paper that justifies a per-step thinking cap and a difficulty-dependent budget: easy steps (windows, observe, verify) get a small budget, hard steps (act composition after a confusing screen) get a larger one. It also predicts our P1 outlier (68.2s think before the first act) may have been overthinking, not a slow network. Test: cap reasoning effort per step type and count flips and failures on held-out tasks.

### 1.5 Overthinking measured at scale (Guan et al., Oct 2026) - SECONDARY

- Source: Guan et al., Oct 2026, via the hxallai.com research review (SECONDARY summary). 92,448 reasoning traces sampled from 6 models (GPT-OSS-20B, Qwen3 thinking variants, DeepSeek-R1 distilled series) across 4 benchmark suites.
- Tried: find, for each trace, the point where accuracy first peaks, and count how many thinking tokens come after that point.
- Measured: on average 64.3 percent of thinking tokens appear AFTER accuracy has already peaked. Thinking all the way to the end is on average 9.03 percentage points LESS accurate than stopping at the right moment. Thinking to the end averaged 71.78 percent accuracy at an average cost of 8,281 thinking tokens. On MATH500 the overthinking ratio reached 78.8 percent with only 1.83 points of degradation; on GPQA the ratio was 61.4 percent but degradation reached 16.98 points.
- Applies: YES, same guardrail as 1.4, stronger numbers. Supports early stopping of reasoning: if the model has already produced a confident action, extra thinking is pure latency and can even hurt. Practical test: stop the act step at the first complete valid action instead of letting the model deliberate further (needs a validity check, not a second model call).

### 1.6 Claude extended thinking, budget_tokens (Anthropic, Feb 2025)

- Source: Anthropic, Claude 3.7 Sonnet extended thinking, Feb 2025; reported in the blazity.com reasoning-levels review (SECONDARY for the numbers).
- Tried: a numeric thinking budget (budget_tokens, 1,024 up to tens of thousands) instead of a low/medium/high dial.
- Measured: logarithmic accuracy gains on AIME math as the budget increases. With 256 independent samples and a 64K-token thinking budget, Claude 3.7 Sonnet reached 84.8 percent on GPQA, including 96.5 percent on physics. Gains flatten: each doubling buys less.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY directly (we do not run Claude as the executor), but it confirms the dial shape: numeric budget, log gains, flat tail. Useful if the executor ever switches to a model with a numeric budget. No action now.

### 1.7 Do not Think Twice: over-reasoning impairs confidence calibration (arXiv 2508.15050, Aug 2025)

- Source: "Do not Think Twice! Over-Reasoning Impairs Confidence Calibration", arXiv 2508.15050, Aug 2025 (cited in the temperature2.com review).
- Tried: extended reasoning and its effect on the model's own confidence signalling.
- Measured: extended reasoning specifically hurts the model's ability to accurately signal how confident it should be in its own answer (directional finding reported in the review; exact numbers not re-verified here).
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. If any prototype uses the model's self-reported confidence as a routing or acceptance signal (see section 5, cascades), long reasoning makes that signal less trustworthy. Prefer external checks (schema validity, screen state, command exit code) over self-confidence. Do not build a router on the model's own confidence after a max-effort think.

## 2. Prompt and context reduction, prompt caching, KV-cache reuse

### 2.1 LLMLingua (Jiang et al., Microsoft, arXiv 2309.17453, Oct 2023, EMNLP 2023)

- Source: Huiqiang Jiang, Qianhui Wu, Chin-Yew Lin, Yuqing Yang, Lili Qiu, "LLMLingua: Compressing Prompts for Accelerated Inference of Large Language Models", arXiv 2309.17453, Oct 2023.
- Tried: coarse-to-fine prompt compression with a small language model (GPT-2 or LLaMA) scoring token importance: drop low-importance tokens from instructions, questions, and demonstrations.
- Measured: up to 20x compression with only a 1.5-point performance drop (GSM8K exact-match fell 1.44 points at 14x and 1.52 points at 20x). Wall-clock latency on a single V100 GPU, GSM8K: 8.6s uncompressed; 4.9s at 2x compression (1.7x speedup); 2.3s at 5x (3.3x); 1.3s at 10x (5.7x). Compressor overhead is included in those end-to-end numbers.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS, and with a strong caveat (see 2.5). The speedup is real but was measured on self-hosted models where prefill is the bottleneck. Our executor is a remote Codex model, so local compression only helps if the prompt is long enough that prefill dominates the round trip, and only if the compressor runs faster than the time it saves. Our prompts are short (a few thousand tokens plus one screenshot), so the break-even is doubtful. Test only if prompt length grows; do not assume the 5.7x transfers.

### 2.2 LongLLMLingua (Jiang et al., Microsoft, arXiv 2310.06839, Oct 2023)

- Source: Jiang et al., "LongLLMLingua: Accelerating and Enhancing LLMs in Long Context Scenarios via Prompt Compression", arXiv 2310.06839, Oct 2023.
- Tried: question-aware compression for long contexts: rank documents by relevance to the question, allocate compression budget by rank, keep more tokens where the answer likely sits.
- Measured: NaturalQuestions, about 4x fewer tokens, performance up 21.4 percent (75.0 percent vs 54.1 percent original when ground truth was at the 10th position). LongBench at 6x compression: 48.3 percent vs 44.0 percent original. Cost reduction 94.0 percent on LooGLE. End-to-end latency on about 10k-token prompts at 2x to 6x compression: 1.4x to 2.6x speedup (LongBench table: 10.0s at 3x vs 16.0s-ish baseline, 6.1s at 6x for 2.6x speedup).
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the current loop (our prompts are not 10k-token documents and there is no retrieval ranking step), but the mechanism matters for one future case: if history grows to many turns, question-aware compression of old observations beats blind truncation. Keep as a later option, not a first prototype.

### 2.3 LLMLingua-2 (Pan et al., Microsoft, arXiv 2403.12968, Mar 2024, Findings of ACL 2024)

- Source: Pan, Wu, Jiang, et al., "LLMLingua-2: Data Distillation for Efficient and Faithful Task-Agnostic Prompt Compression", arXiv 2403.12968, Mar 2024.
- Tried: reframe compression as token classification with a small bidirectional encoder (XLM-RoBERTa-large), trained by distillation, instead of a causal small model.
- Measured: 3x to 6x faster than existing prompt compression methods; end-to-end latency speedup 1.6x to 2.9x at 2x to 5x compression. MeetingBank latency on a V100-32G: 14.9s uncompressed; 9.4s at 2x (1.6x), 7.5s at 3x (2.1x), 5.2s at 5x (2.9x). Compressor overhead 0.4s to 0.5s vs 1.5s to 2.9s for LLMLingua-1.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS, same caveat as 2.1, but this is the best compressor candidate if compression is ever tested: lowest overhead, task-agnostic, small encoder that could run on the laptop CPU or the RTX 4050. Still gated on prompt length (see 2.5).

### 2.4 RECOMP (Xu et al., 2023)

- Source: Xu et al., "RECOMP: Improving Retrieval-Augmented LMs with Compression and Selective Augmentation", 2023. Numbers below from the EdenAI comparison post, 2026-08-07 (SECONDARY).
- Tried: extractive compression (return key sentences verbatim) or abstractive compression (small model summarizes retrieved text) before the main model sees it.
- Measured (EdenAI head-to-head, RAG task): RECOMP extractive 5x compression at 80ms compressor latency; abstractive 8x at 350ms; LLMLingua-2 8x at 150ms; LongLLMLingua 10x at 200ms. On code understanding, RECOMP was fastest end-to-end (1.8s) but lowest correctness (68.9 percent vs 78.5 percent uncompressed).
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the current loop (no retrieval step). The extractive variant is the cheapest compressor shape ever measured (80ms), which is a useful datum if a compression prototype is built: start extractive, not abstractive.

### 2.5 Prompt Compression in the Wild (Kummer, Jurkschat, Farber, arXiv 2604.02985, Apr 2026) - the reality check

- Source: Cornelius Kummer, Lena Jurkschat, Michael Farber, "Prompt Compression in the Wild: Measuring Latency, Rate Adherence, and Quality for Faster LLM Inference", arXiv 2604.02985, Apr 2026.
- Tried: the first systematic large-scale study of the trade-off: 30,000-plus end-to-end runs, 5 open-source model setups (7B to 70B) plus 2 proprietary APIs, 3 GPU classes (A100, GTX 1080 Ti, Apple M1 Pro), prompt lengths 100 to 50,000 tokens, compression ratios 1.5x and up. Compression overhead timed separately from decoding latency.
- Measured: LLMLingua achieves up to 18 percent end-to-end speedup when prompt length, compression ratio, and hardware are well matched, with response quality statistically unchanged. OUTSIDE that window the compression step dominates and cancels out the gains. Largest gains only once prompts exceed about 5k tokens. Memory use falls up to 75 percent. Compression can move a workload off a data-center GPU onto a commodity card with only a 0.3s latency increase. The authors ship an open-source profiler that predicts the latency break-even point per model and hardware pair.
- Applies: YES, as the decision rule. This paper is the reason standalone prompt compression is ranked low for our loop: our per-step prompts are short (system prompt plus one screenshot plus a short history), almost certainly below the 5k-token break-even, so compression overhead would eat the saving. DO NOT build a compression prototype before measuring prompt token counts per step and running their break-even logic. This is the strongest NEGATIVE-adjacent evidence in this survey: the headline 5.7x from 2.1 does not survive contact with short prompts and real overhead. The synthesis log already records this as NEGATIVE (superseded by masking); this survey independently confirms it from the primary source.

### 2.6 CORE: lightweight prompt compression for edge devices (arXiv 2606.20571, 2026)

- Source: "Less is More: Lightweight Prompt Compression for Question Answering Applications on Edge Devices", arXiv 2606.20571, 2026.
- Tried: CORE, a two-stage sentence-level compression using named entity recognition plus semantic matching, with no small language model at all. Tested on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin and a Huawei Nova smartphone.
- Measured: within a 2,000-token budget, accuracy up at least 30.19 percent vs state-of-the-art baselines, memory down at least 50.47 percent, at least 1.94x speedup on the edge device. For a 37.5k-token context, LongLLMLingua took 668.34s and LLMLingua 341.24s, while CORE took 6.77s. Energy down 95.74 percent vs LLMLingua-2 on the smartphone.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the current loop (QA with retrieval, not GUI actions), but the 341s vs 6.77s contrast is a warning: heavy compressors are unusable in a latency loop, and any compression candidate must be timed including its own overhead. If compression is ever prototyped, CORE's no-SLM design is the shape to copy, not LLMLingua-1.

### 2.7 Coca-Cola: latent context compression for agents (Liu et al., arXiv 2611.06500, Nov 2026) - very new

- Source: Yuetong Liu, Zhehao Zhou, Yitong Zhang, Haorui Chen, Jia Li, "Tools Need Not Speak in Text: Latent Context Compression for Agents", arXiv 2611.06500, submitted 2026-11-04. Numbers via the qelvanotech.blogspot.com summary, 2026-11-17 (SECONDARY).
- Tried: compress tool observations into compact latent tokens (continuous vectors in the agent model's embedding space) instead of text, trained with Action-Preserving Training so the agent's next-action distribution is unchanged.
- Measured: token consumption down 70.01 percent to 89.24 percent, end-to-end runtime down 24.45 percent to 55.97 percent, task performance consistently IMPROVED, on web-search agents across 4 QA benchmarks and 2 model families.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY now (needs training a compression model against the agent model's embedding space, and our executor is a closed model whose embeddings we cannot touch). Flag as a research direction to re-check in 6 months if an open-weight executor is ever adopted. Do not prototype.

### 2.8 Anthropic prompt caching (Anthropic, Aug 2024)

- Source: Anthropic API prompt caching, public beta Aug 2024, docs at docs.anthropic.com. Numbers from Anthropic's launch materials via the gist.ly and aibase.com summaries (SECONDARY).
- Tried: cache the processed prefix (system prompt, tool definitions, long documents) server-side with explicit cache_control breakpoints; reuse across requests within a 5-minute TTL (1-hour option later).
- Measured: cost down up to 90 percent on cached tokens (cache reads at 0.1x base input, writes at 1.25x base). Latency down up to 85 percent on long prompts. Anthropic's examples: a 100,000-token prompt answered in 2.4s cached vs 11.5s uncached (about 80 percent faster); a 10,000-token few-shot prompt 31 percent faster; a 10-turn conversation 75 percent faster.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY directly (executor is Codex, not Claude), but it sets the ceiling for what prefix caching can do in an agentic loop, and it proves the mechanism matters most when the same long prefix repeats every turn, which is exactly our loop shape (same system prompt and tool definitions every step).

### 2.9 OpenAI prompt caching (OpenAI, Oct 2024)

- Source: OpenAI prompt caching docs and "Prompt Caching 101" cookbook, developers.openai.com, Oct 2024 onward.
- Tried: automatic server-side caching of prompt prefixes over 1,024 tokens, in 128-token increments. No code change needed. Cache hits visible in usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens.
- Measured: latency down up to 80 percent for prompts over 10,000 tokens; cached-token cost down 50 percent on older models, 90 percent (0.1x) on GPT-5.6 and later. Cache lifetime 5 to 10 minutes of inactivity by default (up to 1 hour off-peak, up to 24 hours on request for some models).
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS, and it is a free check, not a build. The Codex CLI talks to OpenAI servers, so our repeated system prompt may ALREADY be cached server-side if the prefix is stable and over 1,024 tokens. OPEN CHECK (from the synthesis log): read one Codex usage record and look for cached-token fields; if caching is already hitting, there is nothing to build, and if the prefix is unstable (timestamps, changing window lists at the front), fix the prefix order so the stable part comes first and the dynamic part last. Cache TTL (5 to 10 minutes) comfortably covers our 36s tasks. Blocked only by the out-of-credit API key for direct API tests; the Codex CLI path can still be inspected from its own usage output.

### 2.10 Anthropic Message Threads: incremental transport (Anthropic, Nov 2026)

- Source: Anthropic Message Threads, launched 2026-11-02, enabled by default in Claude Code. Numbers via the yage.ai analysis, 2026-11-12 (SECONDARY).
- Tried: keep conversation state server-side at the API gateway, so each turn sends only the new content instead of re-uploading the full history; prompt caching still applies on the GPU side.
- Measured: median per-turn request payload in long conversations shrank from roughly 800 KB to 1.8 KB. Eliminating redundant uploads, schema validation, and re-tokenization at the gateway cuts median TTFT by roughly 35 percent on requests of 500k to 1M tokens. Token consumption and bills are UNCHANGED (the model still reads the full history every turn). For computer-use tasks with a screenshot every step, one CTO estimate cited 191 MB cumulative upload stateless vs 7.5 MB with incremental transport.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY directly (Claude-only, and it does not cut tokens or model think), but it names a real cost our loop also pays: re-sending the full history plus a fresh screenshot every turn. If the Codex API ever offers an equivalent stateful session, adopt it. Until then, the local equivalent is masking (section 3.1): send less history.

### 2.11 vLLM PagedAttention and automatic prefix caching (Kwon et al., 2023; vLLM docs, 2024 onward)

- Source: Woosuk Kwon et al., "Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention", SOSP 2023 (arXiv 2309.06180). Prefix-caching numbers from independent 2026 writeups (SECONDARY): a vLLM v0.6.3 run on a single RTX 4090 24GB with Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct AWQ 4-bit, and the ninadpathak.com inference-optimization review citing NVIDIA's Nov 2026 GLM-5.3 runs and the Modelplane Open Field Guide.
- Tried: allocate the KV cache in 16-token pages on demand (PagedAttention) instead of contiguous blocks; hash full blocks and reuse them automatically when a new request shares a prefix.
- Measured: vLLM paper: only 20.4 percent to 38.2 percent of KV-cache memory held real tokens before PagedAttention; after, 96.3 percent. At the same latency, 2x to 4x the throughput of FasterTransformer and Orca; up to 24x effective throughput over naive static batching in official benchmarks. Prefix caching on the RTX 4090 test: identical 4,000-token system prompt, TTFT 1,298.5ms uncached vs 186.4ms cached (85.6 percent cut); total request 6.18s vs 5.03s. The Modelplane guide reports about two-thirds of prompt tokens hit the prefix cache in coding-agent runs, because agents resend their transcript every turn. NVIDIA's GLM-5.3 table shows throughput rising to 607.8 tok/s at 16 concurrent requests then falling to 385.0 at 32: prefix caching helps, but saturation reverses gains.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS, for the LOCAL model path only. vLLM prefix caching is the local equivalent of 2.9, and it is what makes a small local grounder or router cheap (see section 13). It does NOT help the remote Codex executor at all. If a local model is ever served with vLLM on the RTX 4050, turn prefix caching on (it is on by default) and keep the system prompt byte-identical across turns. Note the VRAM limit: a 7B model at Q4 plus KV cache fits in 6GB only with a short context, so test fit before assuming the cache survives.

### 2.12 Long context is expensive even with caching (NVIDIA Research, 2026, via yage.ai)

- Source: NVIDIA Research long-context study, 2026, reported in the yage.ai analysis of Anthropic Message Threads (SECONDARY).
- Tried: measure GPU cost per generated token as active context grows, on H100 data-center GPUs and DGX Spark desktop workstations.
- Measured: stretching active context from 16K to 256K tokens drives GPU cost per generated output token up 3.6x to 7x in aggregate H100 benchmarks (up to 7x in the yage.ai summary). On DGX Spark, single-token generation latency in the latter half of long runs roughly doubles on some models.
- Applies: YES, as a second justification for masking (section 3.1). Even with perfect KV-cache reuse, a long history makes every generated token slower because the GPU must read the whole prefix cache for every new token. Short history is not just cheaper, it is faster per token. This is the mechanism behind the OSWorld-Human finding that later steps take 3x longer (section 3.4).

## 3. Observation masking, history management, observation compression

### 3.1 The Complexity Trap: observation masking vs LLM summarization (Lindenbauer, JetBrains Research, arXiv 2508.21433, Aug 2025)

- Source: Tobias Lindenbauer, "The Complexity Trap: Simple Observation Masking Is as Efficient as LLM Summarization for Agent Context Management", arXiv 2508.21433, Aug 2025. Code and data: github.com/JetBrains-Research/the-complexity-trap. Cited in both the latency and grounding surveys; merged here as one entry.
- Tried: SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified across 5 model configurations, comparing 3 context strategies: raw agent (full history), LLM summarization (OpenHands/Cursor style, an LLM condenses old turns), and simple observation masking (drop older tool outputs, keep reasoning and actions in full). Plus a hybrid of masking plus summarization.
- Measured: observation tokens make up around 84 percent of an average SWE-agent turn. Both masking and summarization more than halve cost vs the raw agent. Masking HALVES cost relative to the raw agent while matching, and sometimes slightly exceeding, the solve rate of LLM summarization. The hybrid cuts cost a further 7 percent vs masking alone and 11 percent vs summarization alone.
- Applies: YES, top 3 priority. This is the cheapest big win in the whole survey: masking needs zero extra model calls (summarization needs one, which costs think time), and it attacks exactly the token type that dominates a turn. Direct mapping to our loop: keep only the latest 1 to 2 screenshots in full, mask older observation images to a short text line (or drop them), keep the action and reasoning history. Our think-before-observe and think-before-act gaps (7.7s and 14.9s) both involve re-reading history, so a shorter history should cut both. Test: mask all but the latest screenshot, measure think gap per step and success on held-out tasks. Note the failure mode the paper implies: masking can grow the context without bound over very long tasks, so pair it with a hard turn cap or a periodic summary for long tasks.
- DO NOT REDO: LLM summarization of history before testing masking. Summarization costs an extra model call and is no better than masking on solve rate.

### 3.2 Self-GC: self-governing context (Hao et al., Xiaohongshu, 2026)

- Source: Xubin Hao et al., Xiaohongshu (RedNote), "Self-Governing Context", 2026, via the deeplearning.ai report (SECONDARY).
- Tried: when input tokens pass 30 percent of the context window, a planner model (default Qwen3.6-Plus) decides per history item to retain, fold (move aside with a pointer note), mask (keep opening and closing text, cut the middle), or prune (delete). Actions are applied on a copy first, and anything touching the latest request is discarded. Shortening only happens if it is estimated to cut future cost including cache savings.
- Measured: on 33 demanding conversations, removed 43.95 percent of input tokens while keeping necessary details 84.85 percent of the time. On 332 conversations, removed 31 percent to 34 percent of input tokens while keeping necessary details 91.27 percent to 94.58 percent across 3 planner models. It removed less history than baselines but was far less likely to lose useful information.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. The fold/mask/prune taxonomy is directly usable as a design, but the planner-model call costs a model round trip, which in our loop is the thing being saved. Only worth it if the planner is a small local model (see section 13) or if it runs rarely (every N turns, not every turn). Simpler first step is 3.1 masking by rule; Self-GC is the upgrade if rule masking loses needed detail.

### 3.3 REVISION: temporal visual redundancy reduction (arXiv 2605.11212, 2026)

- Source: "REVISION: Scaling Computer-Use Agents via Temporal Visual Redundancy Reduction", arXiv 2605.11212, 2026.
- Tried: train the multimodal model on trajectories where redundant visual patches are removed across consecutive screenshots, using a learned patch selector that compares patch representations over time and filters visually redundant regions while preserving spatial structure.
- Measured: across OSWorld, WebTailBench, and AgentNetBench, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B with 5 history screenshots, REVISION reduces token usage by 46 percent on average while IMPROVING success rate by 3 percent. Consecutive GUI screenshots largely overlap, so much of the visual cost is redundant.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY as published (needs training a patch selector and retraining the agent model; our executor is closed). But the 46 percent number quantifies the prize for a training-free version: diff-based masking. Our loop already sends screenshots at max-width 960; a cheap local diff (pixel or perceptual hash) could drop unchanged regions or whole unchanged screenshots before they reach the model. That is a local image-processing prototype, not a model-training one. Worth one prototype arm, measured on think gap.

### 3.4 OSWorld-Human: where computer-use latency actually sits (arXiv 2506.16042, 2026) - MERGED, cited in all three surveys

- Source: Abhyankar, Qi, Zhang, "OSWorld-Human: Benchmarking the Efficiency of Computer-Use Agents", arXiv 2506.16042, submitted 19 Jun 2025, revised 18 May 2026, MLSys 2026. Primary source, arXiv HTML read by the grounding worker. The latency and planning surveys cite the same paper; all three agree on the numbers below, with one version difference noted.
- Tried: step-breakdown profiling of Agent S2 and GTA1 on 39 representative OSWorld tasks (latency survey) and 37 tasks (grounding survey), splitting each trajectory into information retrieval, step planning, step grounding, action-taking, screenshotting, judging, and reflection. Plus a human-trajectory efficiency benchmark (OSWorld-Human) over 16 agents, with human-determined optimal trajectories for all 369 OSWorld tasks and a Weighted Efficiency Score (WES).
- Measured: planning accounts for more than half, sometimes close to 75 percent, of total task latency for both S2 and GTA1; planning plus reflection together 75 to 94 percent of total task latency across tasks. Judging (GTA1) and reflection (S2) are the second biggest sink, 22.5 percent and 33.6 percent of total task time. Each successive step can take 3x longer than steps at the beginning of a task, because prompt tokens (history) accumulate. Even the best agents take 2.7x to 4.3x more steps than necessary (v2 abstract; the v1 abstract says 1.4 to 2.7x, the difference is the metric version, cite the v2 range). 23 percent of errors are poor visual grounding, causing up to 30 extra steps on a single task. Best-performing agent on OSWorld scores 42.5 percent success but only 17.4 percent on the strictest efficiency metric. Observation-type finding: including a11y trees generally INCREASES per-task latency (tree generation time plus token count). Screenshot-only history does NOT improve performance; only text-based action history helps (cited via the FSP survey; the FSP page attributes this to "Xiao et al. 2025" while the paper authors are Abhyankar et al., a citation inconsistency in the secondary source, flag it). End-to-end latency is measured in tens of minutes for tasks humans finish in a few minutes.
- Applies: YES, this is the paper the pivot stands on. It independently reproduces this program's own profile (think 94 percent, broker 4 percent) on a different agent and benchmark, which means the finding is not an artifact of this harness. Its prescriptions map directly onto the prototype picks: fewer planning/reflection calls (no separate reflection per step), text history instead of image history (keep only the last 1 to 3 screenshots, summarize the rest as text), and do not add a11y trees to the prompt by default. Use its step taxonomy when logging our own runs. Every prototype should be judged against it: does it cut the NUMBER of model calls, and does it stop history from growing? A prototype that cuts per-call cost but adds calls fails this test.
- Think-time lever: direct and quantified. Every planning or reflection call removed is 75 to 94 percent of the latency it sat in. The 3x step-lengthening finding means history pruning is not a cost optimization, it is a latency optimization: a long task gets slower per step unless history is capped.

### 3.5 cua-speedrun: faster environment made the agent SLOWER (Aggarwal et al., arXiv 2609.40284, Sep 2026) - NEGATIVE

- Source: Pranjal Aggarwal, Lawrence Keunho Jang, Sean Welleck, Daniel Fried, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Jing Yu Koh, "cua-speedrun", arXiv 2609.40284, submitted 2026-09-30. Numbers via the blolabel.ai analysis, 2026-10-03 (SECONDARY, quoting Tables 4 and 11).
- Tried: one timed interface (observe, step, done) for OSWorld, OSWorld 2.0, CUA-World, and MyPCBench on QEMU VMs, splitting each task into agent time and environment time. Compared the standard runtime against FastCUA, which answers in milliseconds.
- Measured: on 50 OSWorld tasks, 5 seeds, GPT-6 Astra (low) via Codex: environment time fell from 13.80s to 5.08s per task, but total task time ROSE from 89.54s to 99.02s, agent time rose from 75.74s to 93.94s, action batches rose 40 percent (5.86 to 8.20 per task), and the agent asked for 4x as much explicit waiting (1.12s to 4.74s). Score was flat (90.42 percent vs 90.82 percent). Cause: screenshots came back before the interface had redrawn, so the agent spent turns re-asking. The authors estimate a wait of about 100ms would have done; the agent waited 5.8s instead. A related datum in the same analysis: Gemini 3 Flash Preview scored 33.6 percent in 492.2s at low effort and 57.6 percent in 268.0s at medium effort, and medium was also cheaper ($0.592 vs $1.401 per task).
- Applies: YES, as a NEGATIVE. Do not speed up screenshot delivery without a redraw check. Our observe service is already 0.16s to 0.21s and our screen-changed refusal cascade (P1, about 90s) is the same failure shape: acting on a stale screen. Any prototype that makes observation faster must include a settle or redraw guard, or it will cost more think than it saves. Also note the Gemini datum: higher effort was BOTH faster and cheaper because it finished in fewer steps, which warns against a naive "always lowest effort" rule. Measure steps-to-finish, not just seconds per step.

### 3.6 Screenshot downscale (resolution vs accuracy)

- What the literature says: most vision models are optimized for 1280x800 or 1920x1080; 4K needs heavy downsampling that blurs tiny 10px icon buttons (secondary roundup, Nov 2026). FastFocus (IJCAI-26) shows the opposite failure too: excessive resolution causes attention dispersion and coordinate hallucination ("semantic fragmentation"), and low-resolution inputs act as effective semantic aggregators. The local GROUND worker measured the byte side: full 924x581 frame 76,779 bytes at max-width 3840, crops far smaller; local capture time barely moves with size (37ms full vs 29 to 32ms crop).
- What is measured: no paper in this survey isolates downscale factor vs think time for a Codex-class model. ScreenSpot-Pro's difficulty is largely a resolution story (targets 0.035 percent of screen), and every zoom paper in section 4 is evidence that downscaling the whole frame loses the small targets first.
- Applies: MAYBE. The harness already downscales to max-width 960, and the profile notes the model reads 924x1176 images. Downscaling further saves tokens but risks the small-target accuracy that Pro shows is fragile. Test one step: 960 vs 1280 max-width on the held-out small-target cases, measuring misclicks, not bytes. Do not downscale below the size at which the smallest target in the suite (16px icons) stays legible.
- DO NOT REDO: assuming smaller images mean faster tasks. The GROUND worker already proved local capture is near-fixed cost; the only saving is vision tokens, which is unmeasured until the executor instruments the vision slice of the gap (measurement 2 in opt-grounding-research.md section 7.4).

### 3.7 Cropping (predict-then-verify, native 1:1)

- What the literature says: see section 4.13. Crops are the single most replicated accuracy win in GUI grounding, across five independent methods and three model families, all training-free.
- What is measured locally: GROUND worker probe (2026-11-17): crop 200x400 is 9.5x fewer bytes than full frame, 300x200 is 14.0x fewer, 90x90 is 31.9x fewer; encode time 18.3ms full vs 0.8 to 2.9ms crop; total local per frame about 84ms full vs 57 to 60ms crop (1.4x). A crop also narrows the screen_changed preflight to the crop region, so changes outside the crop can no longer refuse an act.
- Applies: YES, already landed as Step 2. The literature adds two refinements the local design should adopt: (a) include at least one neighbouring control in the crop for disambiguation (the tc06 compress misclick class), and (b) on a failed grounding, stamp the failed point on the next observation (RegionFocus image-as-map, 43.2 vs 37.2 for text history) instead of only describing it in text.
- Think-time lever: indirect and conditional. A crop observation is a smaller vision payload, but it is still a full think call. The win is fewer retries, not a cheaper call. The local rule stands: crop when the target is known within about 50px; full frame when finding something or confirming state; never crop so tight that the discriminating context leaves the frame (tc08 timetable lesson: a crop lost the day/time columns).

### 3.8 Diff-based and incremental observation

- What it tried: InnvoLabs Screen AST plus frame diffing (blog, 5 Sep 2026): divide the display into a grid of perceptual tile hashes, send only bounding-box crops of changed regions; token cost 1,600 per frame to about 240, an 85 percent saving; frame processing 680ms to 42ms; task completion 64.5 to 94.8 percent on 250 enterprise tasks (vendor numbers, single source, no independent reproduction found).
- What is measured elsewhere: GUIPruner (arXiv 2602.23235, Feb 2026) is the peer-reviewed version of the same idea: Temporal-Adaptive Resolution (decay-based resizing of history) plus Stratified Structure-aware Pruning (keep interactive foregrounds and semantic anchors); on Qwen2-VL-2B it gives 3.4x fewer FLOPs and 3.3x faster vision encoding while retaining over 94 percent of performance.
- Applies: MAYBE, gated. Diffing saves tokens and vision-encoding time, which is the right currency, but it has a staleness hazard the local contract already forbids: a reconstructed frame is not a fresh observation (test-cases.md rules, and GROUND worker rank 7). A diff is safe only as a filter for WHAT to re-observe (changed regions get a fresh crop), never as a substitute for the observation itself. The 90s observation rule and screen_changed preflight must still run on fresh pixels.
- DO NOT REDO: pixel caching / observation reuse. Already rejected in the 10x synthesis and by GROUND rank 7, and the literature gives no counter-evidence. Diffing the decision of where to look is fine; reusing old pixels as ground truth is not.
- Think-time lever: direct if it cuts vision tokens per step (GUIPruner 3.3x vision encoding is the number to beat), but only if the implementation runs locally and does not add a model call to compute the diff. A hash-based diff is local and cheap; a model-based diff is not.

### 3.9 Foveated perception (scan / focus / read)

- Author and date: Foveated Screen Perception (Eric Li et al., project page ericxl.github.io, 2026; cites work through 2026).
- What it tried: a single observe tool with three modes mapped to primate vision streams: scan (low-res grayscale full-screen with change annotations), focus (high-res full-color crop at given coordinates), read (symbolic text extraction). The agent selects its mode per step (top-down attentional control). Grayscale-vs-color is treated as a first-class choice alongside crop-vs-full-frame.
- What it measured: a 2x2 factorial evaluation (GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6, ScreenSpot-Pro and OSWorld) is described, but the page text available to this worker states a PROJECTED 10 to 13x reduction in observation tokens per task (typical step 40 tokens for read or 200 for scan vs 1300 for a full screenshot), not a completed measured end-to-end latency win. Supporting numbers cited from others: full screenshot about 1300 tokens on Claude, 765 to 1105 on GPT-4o; a 50-step task burns about 65,000 tokens on screenshot encoding alone; screenshot tokens are 80 percent or more of total context. GUI-Eyes 3B: 44.8 percent Pro with learned crop decisions vs 30.2 percent without.
- Applies: YES as the design to steal, NO as a proven win to assume. The three-mode observe tool maps cleanly onto this harness: scan is a downscaled grayscale full frame, focus is the existing crop path, read is OCR or a11y text. The read mode is the highest-value addition: most verification steps in this suite are reading a label, an error message, or a terminal line, and text at 10 to 20 tokens replaces a 1300-token image for those steps. But the 10 to 13x figure is a projection; measure it on the held-out tasks before claiming it. See section 16: FSP 10 to 13x is flagged UNVERIFIED as a measured win.
- Think-time lever: direct and the largest projected in this survey, IF the model can be trusted to pick read mode when text suffices. Risk: a wrong mode choice costs a second observation (a wasted think call). Mitigate by making read the default for verification steps where the expected state is textual (terminal output, dialog text, file names), and focus the default for grounding steps.

### 3.10 Text, DOM, and OCR instead of images

- What the literature says: DirectShell (Gehrken, Feb 2026, dev.to and Product Hunt): Windows UI Automation tree as SQLite, 50 to 200 tokens per perception vs 1200 to 5000 for a screenshot and 5000 to 15000 for a full tree dump; claims 85 percent task success using only a11y text, and a 360-cell spreadsheet filled in 90 seconds. Agent-desktop (Vercel Labs, v1.0.2, 30 Oct 2026, Apache-2.0): reads the OS accessibility tree on macOS, Windows, Linux (Ubuntu 24.04+), returns JSON with a stable ref per element; skeleton traversal (3 levels deep, then drill down) cuts tokens 78 to 96 percent self-reported (Apple Maps 3811 to 273 tokens per look; a simulated 52-step task 198k to 31k tokens). Both are vendor/self-reported numbers with no independent benchmark found in this survey.
- What is measured in peer-reviewed work: OSWorld-Human (section 3.4): screenshot-only history does NOT improve performance; only text-based action history helps. VisualWebArena (Koh et al. 2024): GPT-4V text-only (a11y tree) 7.3 percent, plus screenshot 15.1 percent, plus Set-of-Marks 16.4 percent. So text alone is weak (7.3), screenshot doubles it (15.1), and labels add a little more (16.4). Text replaces images only for reading and verification, never for discovery or grounding.
- Applies: MAYBE, narrowly. The read mode in 3.9 is the safe form: OCR text (tesseract is already in the harness, though English-only, which misread Vietnamese diacritics in P4) or a pruned a11y subtree for verification steps. The broad form (drive the whole task from the tree) is a NO for this suite: GTK popups are not exposed, the VS Code editor surface is uninformative via a11y, and a full tree dump costs MORE tokens than a screenshot. The Mage-VL finding reinforces the caution: adding a11y data to the GUI-plus-coding harness made the teacher take MORE steps (35.33 vs 25.0), not fewer.
- DO NOT REDO: full-tree dumps as observation. Measured to cost 5000 to 15000 tokens, worse than a screenshot, and already rejected locally (Astra review section 4b, GROUND rank 7). Only skeleton or query-scoped tree reads are admissible.
- Think-time lever: direct for verification steps (text is cheaper for the model to read than an image), negative for grounding steps (the model still needs the image to locate things).

### 3.11 Accessibility-tree grounding (AT-SPI extents)

- What the literature says: WindowsAgentArena (Bonatti et al. 2024): GPT-4V-1106 plus OmniParser scores 12.5 percent without the UIA tree and 19.5 percent with it, a 57 percent relative boost; human 74.5 percent. OmniParser vs UIA comparisons (secondary): UIA gives near-zero latency and deterministic boxes for well-behaved native apps, but fails entirely on custom-rendered UI, Electron apps with broken a11y, and games.
- What is true locally: the harness a11y.py (136 lines, read by GROUND worker) has NO coordinate path at all: zero matches for component, position, extents. describe() returns ref, role, name, actions, editable, pressed/checked/selected/expanded/disabled, but never a position. Native click via do_action exists and works for exposed controls, but produces no image evidence and no coordinates.
- Applies: MAYBE, as a prototype: add AT-SPI Component get_extents to a11y.py, validate it against a fresh crop on the suite's apps, and measure coverage per app before trusting it. The prototype plan's pick 4 (AT-SPI extents plus DOM-first browser fast path) is the right shape. Expected value is highest for native GTK dialogs and Nautilus, lowest for VS Code editor and Pinta canvas (custom-rendered). The WAA 12.5 to 19.5 percent result says the tree helps when it exists; the local GTK-popup blindness says it often does not exist where it matters most.
- Think-time lever: direct if extents replace a Luna grounding call with a local lookup; zero if the tree is incomplete and the model still needs the image. Measure coverage first (GROUND measurement 5), then decide.

### 3.12 Set-of-Marks (SoM) prompting

- Author and date: Yang et al. 2023 (arXiv 2310.11441, Microsoft Research).
- What it tried: partition the image into regions with an off-the-shelf segmentation model (SEEM/SAM), overlay each region with a mark (number, letter, box, mask), and let the model answer by mark instead of by coordinates. No marks are leaked to the text input; the model must associate marks with visual content.
- What it measured: GPT-4V with SoM beats the fully fine-tuned SOTA referring-expression model on RefCOCOg in a zero-shot setting. VisualWebArena: SoM lifts GPT-4V from 15.1 (screenshot) to 16.4 percent. OmniParser is SoM operationalized for UI: it replaces SAM with a YOLO detector plus Florence-2 captions and numbered overlays.
- Applies: YES as a technique, already partially present via OmniParser-style thinking and the local crop path. SoM converts continuous coordinate regression (which VLMs do badly, with 10 to 30px drift on dense pages per secondary analysis) into discrete selection (pick mark 17), which is an easier task for the model and therefore a cheaper think call. The cost is the segmentation/detection pass that proposes the regions; that pass must be local (YOLO) or it adds a model call.
- Think-time lever: direct in principle (discrete choice is faster to reason than coordinate regression), unmeasured for Luna specifically. Worth one prototype arm: numbered overlay from a local detector vs raw coordinates, measured on misclick rate and think-before-act time.

### 3.13 OmniParser v1, v1.5, v2

- Author and date: Lu et al. 2024 (arXiv 2408.00203, Aug 2024; v1.5 Nov 2024; v2 Jan/Feb 2025). Cited in both the latency and grounding surveys; merged here.
- What it tried: pure-vision screen parsing: fine-tuned YOLOv8 (later YOLOv9-E, MIT-licensed detector in v2) finds interactive elements and boxes; fine-tuned Florence-2-base captions each element functionally; output is a numbered SoM overlay plus a structured element list. V1.5 adds interactability prediction (filter non-interactive logos and static text) and finer small-icon detection.
- What it measured: v2 (Hugging Face model card, primary source): 39.6 average accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro; average latency 0.6s per frame on A100, 0.8s on a single RTX 4090; 60 percent latency improvement vs v1. v1 secondary reports: 2 to 3s per screenshot on an RTX 4090 for detection plus captioning sequentially; GPT-4V grounding on desktop from about 41 to nearly 75 percent (plus 33.6 points) with OmniParser; icon disambiguation up over 45 percent relative. OSWorld: OmniParser plus GPT-4o improves task completion by 18.2 percent (secondary blog, model and step budget not fully specified, treat as directional). WindowsAgentArena: best result 39.4 percent task success with OmniParser, screenshot-only, per secondary comparison (vs Navi 19.5 percent with UIA in the original paper; different agents and dates, do not compare directly). Independent deployment report (AitherOS blog, Mar 2026): YOLO detection 50 to 100ms, Florence captioning for 20 elements 200 to 400ms, full parse with OCR 500 to 800ms on an RTX 3080 10GB; models consume about 2 to 4GB VRAM.
- Applies: MAYBE. The VRAM (2 to 4GB) fits the RTX 4050 6GB, and the latency (0.5 to 0.8s on a 3080/4090) is acceptable if it replaces a Luna grounding call. Two blockers to check before prototyping: (a) license: the YOLOv8 icon_detect weights are AGPL-3.0 (viral copyleft if used in a service), while the v2 YOLOv9-E detector is MIT-licensed, so use the v2 MIT detector only; (b) the 0.6 to 0.8s is on A100/4090, and the 4050 is far slower, so measure locally before assuming sub-second. The prototype plan already resolved this the same way (detection plus OCR via MIT-licensed YOLO v3 only).
- Think-time lever: direct if it replaces Luna grounding calls; the 0.5 to 0.8s local parse is 10 to 20x cheaper than a 5 to 18s Luna think. But it ADDS a local step to every observation, so it only wins when the element list actually lets Luna skip its own grounding reasoning. If Luna still re-grounds from the image, OmniParser is pure overhead. Latency-survey angle: 0.8s of local grounding on a 4090 replaces part of a 14.9s think-before-act that currently includes the model reading the screenshot and guessing coordinates. On the RTX 4050 it will be slower than 0.8s, so measure locally before adopting.

## 4. Grounding models, grounding techniques, and benchmarks

### 4.1 ScreenSpot and ScreenSpot-v2 (the grounding yardstick)

- Author and date: Cheng et al. 2024 (SeeClick paper, Jan 2024); ScreenSpot-v2 re-annotation by Wu et al. 2024.
- What it tried: the first realistic GUI grounding benchmark: screenshot plus instruction, predict a point, correct if inside the ground-truth box. ScreenSpot-v2 has 1,272 targets across mobile, desktop, web, split text vs icon/widget. V2 re-annotated 11.3 percent of samples to fix wrong boxes and ambiguous instructions.
- What it measured: GPT-4V 16.2 percent average; SeeClick 53.4 percent; UGround 73.3 percent standard setting, 75.6 percent agent setting (GPT-4 planner), 81.4 percent with GPT-4o planner; ShowUI-2B 75.1 percent; OS-Atlas-7B 82.5 percent; UI-TARS-7B 91.6 percent; UI-TARS-1.5 94.2 percent; Jedi-7B 91.7 percent; GroundGUI-8B 95.2 percent; Ferret-UI Lite-3B 91.6 percent; GUI-G2-7B 92.0 percent. Recent audit (arXiv 2610.36831): box scoring under-counts correct clicks by 0.2 to 1.8 points on ScreenSpot-v2, and a different training seed alone moves accuracy up to 2.6 points, so gaps under about 1.5 points between 7 to 8B models are noise.
- Applies: YES as the evaluation yardstick for any local grounder candidate. It is a grounding-only test, not an end-to-end task test, so a high ScreenSpot score does not guarantee fewer think calls. Always pair with a held-out task run.
- Think-time lever: indirect. A grounder that is right first time removes misclick-recovery loops, each of which costs a full think cycle plus re-observation.

### 4.2 ScreenSpot-Pro (high-resolution professional grounding)

- Author and date: Li et al. 2025 (arXiv 2503.00757 era; project page likaixin2000.github.io).
- What it tried: 1,581 expert-annotated instructions in authentic high-resolution screenshots from 23 professional applications across 5 industries and 3 operating systems. Targets are tiny: median target covers 0.035 percent of the screen on the Pro set vs 0.72 percent on v2, about 20x smaller (dev.to independent benchmark, 2026). ScreenSpot-Pro targets occupy only 0.07 percent of total image area (FSP survey).
- What it measured: best model at launch only 18.9 percent (OS-Atlas-7B). UI-TARS-7B 35.7 percent; UI-TARS-1.5-7B 49.6 percent; UI-TARS-1.5 largest 61.6 percent; OpenAI CUA 23.4 percent; Claude 3.7 27.7 percent; Jedi-7B 39.5 percent; GroundGUI-8B 63.9 percent; MEGA-GUI 73.18 percent; AdaZoom-GUI 76.8 percent (per FSP survey, current best cited there); Ferret-UI Lite-3B 53.3 percent, beating UI-TARS-1.5-7B by over 15 points despite being 3B. RegionFocus lifts Qwen2.5-VL-72B to 61.6 percent. ScreenSeeker lifts OS-Atlas-7B 18.9 to 48.1 percent.
- Applies: YES, this is the benchmark closest to the hard cases in this suite (small icons in dense desktop apps, e.g. Pinta toolbar, VS Code menus). A grounder candidate must be judged on Pro, not only on v2, because v2 is near saturation (93 to 96 percent for leading 7 to 8B grounders) and no longer discriminates.
- Think-time lever: indirect, same as 4.1, but stronger: Pro-class misses are exactly the misclicks that cause multi-minute recovery loops here.

### 4.3 SeeClick (the first visual GUI agent)

- Author and date: Cheng et al. 2024 (arXiv 2311.08868, Jan 2024).
- What it tried: a visual-only GUI agent (screenshots only, no a11y tree) with GUI grounding pre-training, plus the ScreenSpot benchmark itself. Proved the central claim that grounding gains transfer to downstream agent tasks.
- What it measured: ScreenSpot average 53.4 percent (mobile text 78.0, mobile icon 52.0, desktop text 72.2, desktop icon 30.0, web text 55.7, web icon 32.5), vs GPT-4V 16.2 percent and CogAgent 47.4 percent. Icon/widget grounding was far weaker than text grounding in every platform, a pattern that persists in every later model.
- Applies: YES as history and as the origin of the grounding-pretraining recipe, but NO as a model to deploy: 9.6B, 2024-era accuracy, superseded by every model below. Its lasting contribution is the benchmark and the finding that grounding pre-training is the highest-leverage training stage (confirmed by the Aguvis ablation, 4.7).
- Think-time lever: none directly today; historical only.

### 4.4 Ferret-UI, Ferret-UI 2, Ferret-UI Lite - MERGED, cited in grounding and planning surveys

- Author and date: You et al. 2024 (Ferret-UI, arXiv 2404.05719, Apr 2024); Li et al. 2024/2025 (Ferret-UI 2, arXiv 2410.18967, ICLR 2025); Yang et al. 2026 (Ferret-UI Lite, arXiv 2509.26539, Aug 2026, dated 24 Aug 2026 in the paper).
- What it tried: Ferret-UI: grounded mobile UI understanding with referring and grounding tasks (OCR, icon recognition, widget classification, find widget/icon/text). Ferret-UI 2: universal UI understanding across iPhone, Android, iPad, web, AppleTV, with adaptive multi-grid high-resolution encoding and GPT-4o plus Set-of-Marks training data generation. Ferret-UI Lite: a compact 3B end-to-end GUI agent for mobile, web, desktop, built for on-device use.
- What it measured: Ferret-UI beats GPT-4V on advanced mobile tasks and reaches 76 percent zero-shot on the UIBert referring-expression task; anyres adds about 2 points on iPhone referring and grounding. Ferret-UI 2: 91.4 percent pointing accuracy on mobile screens, 84.6 percent on desktop tiny buttons, 88.2 percent on web dashboards (secondary blog summary of the paper; treat the desktop and web numbers as vendor-reported, not independently reproduced). Ferret-UI Lite-3B (primary source, arXiv HTML): ScreenSpot-V2 91.6, ScreenSpot-Pro 53.3, OSWorld-G 61.2 (abstract says 61.2; body table says 55.3 on OSWorld-G, an internal inconsistency in the paper, flag it, see section 16); AndroidWorld 28.0 percent, OSWorld 19.8 percent as an end-to-end agent. On Pro it beats UI-TARS-1.5-7B by over 15 points and sits only slightly below GUI-Owl-7B (54.9).
- Applies: YES, Ferret-UI Lite-3B is the top local-grounder candidate for this machine (re-ranked FIRST in the prototype plan by this merged survey; the original plan had it second). 3B fits comfortably in 6GB VRAM even at higher precision, and its Pro score at 3B is the best small-model result found in this survey. Caveat: its end-to-end agent scores (28.0 AndroidWorld, 19.8 OSWorld) are modest, so use it as a grounder behind the Luna planner, not as a standalone agent. Grounds well and plans poorly (GUI navigation only 19.8 percent on OSWorld), which is exactly the split the cascade wants: ground local, plan with Luna, never let the small model plan.
- Think-time lever: direct, if it replaces a Luna vision grounding call with a fast local call. A 3B local forward pass is sub-second on an RTX 4050 class GPU, vs 5 to 18s of Luna think per grounding step. The saving is only real if the grounder is invoked instead of a Luna call, not in addition to one. Verify-first design (local guess, Luna confirms from a tiny crop) adds a call and can erase the win; prefer local guess plus automatic execution with post-action verification.
- Prototype gate: no local model was run in any survey (read-only tasks). Every "fits in 6GB VRAM" claim for Ferret-UI Lite-3B and UI-TARS-1.5-2B is a size-based judgment, not a measured load test. The prototype worker must load each candidate on the laptop and report actual VRAM and per-call latency before it is used in the loop.

### 4.5 CogAgent

### 4.6 OS-Atlas (OS-ATLAS)

- Author and date: Wu et al. 2024 (arXiv 2410.23218, submitted 30 Oct 2024).
- What it tried: a foundation action model for generalist GUI agents. Built an open toolkit for synthesizing multi-platform GUI grounding data and released the largest open cross-platform grounding corpus at the time: over 13 million GUI elements across Windows, Linux, macOS, Android, web. Two-stage: large-scale grounding pre-training (OS-Atlas-Base), then multitask agent fine-tuning.
- What it measured: ScreenSpot 82.5 percent average (7B, per GUI-G2 table: mobile text 93.0, mobile icon 72.9, desktop text 91.8, desktop icon 62.9, web text 90.9, web icon 74.3); ScreenSpot-Pro 18.9 percent at launch (best model then), later lifted to 48.1 percent by ScreenSeeker without retraining; OSWorld about 14.6 percent (OS-Atlas-Base-7B in the Aguvis comparison table). UI-Vision element grounding final average 9.02 percent (7B) on that harder benchmark.
- Applies: MAYBE. The 7B model is borderline on 6GB VRAM (quantized it may fit, but with KV cache and a vision encoder it is tight, and its Pro score is now far behind Ferret-UI Lite-3B and UI-TARS-1.5-7B). Its real value here is the DATA recipe and the open 13M-element corpus, not the weights. If the swarm ever fine-tunes a small grounder, OS-Atlas data synthesis is the recipe to copy.
- Think-time lever: indirect (accuracy), same as 4.1. No direct latency win claimed in the paper.

### 4.7 Aguvis

- Author and date: Xu et al. 2024/2025 (arXiv 2412.04454, ICML 2025).
- What it tried: the first fully pure-vision cross-platform GUI agent framework (screenshots only, no a11y, no DOM). Two-stage training: stage 1 GUI grounding pre-training, stage 2 agent planning and action.
- What it measured: ScreenSpot average 78.2 percent (7B: mobile text 93.4, mobile icon 76.9, desktop text 91.0, desktop icon 66.8, web text 85.7, web icon 55.5); OSWorld 14.79 percent (7B); AndroidWorld 21.8 percent (72B); MobileMiniWob++ 62.5 percent (72B). Ablation: removing stage 1 grounding pre-training drops ScreenSpot 78.2 to 69.5 and OSWorld 14.79 to 10.2, so grounding pre-training is worth plus 8.7 points grounding and plus 4.6 points end-to-end. GPT-4o plus Aguvis-7B as grounder reaches 37.1 percent on AndroidWorld (MobileUse comparison table), vs 32.8 for GPT-4o plus UGround.
- Applies: MAYBE as a design reference (pure vision, two-stage training), NO as a model to deploy: 7B, 2024-era accuracy, superseded on every benchmark by UI-TARS-1.5, Jedi, and Ferret-UI Lite. Its ablation is the cleanest evidence in the survey that grounding quality is the highest-leverage training investment.
- Think-time lever: indirect. The ablation quantifies how much end-to-end success (and therefore recovery-loop think time) grounding buys.

### 4.8 UGround and UGround-v1

- Author and date: Gou et al. 2025 (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.05243).
- What it tried: universal visual grounding for GUI agents from a simple recipe: web-based synthetic data plus slight adaptation of the LLaVA-NeXT 7B architecture, with AnyRes for varied screenshot sizes. Collected the largest grounding dataset at the time: 10M GUI elements and referring expressions over 1.3M screenshots. Planner plus grounder split: a strong planner (GPT-4/GPT-4o) writes referring expressions, UGround returns pixel coordinates.
- What it measured: ScreenSpot standard setting 73.3 percent average; agent setting 75.6 percent with GPT-4 planner, 81.4 percent with GPT-4o planner (vs SeeClick 52.3 percent in the same setting). UGround-v1-7B: ScreenSpot-v2 87.1 to 92.1 percent depending on table, ScreenSpot-Pro 31.1 to 32.5 percent, UI-Vision 12.9 percent final average, OSWorld-G 36.4 percent. As grounder behind GPT-4o: 32.8 percent AndroidWorld, 16.1 percent OSWorld (BiGrounder comparison table). Data scaling: returns diminish after 100K screenshots; just 50K screenshots (about 600K elements) already beats SeeClick by over 10 points.
- Applies: MAYBE. The planner-plus-grounder split is exactly this program's shape (Luna plans, local model grounds), and UGround is the paper that proved the split works. But the 7B weights are superseded on Pro (32 percent) by Ferret-UI Lite-3B (53.3 percent) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (49.6 percent). Adopt the architecture, not the weights. The data-scaling finding matters for any future fine-tune: 50K screenshots is enough to beat the prior SOTA, so a small targeted fine-tune is cheap, not a moonshot.
- Think-time lever: direct in architecture. Splitting planner and grounder means the expensive reasoning model is called once per plan, not once per coordinate guess. That is the same shape as the prototype plan's pick 3 (multi-candidate grounding with tiny-crop verification).

### 4.9 UI-TARS and UI-TARS-1.5 - MERGED, cited in all three surveys

- Author and date: Qin et al. 2025 (ByteDance Seed; UI-TARS-1.5 Feb 2026 writeup).
- What it tried: a native GUI agent VLM based on Qwen2.5-VL, trained for GUI grounding plus agentic reasoning with inference-time thinking (more compute per step buys accuracy on complex tasks).
- What it measured: UI-TARS-7B: ScreenSpot-V2 91.6, ScreenSpot-Pro 35.7, OSWorld-G 47.5. UI-TARS-1.5-7B: ScreenSpot-V2 89.0, ScreenSpot-Pro 49.6, OSWorld 27.5 percent at 100 steps, AndroidWorld 64.2 percent, WindowsAgentArena 42.1 percent. UI-TARS-1.5 largest: ScreenSpot-V2 94.2, ScreenSpot-Pro 61.6, OSWorld 42.5 percent at 100 steps. UI-TARS-72B with RegionFocus: Pro 38.1 to 50.2 percent. UI-TARS-1.5-7B as grounder behind GPT-4o: 37.9 percent AndroidWorld, 19.1 percent OSWorld. (Latency survey, SECONDARY via lilting.ch and paperclipped.de reviews: UI-TARS-1.5-7B reaches 49.6 percent on ScreenSpot Pro, about twice OpenAI CUA at 23.4 percent and Claude 3.7 at 27.7 percent; the 7B scores 27.5 percent on OSWorld at 100 steps; the largest reaches 42.5 percent; the 7B runs on a single consumer GPU.)
- Applies: MAYBE. UI-TARS-1.5-2B is the second local-grounder candidate (the original prototype plan listed it first; this merged survey ranks Ferret-UI Lite-3B ahead on Pro accuracy, UI-TARS-1.5-2B ahead on ecosystem maturity and Qwen2.5-VL tooling). The 7B version is borderline on 6GB VRAM and NOT the default. The 72B versions are irrelevant locally. Note the inference-time thinking design: UI-TARS deliberately spends MORE compute per step for accuracy, which is the opposite of the think-time goal unless it prevents a recovery loop. Use the 2B as a fast grounder, not as a reasoning agent.
- Think-time lever: direct if the 2B replaces Luna grounding calls; negative if the thinking variant is used, since it lengthens each call.
- Local fit verdict (from the prototype plan, section 2, kept here): UI-TARS-1.5-2B FITS, about 2.5GB VRAM at int4, TTFT about 120ms on a 4090 at int4, warm p50 91ms in the Utter measurement on an RTX 3090 Ti. Accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro is UNMEASURED for the 2B in the sources found; the 7B scores 61.6 percent on ScreenSpot-Pro and 94.2 percent on ScreenSpot-V2 (note: the grounding survey reports the 7B Pro at 35.7 and the 1.5-7B Pro at 49.6; the 61.6 figure is the UI-TARS-1.5 largest size, so cite per size, not per family). A 20-sample local grounding test on his apps, including Vietnamese dialog text and small menu rows, is the cheapest way to get a number before building the cascade around it. UI-TARS-1.5-7B: BORDERLINE, rejected as the default, about 6GB VRAM at int4, TTFT about 250ms on a 4090; 7B needs about 17GB at BF16 and about 4.8GB at Q4_K_M for weights alone, before the vision tower and KV cache, so it does not fit comfortably and would be slower than the API call it replaces. Do not prototype the 7B as the grounder. If the 2B fails the accuracy test, try Ferret-UI Lite 3B next, not the 7B.

### 4.10 Jedi and OSWorld-G

- Author and date: Xie et al. 2025 (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track, spotlight; arXiv 2505.13227, submitted 17 Jun 2025).
- What it tried: OSWorld-G, a 564-sample fine-grained grounding benchmark (text matching, element recognition, layout understanding, precise manipulation, infeasibility/refusal), plus Jedi, a 4-million-example synthetic grounding dataset built by multi-perspective decomposition of UI tasks, and multi-scale Jedi models.
- What it measured: Jedi-7B: ScreenSpot-v2 91.7, ScreenSpot-Pro 39.5, OSWorld-G 54.1 (text matching 65.9, fine-grained manipulation 46.9). Jedi-3B: 88.6, 36.1, 50.9. As grounder behind a planner: Jedi-3B plus GPT-4o reaches 22.4 percent OSWorld (15 steps) and 29.1 percent WindowsAgentArena; Jedi-7B plus GPT-4o reaches 27.0 percent OSWorld (100 steps); Jedi-7B plus o3 reaches 50.2 percent OSWorld (100 steps). GPT-4o alone is 5.0 percent OSWorld (15 steps), so grounding lifts the same planner 5.0 to 22.4 percent at the same step budget. The project page headline claims improved grounding lifts OSWorld 23 to 51 percent; the 51 percent figure is the o3-planner number, not a like-for-like comparison, so cite the 5.0 to 22.4 pair instead.
- Applies: YES as a benchmark (OSWorld-G is the best fine-grained grounding test, and its refusal/infeasibility dimension matches the "is this control actually there" check this harness needs) and MAYBE as a model (Jedi-3B is a credible small-grounder candidate, but Ferret-UI Lite-3B beats it on Pro 53.3 vs 36.1 and on OSWorld-G 61.2/55.3 vs 50.9). The dataset construction method (decompose one UI task into many grounding views) is worth copying if the swarm synthesizes its own grounding data for the suite's apps.
- Think-time lever: indirect (accuracy), plus a benchmark contribution: OSWorld-G's refusal dimension tests whether a grounder can say "not here" instead of guessing, and a wrong guess costs a full recovery loop.

### 4.11 ShowUI

- Author and date: Lin et al. 2024 (arXiv 2411.17465, CVPR 2025).
- What it tried: a 2B vision-language-action model based on Qwen2-VL-2B, with UI-connected-graph-guided visual token selection (drop redundant visual tokens using the UI element graph) and interleaved vision-language-action streaming. Only 256K training samples.
- What it measured: ScreenSpot zero-shot 75.1 percent average (mobile text 92.3, mobile icon 75.5, desktop text 76.3, desktop icon 61.1, web text 81.7, web icon 63.6), beating UGround-7B (73.3 percent) with 3.5x fewer parameters and 5x less data, and CogAgent-18B (47.4 percent) with 9x fewer parameters. Token selection cuts redundant visual tokens 33 percent and gives 1.4x speedup in training. Ablation: token MERGING degrades grounding 70.8 to 42.3 percent (pooling discards positional information), while token SELECTION is almost lossless (70.8 to 70.4 percent) because it retains positional encodings.
- Applies: YES as a design lesson and MAYBE as a model. The token-selection result is the most actionable efficiency finding for any local VLM work: never merge or pool visual tokens for grounding, select them. The 2B model itself is a third local-grounder candidate, behind Ferret-UI Lite-3B and UI-TARS-1.5-2B, because its Pro score is not reported in the sources found and its v2-era 75.1 percent is now mid-pack.
- Think-time lever: direct and proven in miniature: 33 percent fewer visual tokens per frame with no accuracy loss is a direct cut to the vision payload the reasoning model must consume, and 1.4x speedup is measured, not projected.
- DO NOT REDO: token merging/pooling for grounding. Measured to destroy accuracy (70.8 to 42.3 percent). Any proposal to pool, average, or downsample visual tokens before grounding must be rejected on this evidence.

### 4.12 GroundGUI, Phi-Ground, BiGrounder, GUI-G2, GUI-Owl, Mage-VL (2025 to 2026 state of the art)

- GroundGUI-8B (2026, arXiv 2610.19100 era): ScreenRef dataset (586K screenshots, 633K referring expressions); ScreenSpot-V2 95.2, ScreenSpot-Pro 63.9, OSWorld-G 65.4, MMBench-GUI L2 86.8, UI-Vision 38.3. Best 8B grounding reported in this survey. Applies: NO locally (8B does not fit 6GB VRAM comfortably), YES as proof that data quality beats scale: it beats 32B models (ScaleCUA-32B, OpenCUA-32B, GTA1-32B) at 8B.
- Phi-Ground (2025, arXiv 2507.23779): under-10B SOTA in agent settings; end-to-end 43.2 on ScreenSpot-Pro and 27.2 on UI-Vision (7B DPO). Notes current end-to-end grounding models still score under 65 percent on ScreenSpot-Pro and UI-Vision, far from deployment-ready. Applies: MAYBE as a 4B/7B candidate if weights and VRAM check out; the 4B-16C-DPO variant (38.0 Pro) is the more relevant size. Needs a VRAM check before any prototype.
- BiGrounder-4B (arXiv 2611.07765): bidirectional training; as grounder behind GPT-4o it reports 72.4 percent AndroidWorld and 42.8 percent OSWorld, far above UGround-V1-7B (32.8/16.1) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (37.9/19.1) in the same table. Applies: MAYBE with a flag. These end-to-end numbers are dramatically higher than every other grounder-plus-GPT-4o result in this survey, and the paper is very recent (Nov 2026). Treat as UNVERIFIED until an independent run reproduces it; do not re-rank the prototype plan on it. If true, a 4B bidirectional grounder would be the best candidate; verify first. See section 16.
- GUI-G2-7B (arXiv 2507.15846 era): Gaussian reward modeling for grounding; ScreenSpot-v2 93.3, ScreenSpot-Pro improvement of 24.7 percent over UI-TARS-72B (relative, per abstract). Applies: NO locally (7B), YES as a training-method reference (reward shaping on click distributions beats plain SFT for grounding).
- GUI-Owl-1.5 (Mobile-Agent-v3.5, arXiv 2602.16855, 2026): 2B/4B/8B/32B/235B native GUI agent; 8B-Thinking reaches 56.5 OSWorld-Verified, 71.6 AndroidWorld, 80.3 ScreenSpot-Pro; 8B non-thinking 52.9 OSWorld-Verified. Applies: MAYBE for the 2B/4B sizes as grounder candidates, unranked until a Pro score for the small sizes is found. The 8B scores show the current open-source ceiling for end-to-end desktop agents.
- Mage-VL-Cowork 4B (Microsoft, 30 Oct 2026, Apache-2.0): switches turn by turn between GUI clicks and shell code; 66.0 percent OSWorld-Verified at 50 steps (two-run average 65.33 and 66.75, 361 tasks). Its own controlled comparison: GUI-plus-coding harness gives the GPT-5.4 teacher 92.0 percent in 25.0 mean steps vs 89.6 percent in 35.33 steps with accessibility data added. Applies: NO as weights (9.48GB, does not fit 6GB VRAM unquantized, per prototype plan verification), YES as the strongest evidence for the code-execution lever: the same teacher, same tasks, 10 fewer steps per task when it can run code instead of clicking, and adding a11y data made it SLOWER (more steps), not faster. That second finding is a warning against stuffing the prompt with tree data.
- FOCUS (arXiv 2503.06470, Mar 2025): fast/slow dual-system grounding, 2B model, 77.4 percent ScreenSpot, 13.3 percent ScreenSpot-Pro. Applies: NO as a model (Pro score is poor), YES as a design pattern: fast direct prediction first, deliberate analysis only when the fast path is uncertain. That is the cheapest think-time shape: do not pay for reasoning you do not need.

### 4.13 MEGA-GUI and RegionFocus-adjacent leaders (zoom/crop at inference time)

- MEGA-GUI (Samsung, 2025): multi-stage bidirectional adaptive zoom; ScreenSpot-Pro 73.18 percent, OSWorld-G 68.63 percent, beating RegionFocus (61.60 percent Pro) by 11.58 points. Applies: NO as a model (size and serving cost not established for this laptop), YES as the current zoom-crop ceiling: multi-stage zoom with bidirectional refinement is the best-known training-free accuracy technique, and it is pure inference-time cost.
- RegionFocus (Luo et al., arXiv 2505.00684, May 2025, rev Jul 2025, ICCV 2025): visual test-time scaling, dynamic zoom on relevant regions plus an image-as-map mechanism (pink-star markers at prior attempt locations to prevent revisiting). UI-TARS-72B Pro 38.1 to 50.2 percent; Qwen2.5-VL-72B Pro 47.8 to 61.6 percent (SOTA at the time); gains of 28+ points on ScreenSpot-Pro and 24+ points on WebVoyager over base VLMs. UI-TARS-7B plus RegionFocus outperformed UI-TARS-72B overall. Ablation: image-as-map history 43.2 vs text history 37.2. Applies: YES in principle (this is Step 2's intellectual parent), MAYBE in practice: each zoom round is an extra vision call, so it trades think time for accuracy. Use it only on failure or uncertainty (the verify step), not on every grounding. The image-as-map finding (visual landmarks beat text history for revisit-avoidance) is worth stealing for the retry path: stamp the failed point on the next observation instead of describing it in text.
- ScreenSeeker (Zhang et al. 2025): GPT-4o planner guides cascaded recursive cropping, OS-Atlas-7B grounds within each patch; OS-Atlas-7B Pro 18.9 to 48.1 percent, a 154 percent relative gain, no training. Applies: YES as a technique, with the same cost warning as RegionFocus: the planner calls are expensive, so the cascade must be driven by the cheap local grounder, not by Luna.
- ZoomClick (Zheng et al. 2025, Princeton): training-free 3-stage zoom cascade on a 2x2 grid, depth 3, 0.5x shrinkage, minimum crop 768px; UI-Venus-72B Pro 61.4 to 73.1 percent. AdaZoom-GUI (Wang et al. 2026): GRPO-trained conditional zoom, zooms only on predicted small elements; 76.8 percent Pro, current best cited in the FSP survey. GUI-Eyes (Chen et al. 2026): RL-trained zoom decision, 3B model learns when to crop; 44.8 percent Pro vs 30.2 percent without, a 48 percent relative improvement from perception control alone. FastFocus (IJCAI-26): coarse-to-fine, low-resolution attention as a semantic aggregator first, then parallel verification on zoomed views; identifies "semantic fragmentation" (excessive resolution disperses attention and causes coordinate hallucination) as a failure mode. Applies: YES, collectively they prove the same point five ways: zoom helps, but only when conditioned on need. The learned-zoom results (AdaZoom, GUI-Eyes) say the decision of WHETHER to zoom is itself worth learning or at least gating on predicted element size. FastFocus warns that higher resolution is not always better: a low-res first pass can be more stable than a native-res first pass.
- Think-time lever for 4.13 as a group: accuracy per think call goes up, think calls per successful grounding can go down (fewer retries), but think calls per grounding attempt go up (extra zoom rounds). Net think time is an empirical question per task shape; measure it on the held-out tasks with the think-time breakout, do not assume it.
- Zoom refinements carried into the prototype plan (Pick 3): gate zoom on small or uncertain targets; stamp failed points on retry observations (RegionFocus image-as-map 43.2 vs 37.2).

### 4.14 OSWorld (original)

- Author and date: Xie et al. 2024 (arXiv 2404.07972).
- What it tried: 369 real tasks on Ubuntu (plus 43 Windows tasks) across Chromium, LibreOffice, GIMP, VLC, VS Code, Thunderbird, file system, multi-app workflows. Hand-written setup, getter, and evaluator per task (134 distinct evaluation functions; 9 students, about 1800 person-hours over 3 months). Observation: screenshot, a11y tree, or Set-of-Marks overlay. Action: pyautogui code plus WAIT, FAIL, DONE. Step limit 15.
- What it measured: humans 72.36 percent; GPT-4 with a11y tree 12.24 percent; GPT-4V with Set-of-Marks 11.77 percent; GPT-4V screenshot-only 5.26 percent (one source reports 5.80 percent for the best screenshot-only model at release; the difference is the exact model and split, cite the range 5.3 to 5.8). Multi-app workflows peak 6.57 percent vs 13.74 percent single-app. Failure analysis on 550 failed GPT-4V runs: more than 75 percent contained mouse-click inaccuracies.
- Applies: YES as the reference benchmark and as the source of the grounding-bottleneck claim. Its 15-step limit is much tighter than this program's held-out tasks, so do not compare success rates directly. Its lasting lesson for the pivot: grounding errors dominate failures, and multi-app tasks are disproportionately hard.
- Think-time lever: none directly; it is the measuring stick, not a technique.

### 4.15 OSWorld-Verified

- Author and date: HKU, Salesforce AI Research, CMU, Waterloo (Xie, Zhang, Chen, Li et al.); released 28 Jul 2025.
- What it tried: fixed about 300 reported problems with OSWorld tasks and checkers, moved evaluation to parallel AWS (50x parallelism, full run under an hour), declared earlier scores non-comparable. 361 tasks when the 8 Google Drive tasks are skipped (369 with them). Leaderboard lists runs at 15, 50, 100, and 150 step caps.
- What it measured: top entry 90.19 percent (Intelligence-Indeed Agent, 25 Jul 2026, 100 steps, 325.59 of 361 tasks, coding-based actions, underlying model not named on the board; repo names claude-opus-4-7); top general model with no add-ons 85.96 percent (claude-fable-5, 1 Aug 2026, 100 steps). - Think-time lever: the step-budget swing (9.1 to 23.0 percent) is itself a think-time lesson: giving an agent more steps raises success a lot, which means step count and think time are the same budget viewed two ways. Cutting steps per task without cutting success is the whole game.

### 4.16 OSWorld 2.0 and 2.1 (long-horizon)

- Author and date: Yuan et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.29537, Jun 2026; v2.1 released 16 Sep 2026).
- What it tried: 108 long-horizon real-world workflows spanning 31 self-hosted websites and professional desktop apps. Tasks average more than 300 agent steps; 69.6 percent take a skilled human over an hour; median human time about 1.6 hours (about 48x a typical OSWorld 1.0 task); average 27 scoring checkpoints per task for partial credit; 500-step cap.
- What it measured: at release, best system (Claude Opus 4.8, max thinking, batched tools) 20.6 percent binary completion, 54.8 percent partial credit; Claude Opus 4.7 18.2/48.9; GPT-5.5 plateaus near 13 percent. On the v2.1 board (updated 15 Oct 2026), Claude Opus 5 at max reasoning reaches 44.33 percent binary and 77.67 percent partial (500 steps); agent frameworks on Claude reach up to 44.44 percent binary and 79.38 percent partial (450 steps). Average tool calls per task 318 (Claude Opus 4.7 max).
- Applies: YES as the current hard test and as the direction of travel: the field moved from 15-step single-shot tasks to 300-step workflows, and the failure mode moved from clicking to memory, constraint tracking, and verification (per the Snorkel reading group: agents lose track of early information, guess instead of asking, skip verification). For this program, OSWorld 2.0's lesson is that long tasks need checkpointed plans and text memory, not more screenshots. That supports the prototype plan's pick 2 (subgoal-only verification) and the local checkpointed-controller design.
- Think-time lever: the benchmark itself is a think-time stress test: 318 tool calls per task means 318 think calls at the naive one-action-one-observation loop. Any technique that batches actions or skips observations attacks this directly.

### 4.17 AndroidWorld

- Author and date: Rawles et al. 2024 (Google DeepMind; arXiv 2405.14573 era).
- What it tried: 116 parameterised task templates across 20 real Android apps (Calendar, Camera, Chrome, Clock, Contacts, Files, Markor, Messages, OsmAnd, Recorder, Retro Music, Simple Calendar Pro, Simple Draw Pro, Simple Gallery Pro, Simple SMS Messenger, Tasks, VLC, Wikipedia, Browser, OpenTracks), on a Pixel 6 emulator (Android 13+). Agent receives screenshots plus the Android accessibility tree each step; returns tap, swipe, type, navigate. Binary success per task, verified by app/system state, not by action sequence. Step budgets commonly 10 to 30 actions per task. Parameterisation (random time/label per run) reduces memorisation.
- What it measured: paper baseline strongest agent (GPT-4 Turbo, a11y tree) 30.6 percent; human about 80 percent. Later: Claude 3.7 Sonnet with chain-of-thought about 65 percent; SPlanner 63.8 percent (plus 28.8 points over VLM baseline); GUI-Owl-7B 66.4 percent, Mobile-Agent-v3 73.3 percent; GUI-Owl-1.5 71.6 percent; UI-TARS-1.5 64.2 percent; MAI-UI 235B 76.7 percent (self-reported, low-credibility source, flag it); MobileUse 62.9 percent. Step-count result: CLI agents average 10.7 steps per task vs 18.6 for GUI agents on the CLI-Advantage suite (Gu et al. 2026, section 8.4 detail below). Benchmark status Nov 2026: leaderboard archived as saturated, top self-reported entries 100 percent pass@1 (five entries, all self-reported and unverified).
- Applies: MAYBE. It is a mobile benchmark, and this program is desktop Linux, so its absolute numbers do not transfer. Its design transfers: parameterised tasks (anti-memorisation, which this program already does with held-out cases), state-based scoring (not action-sequence matching), and the CLI-vs-GUI step-count comparison. The saturation (100 percent self-reported) means it no longer discriminates; do not use it to rank models.
- Think-time lever: the CLI step-count finding (10.7 vs 18.6) is the strongest step-count evidence in this survey and it comes from this benchmark's task suite.

### 4.18 WindowsAgentArena (WAA)

- Author and date: Bonatti et al. 2024 (arXiv 2409.08264).
- What it tried: adapts OSWorld to Windows: 154 multi-step tasks in 6 groups (Office 43, web browsing 30, Windows system 24, coding 24, media/video 21, Windows utilities 12), 11 programs, each task in an isolated Windows VM in Docker, parallelized on Azure ML. Introduced Navi, a multimodal agent with chain-of-thought and advanced screen representation.
- What it measured: full evaluation in as little as 20 minutes parallel vs hours to more than a day serial. Navi best 19.5 percent (GPT-4V-1106 plus OmniParser plus UIA tree) vs 12.5 percent without the UIA tree (a 57 percent relative boost) and vs 74.5 percent for a single casual human user (reference, not a population estimate). Tasks average 8.1 steps; VLC tasks hardest. Jedi-7B plus GPT-4o: 33.7 percent. UI-TARS-1.5: 42.1 percent.
- Applies: MAYBE. Windows-only, so its absolute numbers do not transfer to Wayland Linux, but two findings do: (a) the UIA tree plus OmniParser combination beats either alone (structured plus visual is better than either alone, which supports the local design of a11y confirmation alongside visual grounding, not instead of it); (b) parallel evaluation cuts benchmark time from days to 20 minutes, which is a practical lesson for this program's validation loop (run held-out tasks in parallel where the desktop allows, or at least do not run them serially by hand).
- Think-time lever: indirect. The 12.5 to 19.5 percent UIA finding is an accuracy lever (fewer retries), not a latency lever.

### 4.19 WebArena and VisualWebArena

- Author and date: Zhou et al. 2023/2024 (WebArena, ICLR 2024); Koh et al. 2024 (VisualWebArena).
- What it tried: WebArena: 812 tasks from 241 templates across self-hosted e-commerce, forum, collaborative dev, and content-management sites, with functional correctness checks (answers, page state, DB state). VisualWebArena adds visually grounded web tasks.
- What it measured: WebArena: best GPT-4 agent 14.41 percent vs human 78.24 percent (ICLR 2024 paper); OpenAI's 2025 computer-using agent reported 58.1 percent vs 36.2 percent previous SOTA (different protocol, do not compare directly). VisualWebArena GPT-4V: text-only (a11y tree) 7.3 percent, plus screenshot 15.1 percent, plus Set-of-Marks 16.4 percent.
- Applies: MAYBE, narrowly. Web-only, so it does not cover this program's desktop tasks, but the VisualWebArena ablation (7.3 text-only, 15.1 screenshot, 16.4 SoM) is the cleanest measurement in the survey of what each observation channel is worth: text alone is weak, the image doubles it, labels add a small further gain. That ordering should govern how the harness spends its observation budget: image first for discovery, text for verification, labels as a cheap add-on.
- Think-time lever: indirect. The ablation says a text-only observation is a false economy for discovery steps (it halves success), but the OSWorld-Human finding says text history is fine for memory. Use text for history and verification, images for discovery and grounding, and do not confuse the two uses.

### 4.20 OSWorld-G, UI-Vision, MMBench-GUI (grounding benchmarks beyond ScreenSpot)

- OSWorld-G (Xie et al. 2025, part of the Jedi paper): 564 finely annotated samples across text matching, element recognition, layout understanding, precise manipulation, and infeasibility/refusal, sampled from OSWorld rollouts at 720p and 1080p. Scores: Jedi-7B 54.1, Jedi-3B 50.9, UI-TARS-7B 47.5, Aguvis-7B 38.7, UGround-v1-7B 36.4, OS-Atlas-7B 27.7, Ferret-UI Lite-3B 61.2 (abstract) / 55.3 (body table), GroundGUI-8B 65.4, MEGA-GUI 68.63. Applies: YES, the best fine-grained grounding benchmark, and the only one with a refusal dimension. Use it to test whether a grounder candidate can say "not here".
- UI-Vision (Nayak et al. 2025): element grounding, layout grounding, action prediction; final averages are much lower than ScreenSpot (UI-TARS-72B 25.50, UI-TARS-7B 17.60, UGround-v1-7B 12.90, Aguvis-7B 13.70, Jedi-7B 25.19, GroundGUI-8B 38.3, Phi-Ground-7B 27.2). Applies: MAYBE. It is a harder, more discriminative benchmark than ScreenSpot-v2, but its scores are so low across the board that it is better as a research signal than as a prototype gate. If a candidate is best on UI-Vision and on Pro, that is a strong signal.
- MMBench-GUI L2 (Wang et al. 2025): GroundGUI-8B 86.8 percent. Applies: MAYBE, secondary. Cite it for completeness; do not gate on it without reading the paper.
- ScreenSpot-Pro and ScreenSpot-v2 remain the primary gates (4.1, 4.2) because they are the most widely reported and therefore the most comparable.

## 5. Model routing and cascades

### 5.1 FrugalGPT: LLM cascades (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, Stanford, arXiv 2305.05176, May 2023)

- Source: Lingjiao Chen, Matei Zaharia, James Zou, "FrugalGPT: How to Use Large Language Models While Reducing Cost and Improving Performance", arXiv 2305.05176, May 2023.
- Tried: three strategies, the main one an LLM cascade: order models cheapest to strongest, run the cheap model first, score its answer, escalate only if the score is below a threshold. Also prompt adaptation (compress or prune prompts) and LLM approximation (caching, distillation).
- Measured: 4x to 98x reduction in API cost at equivalent or better quality on the tested benchmarks; up to 4 percent accuracy improvement at the same cost. Best cases are classification and structured-output tasks where correctness is definable and the score distribution separates well.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS, and the condition is the warning in every honest review: a cascade pays the cheap call PLUS the strong call on every escalation, so its tail latency is the SUM of both. For our loop, where one slow step already costs 14.9s of think, a cascade that escalates often is slower than just calling the strong model. Only test a cascade if the cheap tier can settle a large share of steps outright (windows, verify) with a cheap, reliable acceptance check (schema validity, exit code, exact string match), and measure the escalation rate. If escalation exceeds about 30 percent, the cascade loses on latency even while it wins on cost.

### 5.2 RouteLLM: learned routers from preference data (Ong et al., UC Berkeley/LMSYS, arXiv 2406.18665, Jun 2024)

- Source: Isaac Ong, Amjad Almahairi, Vincent Wu, Wei-Lin Chiang, Tianhao Wu, Joseph E. Gonzalez, M. Waleed Kadous, Ion Stoica, "RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data", arXiv 2406.18665, Jun 2024.
- Tried: train a lightweight router (similarity-weighted ranking, matrix factorization, BERT, or a small LLM classifier) on about 80,000 Chatbot Arena human-preference battles, plus small amounts of in-domain or LLM-judge labels. Route BEFORE generation: one model call per query, strong or weak, never both.
- Measured: routing between GPT-4 and Mixtral-8x7B, the best routers needed about half as many GPT-4 calls as random routing to reach the same quality on MT-Bench (about 95 percent of GPT-4's score), for cost savings up to 3.66x vs always using GPT-4. Abstract headline: over 2x cost reduction without sacrificing quality. Router overhead at most about 0.4 percent of GPT-4 generation cost. Routers transferred without retraining to Claude 3 Opus/Sonnet and Llama 3.1 70B/8B pairs with similar gains. Router accuracy itself tops out around 75 percent to 85 percent in secondary reviews.
- Applies: YES, as the design for step-level routing. Pre-generation routing is strictly better than a cascade for latency (one call, bounded time), which is what our loop needs. The practical shape here is NOT a learned router on Arena data: it is a RULE router by step type (windows and verify go to a cheap tier, act composition goes to the strong tier), because our step types are known in advance and the cheap tier's failure mode is visible (a bad windows list breaks the next step immediately). A learned router is a later refinement; a rule router can be tested this week. The open question is what the cheap tier IS in our setup (see section 13): a smaller Codex model if the CLI offers one, or a local 7B.

### 5.3 AutoMix: self-verification plus POMDP routing (Aggarwal et al., 2023-2024)

- Source: AutoMix, Aggarwal et al., reported in the iotdigitaltwinplm.com routing review (SECONDARY).
- Tried: the small model verifies its own answer few-shot, and a POMDP-based router decides whether to escalate.
- Measured: cost reductions over 50 percent at comparable performance across 5 models and 5 datasets.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. Self-verification by the small model is cheaper than a separate judge, but it still costs a second generation from the small model on every step. Only test where the small model's answer is cheap (short structured output) and the verification prompt is short. Lower priority than the 5.2 rule router.

### 5.4 Hybrid LLM and step-level routing (general finding, 2024-2026)

- Source: multiple secondary reviews (openlegion.ai, generalcompute.com, 2026) summarizing FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, and production practice.
- Tried: split an agent run by step difficulty instead of by whole task: trivial steps (list windows, read a short terminal output, verify an exact string) to a small model; planning and error recovery to the strong model.
- Measured: production estimates cited in these reviews: 65 percent to 80 percent of daily queries do not need frontier reasoning; routing 70 percent to a cheap model cuts total cost about 65 percent. A BERT-class router adds 10ms to 50ms per query; an LLM-based router adds 100ms to 500ms. These are review estimates, not a single paper's measurement, so treat them as planning numbers only.
- Applies: YES. This is the 5.2 rule router restated for our loop, and it matches our measured step profile: windows (4.9s think), first observe (7.7s), and verify (6.7s) are together 19.3s of a 36.4s task, over half, and none of them needs max reasoning. Even routing only those three steps to a model that answers in 2s each would save about 13s per task. The act step (14.9s) stays on the strong model. That is the single clearest routing prototype.

## 6. Planning, plan caching and reuse: compiled routines, skill libraries, workflow memory

### 6.1 Voyager: skill library of executable code (Wang et al., arXiv 2305.16291, 25 May 2023, NeurIPS 2023)

- Tried: GPT-4 Minecraft agent with three parts: automatic curriculum proposing the next task, a skill library storing every successful behaviour as JavaScript code indexed by embedding plus description, and iterative prompting that refines code from game feedback, execution errors, and self-verification. Skills compose: later skills call earlier ones. No fine-tuning.
- Measured: over 160 prompting iterations, 63 unique items discovered, 3.3x the ReAct, Reflexion and AutoGPT baselines (19, 18, 24 items). Travelled 2.3x farther. Unlocked wooden, stone and iron tiers 15.3x, 8.5x and 6.4x faster. Only method to reach diamond tools (1 of 3 runs, about 102 iterations). Zero-shot transfer to a new world: solved all 4 unseen tasks in 3 of 3 trials each, baselines 0. Ablations: random curriculum instead of automatic cut discovered items by 93 percent; removing self-verification cut them by 73 percent; GPT-3.5 instead of GPT-4 for code produced 5.7x fewer unique items.
- Applies: YES, in shape. This is the closest published analogue to the already adopted tc02 procedure, but as CODE, not as a JSON plan. A stored program can loop, branch on intermediate results, and call other stored programs, which a flat action list cannot. The verification step is the load-bearing part: the 73 percent ablation loss says an unverified library is nearly worthless. For this repo the analogue is a Python routine per task shape (open app, type, save-as, verify by file bytes plus SHA), stored with preconditions and a checkpoint, retrieved by task shape, and quarantined after repeated checkpoint failure.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE. Highest-priority reuse mechanism. Cost is one supervisor extraction pass per new shape, amortised over later zero-think runs.

### 6.2 Agent Workflow Memory, AWM (Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig, arXiv 2409.07429, Sep 2024, ICML 2025)

- Tried: induce reusable workflows (routines) from past agent trajectories, offline from training examples or online from test queries on the fly, and put the relevant workflows into the agent prompt to guide later generations. Workflows are text routines, not code. Tested on web navigation only.
- Measured: WebArena with GPT-4, task success 35.5 percent vs 23.5 percent for the BrowserGym agent (plus 51.1 percent relative), 5.9 steps per successful task. Mind2Web step success 45.1 percent vs 36.2 percent for MindAct, task success 4.8 percent vs 2.0 percent (plus 24.6 percent relative step success). Cross-task and cross-domain gains 8.9 to 14.0 absolute points as the train-test gap widens. Workflow induction plus trajectory evaluation adds 10.8 percent compute on top of action generation (about 3,600 extra tokens on a about 33,700-token task).
- Applies: PARTLY. The induction idea transfers directly: extract a routine from a successful trace and inject it on the next similar task. Two limits matter here. First, AWM puts workflows IN THE PROMPT, so every reuse still pays a think call; it saves steps, not think calls, unless the routine is executed deterministically without the model (which is the already adopted level 1, not AWM as published). Second, memory only grows; nothing removes or updates a stale workflow, so a changed app replays a stale routine until it fails.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE, with two changes: execute a retrieved routine deterministically (0 think calls), and add versioning plus quarantine after 2 consecutive checkpoint failures. Do not copy prompt-injection alone and call it caching.
- Negative flag: gains SHRINK with newer models. ReasoningBank re-ran AWM on WebArena: plus 3.6 points on Gemini 2.5 Flash, minus 0.9 points on Claude 3.7 Sonnet, near tie on Gemini 2.5 Pro (47.6 vs 46.7). AWM is not a guaranteed win on a strong model; measure it on Luna before assuming it.

### 6.3 SkillWeaver: self-improving skill APIs (Zheng et al., arXiv 2504.07079, 9 Apr 2025)

- Tried: web agents autonomously discover, practise, distil and hone reusable skills as lightweight Python async APIs over Playwright actions. Three stages: Skill Proposal, Skill Synthesis (distil successful trajectories into APIs), Skill Honing (test and debug APIs with generated test cases). 160 exploration iterations per site.
- Measured: WebArena average success with GPT-4o 22.6 percent to 29.8 percent (plus 31.8 percent relative); with GPT-4o-mini 9.2 percent to 14.1 percent. Real-world live websites 40.2 percent to 56.2 percent (plus 39.8 percent relative). APIs synthesised by a strong agent improve a weaker agent by up to 54.3 percent on WebArena. Limits reported by the authors: skill use at inference stays brittle, some verified APIs pass only because they avoid exceptions rather than truly solving the task, and complex long-horizon tasks still exceed the base agent planning capacity. One live site stayed at 11.1 percent.
- Applies: YES, as the implementation pattern for section 6.1. The honing stage is the part to copy literally: a synthesised routine is not trusted until it passes generated test cases against the real app, and its Usage Log records successes and failures. The Playwright API surface does not transfer to a Wayland desktop, but the API shape does: a Python function over the broker (windows, observe, act) with a docstring stating task, preconditions, and parameters.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE, as the build-and-hone pipeline for the procedure library. The 160-iteration exploration budget is a research number, not a target; this repo has dozens of traces, not hundreds, so hand the synthesis to the supervisor on successful traces only, never to blind exploration.

### 6.4 ReasoningBank: distil reasoning strategies, learn from failures (arXiv 2509.25140, 29 Sep 2025, v2 16 Mar 2026)

- Tried: memory framework that distils generalisable reasoning strategies from an agent's self-judged successful AND failed experiences, in three stages: retrieval, extraction, consolidation. Stores strategies, not raw trajectories and not only successful routines. Also adds MaTTS, memory-aware test-time scaling (parallel rollouts, k=5).
- Measured: WebArena with Gemini 2.5 Flash, success 40.5 percent to 48.8 percent (plus 8.3 points), average steps down up to 1.4; shopping subset minus 2.1 steps (26.9 percent fewer). SWE-Bench-Verified with Gemini 2.5 Pro 54.0 percent to 57.4 percent, minus 1.3 steps per task; with Flash 34.2 percent to 38.8 percent, minus 2.8 steps. Learning from failures as well as successes: 46.5 percent (success only) to 49.7 percent (both). With MaTTS k=5, WebArena 56.3 percent vs 46.7 percent no-memory baseline with Pro, steps 8.8 to 7.1. Ablation that matters: retrieving MORE memories hurts, success falls from 49.7 percent at k=1 to 44.4 percent at k=4.
- Applies: YES, two lessons. First, a failure lesson (what went wrong, in one line, attached to the routine) is worth storing alongside the routine; the repo already has failure modes worth one line each (S2 menu race, dialog-close capture failure, Save vs Save As confusion). Second, retrieve exactly ONE memory item (k=1). More context in the prompt is not better; it degrades the decision and costs tokens.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE. Store one strategy line per routine plus one failure line. Do NOT adopt MaTTS parallel rollouts for speed: k=5 multiplies cost for a success gain, and the track goal is think time, not success at any cost (see section 9.5).

### 6.5 SteP: stacked LLM policies, compose small policies (arXiv 2310.03720, Oct 2023, COLM 2024)

- Tried: define the control state as a STACK of policies. At each step the top policy acts directly, invokes a new policy (push), or terminates (pop). A library of 14 policies for WebArena, each covering multiple intents; complex tasks decompose into policy calls, e.g. book_flight decomposes into fill_text and choose_date.
- Measured: WebArena 14.9 percent to 35.8 percent over GPT-4 SOTA in the v2 abstract (the COLM 2024 camera-ready reports 33.5 percent; version discrepancy noted, use the range 33.5 to 35.8, not one number). Single-policy baseline 23 percent to 36 percent. Tokens per trajectory 2.3x fewer than the flat baseline; average context 22.7K tokens vs 52K for the flat 8k baseline. MiniWoB++ competitive while using significantly less data.
- Applies: PARTLY. The token saving is real and measured, and it comes from the same mechanism the track needs: a small policy sees a small context, not the whole history. For this repo the analogue is per-step tool and prompt pruning: the supervisor call for one routine step should carry that step's tools and state only, not the full session history. But SteP still makes a model call per policy step; it does not remove think calls, it shrinks them.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE as prompt scoping (small context per call), not as a new architecture. The stack-of-policies control flow is overkill for a desktop loop that already has a 3-level supervisor.

### 6.6 Anthropic Agent Skills: human-authored skill folders (announced 16 Oct 2025, open standard 18 Dec 2025)

- Tried: a Skill is a folder with SKILL.md (name, description, instructions) plus optional scripts, templates and reference files. The model loads the description (30 to 50 tokens) on every request and the full body only when the task needs it. A library of 20 skills costs about 1,000 tokens until one is loaded, vs tens of thousands for the equivalent system prompt.
- Measured: NO benchmark win reported. This is a packaging and context-management mechanism, not a measured speed result. The token arithmetic above is the vendor claim, not an independent measurement.
- Applies: YES as packaging. It is the cheapest possible form of section 6.1: the repo already has SKILL.md (87 lines) plus session protocol files; the missing piece is per-routine skill files that load on demand instead of one big prompt every call. The risk is supply chain: a Feb 2026 audit cited in secondary sources found flaws in 36.82 percent of 3,984 public skills; only his own authored skills should ever load in this harness.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE. Write routine skills as small on-demand files, load one at a time. Do not claim a measured saving for this; the saving is context size, and the think-time effect is UNMEASURED until a local A/B runs.

### 6.7 What plan caching is NOT (negative flags for this section)

- Semantic cache as a correctness mechanism: DO NOT DO. A similarity score is not a proof. Two prompts at 0.95 cosine can have different correct answers when they differ only in a filename, a number, or a negation. Exact-match memo on (app, app version, window geometry, dialog class, parameters) plus a postcondition check is the safe form; semantic retrieval is only a candidate finder, never the decider. Prior W-PLAN report section R6 already reached this verdict; the survey confirms it from the literature.
- AWM gains shrink or vanish on newer, stronger models (section 6.2). Do not assume a workflow-memory win measured on GPT-4 in 2024 transfers to Luna in 2026.
- Retrieving more memories hurts (ReasoningBank k=1 beats k=4, 49.7 vs 44.4 percent). A library that injects five routines into every prompt is worse than one that injects one.

## 7. Speculative and parallel planning

### 7.1 UFO2 speculative multi-action execution (Zhang et al., arXiv 2504.14603, Apr 2025, Microsoft)

- Tried: Windows desktop AgentOS. HostAgent decomposes, AppAgents execute with a unified GUI plus native-API action layer. One LLM call predicts N actions; each predicted action is validated live against Windows UI Automation state before execution; only validated actions run. Also Picture-in-Picture virtual desktop for parallel tasks, and retrieval-augmented memory from docs and logs.
- Measured: speculative multi-action execution gives 51 percent fewer LLM calls; action steps reduced by up to 50 percent. OSWorld-W with o1: 32.7 percent success vs 14.3 percent for OpenAI Operator. Windows Agent Arena: about 10 percent higher task completion, described as a 50 percent relative improvement over the best current CUA. Native API calls improve completion by over 8 percent. Hybrid UIA plus vision control detection recovers over 25 percent of previously failed interactions.
- Applies: YES, and this is the single most directly transferable speculative mechanism in the survey. The validation step is what makes it safe: predict a batch, check each action against live UI state, execute only the valid prefix, stop and re-plan at the first invalid action. That maps exactly onto this repo: predict a batch of broker actions, validate each against hyprctl window state and OCR crop tokens before executing it, never execute a predicted action blind. Limit: UFO2 validation uses Windows UIA; the Linux analogue is AT-SPI plus hyprctl plus OCR crops, which is weaker (GTK popup entries are not exposed, VS Code EditContext queries are uninformative). Validation coverage will be lower, so the safe batch will be shorter.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE, first among speculative ideas. Expected shape: one think call predicts a short batch (2 to 4 actions) for a predictable sequence (open, type, confirm), local validation gates each action, first failure stops the batch. ESTIMATE for this repo: if half the think calls become batches of 2, think calls fall about 25 percent on those tasks; the 51 percent paper number is a ceiling measured on Windows with UIA validation, not a promise for Wayland.

### 7.2 SpecExec for LLM agents: CLAIM UNVERIFIED, citation wrong (flagged, do not use)

- Claimed in secondary blogs (Oct 2026): SpecExec, Fast Speculative Execution for LLM Agents, UC Berkeley, Stanford, Databricks, arXiv 2406.02100, 2.8x end-to-end speedup on WebArena, ToolBench and SWE-bench, 78 percent speculative hit rate (77.5 percent in one version), per-step latency 4.2s to 1.5s, 100 percent exact equivalence to greedy execution via transactional copy-on-write sandboxing.
- Verification done in this survey: arXiv 2406.02100, opened 2026-11-18, is "Exploring Mathematical Extrapolation of Large Language Models with Synthetic Data" by Li et al., submitted 4 Jun 2024. It has nothing to do with speculative execution or agents. The blog citation is WRONG. No primary source for the agent SpecExec 2.8x and 78 percent numbers was found in the searches run. Treat those numbers as UNVERIFIED. Do not put them in the prototype plan, do not cite 2406.02100 for SpecExec. See section 16.
- Verdict: DO NOT USE until someone finds the real primary source. If the real paper exists, its mechanism (predict tool calls during thought generation, execute speculatively in a transactional sandbox, adopt on match, discard on miss) is still worth reading, but the numbers above are not evidence for it.

### 7.3 SpecExec speculative DECODING (Svirschevski et al., arXiv 2406.02532, Jun 2024, NeurIPS 2024)

- Tried: a different SpecExec from section 7.2, and the name collision is the trap. This one is token-level speculative decoding for local inference: a small draft model builds a large draft tree of most-probable continuations, the large target model validates the tree in one pass, up to 20 tokens accepted per iteration.
- Measured: 50B+ parameter models on consumer GPUs with RAM offloading at 4 to 6 tokens per second with 4-bit quantisation, 2 to 3 tokens per second with 16-bit weights; 10 to 18x speedup vs sequential inference on the same offloading hardware. Llama-2-70B single-token generation with offloading on an RTX 3090 takes at least 4.5s without it.
- Applies: NO, for this setup. Speculative decoding needs control of the draft model and the target model and access to token probabilities. His executor is a remote model reached through the Codex CLI; he has no draft model, no logits, and no inference server to modify. The 10 to 18x number is also an offloading-regime number (model does not fit in VRAM), not a general inference speedup.
- Verdict: DO NOT DO. Do not confuse with section 7.2; the shared name has already caused one wrong citation in the secondary literature.

### 7.4 SpecEyes: agentic-level speculative bypass (Huang et al., arXiv 2603.23483, 24 Mar 2026)

- Tried: a lightweight, tool-free small model speculatively answers queries that do not need the deep agentic loop, bypassing expensive tool-use chains. Cognitive gating by answer separability (margin among top-K logits, no oracle labels): confident answers are accepted directly, the rest fall back to the full agentic loop. A heterogeneous parallel funnel runs the stateless small model concurrently to mask the stateful large-model execution.
- Measured: 1.1x to 3.35x speedup over the agentic baseline on V* Bench, HR-Bench and POPE, accuracy preserved or improved up to plus 6.7 percent. Tool-free candidate fraction about 80 percent, speculative acceptance about 71 percent (averaged across benchmarks, min aggregation variant best).
- Applies: PARTLY, in shape only. The benchmarks are visual question answering, not desktop tasks, so the 1.1 to 3.35x number does not transfer. The transferable idea is the gate: a cheap local check that answers "is this step already decided?" before paying a Luna think call. In this repo the gate is not logits (no access) but a deterministic local check: file state, window state, OCR tokens. Where a local check decides the step, skip the think call entirely, which is stronger than SpecEyes (it still pays the small model).
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE (confidence-gated bypass), with local deterministic checks as the gate, not a small model with logits. Do not quote 3.35x for desktop work.

### 7.5 BunshinRL: whole-workspace forking for RL rollouts (Huang et al., Nov 2026, MLSys blog 13 Nov 2026)

- Tried: agentic RL rollout system built on BunshinFork, a live copy-on-write fork of a whole container workspace. Three techniques: memory sharing, tool-prefix caching (past tool calls form a prefix tree; a rollout skips every matching call and forks a writable branch at the first miss), and speculative tool execution (a small classifier predicts the next tool calls from recorded ones, forks the workspace and runs them while the model is still reasoning; on match the result is adopted, on miss the branch is discarded and the real call runs from the pre-speculation snapshot).
- Measured: training Qwen3-VL-8B with GRPO on OSWorld and WebArena shopping. Peak memory down up to 43 percent, total memory down up to 51 percent, end-to-end rollout latency down 23 percent, same reward. Peak memory down 30 percent vs CRIU and 26 percent vs AgentENV on OSWorld.
- Applies: NO as a system, YES for one idea. Whole-workspace forking is RL training infrastructure; this repo runs one live desktop, not parallel training rollouts, and there is no container to fork. Tool-prefix caching DOES transfer: if the current trajectory prefix matches a recorded successful prefix, skip re-deriving those steps. That is section 6 in another form. Speculative tool execution while the model reasons is the most interesting transfer, but it needs a sandbox to run speculative writes in; on a live desktop a speculative write has no undo, so only speculative READS are safe here.
- Verdict: DEFER the system. ADOPT the prefix-cache idea inside the procedure library. Speculative writes: DO NOT DO on the live seat.

### 7.6 LLMCompiler: parallel function calling as a compiled DAG (Kim et al., arXiv 2312.04511, Dec 2023, ICML 2024) - MERGED, cited in latency and planning surveys

- Tried: treat the agent plan as a compiler problem. A Function Calling Planner emits a DAG of tool calls with dependencies; a Task Fetching Unit dispatches calls whose dependencies are satisfied; an Executor runs them in parallel. Dynamic replanning feeds executor results back to the planner.
- Measured: vs ReAct, latency speedup up to 3.7x, cost savings up to 6.7x, accuracy improvement up to about 9 percent. HotpotQA 1.80x speedup and 3.37x cost cut; Movie Recommendation 3.74x and 6.73x; ParallelQA up to 2.27x speedup, 4.65x cost cut, 9 percent accuracy gain; Game of 24 2x vs Tree of Thoughts; WebShop up to 101.7x speedup and 25.7 percent success gain.
- Applies: PARTLY, with a hard limit. The gains are on embarrassingly parallel tool calls (independent searches, independent lookups). Desktop GUI steps are mostly SEQUENTIAL and dependent: you cannot click Save before the dialog opens, and you cannot type the filename before the field exists. The DAG for a desktop task is nearly a straight line, so the parallel speedup collapses to near 1x. Where it does apply is the observation side: independent read-only checks (window list, file bytes, OCR crop) can run concurrently in one batch instead of sequentially. The latency survey adds: the win comes from replacing N sequential model calls with 1 planning call plus parallel execution. Our loop already batches actions into one act request (the v3 discipline), so the easy part of this win is already banked. The remaining LLMCompiler idea is to let the model plan MULTIPLE steps ahead in one call (observe, act, verify as one plan) instead of one call per step. That is exactly the "decide the next step during the current gap" shape our P1 retry demonstrated (0.0s think before the retry act).
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE for reads only (planning survey) and for multi-step planning in one call (latency survey). Parallelise independent local checks; do not expect 3.7x on the action chain. Do not quote the 101.7x WebShop number for desktop work; it is a parallel-tool benchmark, not a GUI benchmark.

### 7.7 ReWOO: plan once, execute without re-thinking (Xu et al., arXiv 2305.18323, 23 May 2023)

- Tried: decouple reasoning from observations. Planner writes the whole plan up front (one LLM call), Workers execute tool calls in parallel without further LLM calls, Solver composes the answer (one LLM call). The planner never sees observations, so its reasoning can be distilled from a 175B teacher into a 7B LLaMA planner.
- Measured: 5x token efficiency and plus 4 percent accuracy on HotpotQA vs ReAct (42.4 percent accuracy on 2,000 tokens vs 40.8 percent on 10,000 tokens). Robust under tool-failure scenarios. Consistent gains across six NLP benchmarks.
- Applies: PARTLY. The token lesson transfers completely: do not re-send the full history and re-think after every observation; that is exactly the think-time cost this track is cutting. The full shape does NOT transfer: a desktop plan written without observations goes stale at the first unexpected dialog, and the paper's own trade is less flexibility for token efficiency. The repo already has the right compromise (plan once per subgoal, deterministic execution, replan only on checkpoint failure), which is ReWOO plus observation-gated replanning.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE. One planner call per subgoal, workers execute without think calls, solver only at the end. Do not adopt the static no-replan plan.

### 7.8 Plan-and-Act: separate planner and executor, train the planner (arXiv 2503.09572, Mar 2025)

- Tried: Planner LLM generates structured high-level plans; Executor LLM translates them into environment actions; feedback goes to both, and the Planner replans when needed. Synthetic data generation annotates ground-truth trajectories with feasible plans, plus 10,000 extra plans and 5,000 failure-analysis plans.
- Measured: WebArena-Lite 53.94 percent success, state of the art at publication (v3 reports 57.58 percent); WebVoyager text-only 81.36 percent. Ablation: no planner 9.85 percent; non-fine-tuned planner 29.63 percent; fine-tuned planner with 10,000 plans 44 percent; full system with replanning 53.94 percent. Most gains attributed to the Planner, not the Executor.
- Applies: PARTLY. The architecture is already his adopted shape (supervisor plans, deterministic executor runs). The training part does NOT apply: it fine-tunes LLaMA-3.3-70B as Planner and Executor, which needs a GPU cluster he does not have, and his API key is out of credit. The useful number is the ablation: the planner is worth about 20 points of success (9.85 to 29.63) before any fine-tuning, which supports spending think budget on ONE good plan up front rather than spreading it across many small re-thinks.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE (already adopted). DEFER the fine-tuning. Do not re-propose training a planner as a next step.

### 7.9 Agent S2: proactive hierarchical planning (arXiv 2504.00906, 1 Apr 2025)

- Tried: compositional generalist-specialist framework. Mixture-of-Grounding for precise GUI localisation, plus Proactive Hierarchical Planning that refines action plans at multiple temporal scales as observations evolve.
- Measured: 18.9 percent and 32.7 percent relative improvements over leading baselines (Claude Computer Use, UI-TARS) on OSWorld 15-step and 50-step evaluations. State of the art on three computer-use benchmarks at publication. No latency or think-time number was reported in the sources read for this survey.
- Applies: PARTLY. Hierarchical planning (a high-level plan refined at two time scales) is the right shape for long desktop tasks, and it is close to the adopted supervisor levels. But without a latency number, its think-time effect is UNMEASURED; a hierarchical planner that re-plans at every scale can easily cost MORE think calls than a flat plan, not fewer.
- Verdict: DEFER as a separate prototype. Fold its one transferable idea (re-plan at subgoal boundaries, not at every step) into the ReWOO-style prototype.

### 7.10 GTA1: test-time scaling, sample K proposals, judge picks one (Yang et al., arXiv 2507.05791, Jul 2025, ICLR 2026)

- Tried: two-stage agent (planner plus grounding model). At each step, sample K candidate action proposals IN PARALLEL, then a multimodal judge selects the best. K=1 reverts to a standard agent. Trades compute for decision quality.
- Measured: K=32 with a 15-step limit beats a K=1 baseline running 100 steps on OSWorld. GTA1-7B with GPT-5 as planner and judge: 61.0 percent OSWorld-Verified, 49.2 percent WindowsAgentArena. GTA1-7B with o3: 45.2 percent OSWorld at 100 steps, vs native CUA o3 42.9 percent at 200 steps. Grounding: GTA1-7B 50.1 percent ScreenSpot-Pro vs UGround-72B 34.5 percent. Cost: total token consumption and compute scale LINEARLY with K.
- Applies: NO for think time. Parallel sampling hides wall time only if the K calls run concurrently AND the judge call is cheap AND the calls are billed in parallel. Through one Codex CLI session, K parallel Luna calls are not a supported cheap primitive, and the judge adds a think call per step. The paper's own cost line is linear in K. It buys success, not speed; for this track that is the wrong trade.
- Verdict: DO NOT DO for speed. Note the success result for the record, but do not prototype K-way sampling as a think-time reduction. It is a think-time INCREASE with a success payoff.

### 7.11 Agent Q and R-MCTS: tree search over actions (Putta et al., arXiv 2408.07199, Aug 2024; R-MCTS arXiv 2410.02052, Oct 2024)

- Tried: Agent Q combines guided Monte Carlo Tree Search over candidate browser actions with a self-critique mechanism and iterative DPO fine-tuning on agent interactions. R-MCTS adds contrastive reflection and multi-agent debate for state evaluation, plus exploratory learning to distil the search into the model.
- Measured: Agent Q, LLaMA-3-70B on real OpenTable booking: zero-shot 18.6 percent to 81.7 percent after SFT plus DPO from one day of MCTS data collection, 95.4 percent with online MCTS at test time. WebShop score 52.4 to 78.5 (84.3 with online search), beating average human performance. R-MCTS with GPT-4o on VisualWebArena: 6 percent to 30 percent relative improvement over SOTA, BUT token consumption 7.3x to 10.1x the baseline (7.3x Classifieds, 7.6x Shopping, 9.7x to 10.1x with multi-agent debate value function).
- Applies: NO for online use on a live desktop. MCTS needs to try actions, observe, and backtrack. On a live desktop most actions are IRREVERSIBLE (a click that deletes, sends, or closes cannot be un-tried), and there is no resettable environment to run rollouts in. The offline part (distil search into the model via DPO) needs fine-tuning infrastructure he does not have. The 7.3x to 10.1x token cost is the opposite of the track goal.
- Verdict: DO NOT DO online tree search. The Agent Q offline lesson (a day of search data can lift a small model a lot) is noted for a future training discussion only, not for this speed track.

### 7.12 Tree of Thoughts: deliberate search, and the equal-budget refutation (Yao et al., arXiv 2305.10601, May 2023, NeurIPS 2023; Stechly et al. 2024)

- Tried: generate several candidate thoughts, score each with the model, keep the best, backtrack when needed. Breadth-first or depth-first search over reasoning.
- Measured: Game of 24 with GPT-4, chain-of-thought 4 percent vs Tree of Thoughts 74 percent. Cost: 5x to 100x more generated tokens than a single chain-of-thought call (paper's own range); a depth-3, branching-factor-4 tree needs up to 64 generation calls for path nodes alone, plus evaluation calls. Dollar figures at 2023 GPT-4 prices: ToT at branching factor 5, 0.74 dollars per case vs 0.47 for chain-of-thought best-of-100; creative writing about 5x a standard CoT call.
- Applies: NO. Stechly et al. (2024) tested reasoning strategies under EQUAL token budgets across five datasets and found chain-of-thought with self-consistency frequently outperforms Tree of Thoughts when compute is equalised. ToT's tree structure forces sequential evaluation that wastes budget on branching overhead. Also, ToT's evaluation step needs the model to reliably distinguish good from bad thoughts; using GPT-3.5 as evaluator instead of GPT-4 dropped Game of 24 from 74 percent to 64 percent. (Stechly finding is SECONDARY-sourced; primary paper not opened.)
- Verdict: DO NOT DO. Proven negative for speed at equal budget. This is the clearest "do not redo it" item in the survey: more branches cost more think calls, and the same budget spent on independent samples does better.

### 7.13 OSWorld-Human as the evaluation lens

- See section 3.4 for the full merged entry. The planning survey's verdict stands: USE AS THE EVALUATION LENS. No prototype needed; this paper is the reason the track pivoted to think time.

## 8. Code execution as the action

### 8.1 CodeAct: executable Python code as the action (Wang et al., arXiv 2402.01030, Feb 2024, ICML 2024)

- Tried: unify the action space into executable Python code. Instead of emitting JSON naming one tool, the agent writes a short program, runs it in a Python interpreter, and the output or error becomes the next observation. Code can loop, branch, keep variables across turns, and call libraries. Released CodeActInstruct (7K multi-turn interactions) and two fine-tuned models (Mistral-7B, Llama-2-7B).
- Measured: across 17 LLMs on APIBank and the new M3ToolEval benchmark, CodeAct outperforms text and JSON actions by up to 20 percent higher success rate. The mechanism matters more than the headline: one code action composes many tool calls, so a task that needed 40 to 50 sequential LLM round trips for pagination or repeated calls needs one program instead.
- Applies: YES, strongly. This is the cheapest structural change available: the harness already runs Python, so a code action is a program that calls the broker functions in a loop with local checks, instead of one broker call per think call. The risk is also the mechanism: a buggy program can do more damage in one turn than a single bad click, so code actions need the same sandboxing the repo already applies to deterministic execution (allowed action set, checked preconditions, no irreversible action without a checkpoint).
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE, top priority with section 8.3. This is the mechanism behind the synthesis plan's pick 1.

### 8.2 CoAct-1: orchestrator delegates to a Programmer or a GUI Operator (Song et al., arXiv 2508.03923, 5 Aug 2025, ICLR 2026, v3 20 Feb 2026)

- Tried: multi-agent system with three roles. Orchestrator dynamically delegates each subtask to either a GUI Operator (VLM, clicks and visual navigation) or a Programmer agent (writes and executes Python or Bash for file management, data processing, environment configuration). Coding bypasses inefficient GUI sequences where programmatic control is better; GUI is kept where visual interaction is necessary.
- Measured: OSWorld 60.76 percent success, state of the art at publication, vs Agent S2.5 at 55.98 percent; WindowsAgentArena 52.5 percent. Average steps 10.15 on OSWorld vs 15 for leading GUI agents (about one third fewer steps). Category wins where code helps most: Calc 70.21 percent, VS Code 78.26 percent, multi-application 47.88 percent. Implementation used AG2, OpenAI o3 for Orchestrator and o4-mini for Programmer, on Linux with an extended OSWorld server.
- Applies: YES in shape, NO in model choice. The delegation rule is exactly right for this repo: file work, text processing, and app configuration go to code; only genuinely visual steps go to GUI. The model choice does not transfer: o3 plus o4-mini on API billing is not his route (Codex Luna credits, API key out of credit), and three model roles mean three think calls per delegation unless the orchestrator decision is made once per subgoal.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE the delegation RULE (code first, GUI only when visual), with ONE Luna call per subgoal making the delegation decision, not a standing three-agent conversation. Do not copy the three-agent runtime.

### 8.3 OpenGCUA: code execution plus harness-to-model decision transfer (arXiv 2610.22200, 15 Oct 2026, Tongyi MAI / Alibaba)

- Tried: generalist computer-use agent where the model chooses how to observe, act, and organise work across visual and programmatic interfaces. Per step the harness executes a Python program using PyAutoGUI and bash, returning output and status. Decisions about what to observe and when to verify move from fixed harness rules to the model. Companion MAI-Gym is the RL gym behind it.
- Measured: OSWorld-v2 offline subset, 82 tasks (26 internet tasks excluded). Full configuration vs batched GUI-only baseline, across eight models including GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5: mean partial score 45.3 to 64.9 (plus 19.6 points), steps per task down 53.0 percent on average. Code execution alone vs GUI plus CLI baseline: plus 6.1 points and minus 14.8 percent steps. GPT-6 Astra: partial score 67.1 to 75.7, steps 102.1 to 33.5 (minus 67.2 percent). On-demand screenshots raised prompt-cache hit rate from 75.6 percent to 86.1 percent for Qwen3.8-Max and 61.9 percent to 76.3 percent for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Real-device macOS, 4 tasks: Qwen3.8-Max used 50.2K reasoning tokens per task vs 2.6K for GPT-6 Astra, completing 3 of 4 vs 4 of 4; model choice dominates cost there.
- Applies: YES, strongest direct evidence in the survey for desktop code-as-action. Two caveats, both in the paper's own framing. First, steps are MODEL CALLS, not wall-clock seconds; the paper does not establish an equal latency reduction, and a code call that writes a long program can itself be a long think call. Second, the baseline is batched GUI-only, so part of the 53 percent is batching, not code alone (code alone is plus 6.1 points, minus 14.8 percent steps). The honest prototype target is therefore the code-alone number first, then batching on top.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE, top priority. Measure think time per task, not just step count, because a longer code-writing think call can eat the step saving. Note for the merger: the synthesis plan already ranked code execution as pick 1 with an ESTIMATE of 35 to 50s per 100s; OpenGCUA is the evidence that estimate rests on, and its code-alone ablation (minus 14.8 percent steps) is the conservative bound to use, not the full 53 percent. The re-ranked prototype plan now uses the code-alone bound.

### 8.4 Programmatic tool calling, vendor claim (Anthropic, Nov 2025, secondary reports)

- Tried: let the model orchestrate tools through code rather than through individual API round trips (Anthropic programmatic tool calling, Nov 2025).
- Measured: vendor self-reported figures only: average token use on complex research tasks down 37 percent. No independent benchmark, no latency number, no desktop evaluation in the sources read.
- Applies: same direction as CodeAct and OpenGCUA, but weaker evidence. The 37 percent is a token number on research tasks, not a think-time number on desktop tasks.
- Verdict: SUPPORTING EVIDENCE only. Do not cite as a measured desktop win.

### 8.5 Beyond the GUI Paradigm: CLI beats GUI on steps (Gu et al., arXiv 2606.19388, Jun 2026)

- Source: Gu et al. 2026, via the blolabel.ai summary of the paper (SECONDARY for the numbers).
- Tried: compare CLI-oracle and GUI agents on the same AndroidWorld task suite (CLI-Advantage suite).
- Measured: a CLI oracle solves 88.8 percent of AndroidWorld in 10.7 steps vs 18.6 for GUI agents; Claude Code on Opus 4.7 reaches 71.8 percent on AndroidWorld with shell access and no mobile training.
- Applies: YES, independent support for code execution as the action (section 8.1 to 8.3). Every step replaced by a shell command is a think call removed, not just shortened. This is the strongest step-count result in the whole merged survey, and it is not a grounding model at all.
- Think-time lever: direct. Fewer steps means fewer think calls, and the CLI steps are also cheaper to reason about than visual grounding steps.

## 9. Verification and judging costs

### 9.1 Reflexion: verbal self-reflection after failure (Shinn et al., arXiv 2303.11366, Mar 2023, NeurIPS 2023)

- Tried: after a failed trial, an evaluator gives a binary or heuristic signal, the model writes a short verbal lesson about what went wrong, stores it in episodic memory, and the next attempt reads it. No weight updates. Max 4 trials in the paper's setup.
- Measured: HumanEval with GPT-4, pass at 1, 80.1 percent single sample to 91.0 percent with Reflexion. AlfWorld 130 of 134 tasks (ReAct plus Reflexion). HotPotQA up about 20 points. BUT MBPP Python got WORSE: 80.1 percent to 77.1 percent, because self-written unit tests had a 16.3 percent false-positive rate (solutions passing self-written tests but failing real ones) vs 1.4 percent on HumanEval. Ablation on the 50 hardest HumanEval Rust problems: base 0.60; reflection without test generation 0.52 (worse than nothing); tests without reflection 0.60; both together 0.68. No help on WebShop, where success needs creative exploration, not fixing one mistake. Cost: worst case with a 4-trial cap is 4 actor calls plus 3 reflector calls, about 7x a single attempt.
- Applies: YES, with the ablation as the rule. Reflection helps ONLY when paired with a GROUNDED external signal (a real test, a file check, an evaluator that is not the agent itself). Reflection on the agent's own opinion hurts. For this repo that means: reflect only after a checkpoint failure with a concrete local signal (file bytes wrong, dialog class wrong, OCR tokens missing), never as a per-step habit, and never let the agent write its own pass criteria.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE, failure-gated only. Per-step reflection is DO NOT DO; it is the 7x worst-case cost for a gain that vanishes without grounding.

### 9.2 ReflCtrl: reflection is 25 to 30 percent of reasoning tokens (arXiv 2512.13979, Dec 2025, NeurIPS 2025 workshop)

- Tried: segment reasoning into steps, identify reflection steps, extract a reflection direction in latent space, steer reflection frequency stepwise.
- Measured: self-reflection consumes 25 to 30 percent of total reasoning tokens. Steering redundant reflections away saves up to 33.6 percent of reasoning tokens while preserving performance, especially in stronger models. Reflection behaviour correlates with internal uncertainty.
- Applies: PARTLY. The measurement is the useful part: about a quarter to a third of think tokens are the model re-checking itself, and much of it is redundant. The METHOD does not transfer: representation steering needs model internals, and he reaches Luna through the Codex CLI, not through weights. The transferable version is a prompt and harness rule: do not ask the model to re-verify what a local check already proved, and cap reflection to one short paragraph on failure.
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE as a prompt rule. Do not attempt latent steering. The 33.6 percent is a ceiling from steering, not a promise for prompting; treat any local saving as UNMEASURED until tested.

### 9.3 Step-level cascade: verify at milestones, not every step (Wei et al., arXiv 2604.27151, 29 Apr 2026)

- Tried: small policy acts by default; two lightweight learned monitors watch the trajectory. Stuck Monitor detects repeated actions and no progress from recent history and triggers recovery by the strong model. Milestone Monitor identifies semantically meaningful checkpoints where sparse verification by the strong model is most informative. Strong model returns control to the small policy if the check passes, takes over only if it fails. Layers on top of existing agents without retraining.
- Measured: experiments on OSWorld and WebArena "consistently improve" the cascade over the baselines, per the abstract. NO exact percentage, step count, or latency number appeared in the sources read for this survey (abstract plus PDF first pages). Do not quote a magnitude for this paper; it is UNMEASURED here.
- Applies: YES, as the verification policy this track should adopt. The design is the answer to the verification-cost question: per-step verification is costly, end-only verification is too late, milestone verification is the middle that the paper argues for. In this repo the monitors do not need to be learned models: a stuck detector is a local rule (same action twice, no state change) and a milestone is a subgoal boundary in the plan (dialog open, file saved).
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE the policy (local stuck rule plus subgoal verification), not the learned monitors. This is the cheapest verification design in the survey.

### 9.4 VeriCUA: agentic verification is 10x the tokens (Google and University of Hong Kong, Oct 2026; blog summary 31 Oct 2026)

- Source: numbers quoted from the saiteja.ai summary blog (SECONDARY), not from the paper text.
- Tried: verification as agentic exploration. After the agent finishes, a verifier builds a rubric from the environment, searches GUI and stored state for evidence, and never accepts the agent's own claim as proof. Four stages, runs after the task, not during it.
- Measured: online (AndroidWorld, OSWorld, ScienceBoard), highest accuracy and lowest false-positive rate in all three; AndroidWorld false-positive rate 7.7 percent vs 16.9 percent for the auto-generated verifier. OSReward-Hard 70.4 vs 65.7 best baseline (plus 4.7). BUT cost on OSReward: 345K to 378K tokens per trajectory vs 33K to 35K for the three baseline judges COMBINED, about 10x. Online: about 39K tokens and 97 seconds per episode vs 16K tokens and 60 seconds for the generated verifier. Recall on OSWorld 71.3 vs 78.1 for the generated verifier (worse). VeriCUA decides only 70 to 82 percent of episodes; abstentions count as fail. A single-call judge on Claude Opus 4.8 scores 69.7 on OSReward-Hard vs VeriCUA on Flash at 70.4; several frontier single-call judges score 89 to 90 on full OSReward, above VeriCUA's 87.2.
- Applies: NO as an in-loop verifier. A 10x token bill and 97 seconds per episode for plus 4.7 points, with worse recall on OSWorld, is the opposite of the track goal. The lesson is narrower: a stronger single-call judge closes most of the gap, so if verification is needed at all, one short call beats an agentic verification loop.
- Verdict: DO NOT DO agentic verification in the loop. Flagged as a proven cost-negative for speed.

### 9.5 BJudge: parallel rollouts plus a judge win OSWorld, at 891 seconds a task (Scaling Agents for Computer Use, TMLR under review, 2026)

- Tried: wide scaling. Generate multiple trajectories in parallel, convert each dense trajectory into a compact behaviour narrative (what the agent did and how it affected the environment, irrelevant step detail filtered out), and let a judge select the best trajectory.
- Measured: OSWorld 72.6 percent success at 100 steps, vs previous best 63.4 percent and human performance 72.36 percent (plus 9.2 points). Cost table with GPT-5: single rollout average 0.72 dollars and 891 seconds per task (median 626s); behaviour-narrative generation 0.11 dollars and 433.4 seconds average; judging N=10, 0.03 dollars and 226 seconds average (median 53.7s). The judging is cheap; the rollouts are the cost.
- Applies: NO for speed. 891 seconds per task is 24x his clean-task mean of 36.4s, before any judging. Parallel rollouts multiply wall time and cost for a success gain; the track goal is the reverse trade. The one transferable idea is the behaviour narrative: a compact summary of what actually happened is a cheaper judge input than a full trace, IF a judge is ever needed.
- Verdict: DO NOT DO parallel rollouts. Proven cost-negative for think time, even though it wins on success.

### 9.6 Agent-as-a-Judge: judge by searching for evidence, not reading the whole trace (ICML 2025; Judgment Labs 2026)

- Tried: an agentic evaluator that observes and gives step-by-step fine-grained assessments of another agent, searching the trajectory for evidence instead of reading all of it. DevAI benchmark: 55 realistic code-generation tasks, 365 hierarchical requirements.
- Measured: agreement with human experts about 90 percent vs 70 percent for LLM-as-a-Judge; evaluation time and cost down about 97 percent (86 hours and 1,297 dollars to about 2 hours and 31 dollars) on DevAI. Judgment Labs, long trajectories: Agent-as-a-Judge scored 0.15 higher evidence F1 up to 1M tokens at 42 percent lower cost than LLM-as-a-Judge, and 0.21 higher past 1M tokens at 62 percent lower cost; at 2M tokens, 0.80 F1 at 0.49 dollars vs 0.60 F1 at 1.48 dollars for LLM-as-a-Judge.
- Applies: PARTLY, and only for OFFLINE evaluation, not for the live loop. These are evaluation-cost numbers, not in-loop verification costs. The transferable design is: a judge should query for the specific evidence it needs (one file check, one OCR crop) instead of re-reading the whole trace and screenshot history. That is exactly the OSWorld-Human lesson (history growth makes later calls 3x slower).
- Verdict: ADOPT SHAPE for offline judging of prototypes. DO NOT DO as a per-step live judge.

## 10. Multi-action batching with validation (cross-cut)

This section is a cross-cut, not new sources: the batching evidence lives in sections 7.1, 7.6, 8.1 and 8.3. Collected here so the ranking can treat it as one item.

- UFO2 (7.1): 51 percent fewer LLM calls, steps down up to 50 percent, with live UI-state validation per action. MEASURED, Windows, UIA validation.
- OpenGCUA batched GUI baseline (8.3): batching alone is part of the 53 percent step cut vs single-action GUI; code alone adds plus 6.1 points and minus 14.8 percent steps over batched GUI plus CLI. MEASURED, OSWorld-v2 offline.
- CodeAct (8.1): one code action composes many tool calls; up to 20 percent higher success than JSON or text actions. MEASURED, APIBank plus M3ToolEval.
- LLMCompiler (7.6): parallel calls help only when calls are independent; desktop action chains are mostly dependent, so batching helps as SEQUENTIAL batching with validation (UFO2 shape), not as parallel execution.
- Rule that every source above agrees on, stated plainly: batch READS freely, batch WRITES only with a per-action local validation and a stop at first failure, never execute a predicted write blind, and never run two writers on one seat. The repo's own evidence supports the rule: the P1 screen-changed refusal cost about 90 seconds of recovery in the profile run, which is what an unvalidated batch would have risked on every task.
- Verdict: PROTOTYPE as UFO2-shape batching (predict 2 to 4, validate each locally, stop at first failure). Do not prototype unvalidated batching; no source supports it.

## 11. Structured outputs and constrained decoding

### 11.1 Outlines: efficient guided generation (Willard and Louf, arXiv 2307.09702, 2023)

- Source: Brandon Willard, Remi Louf, "Efficient Guided Generation for Large Language Models", arXiv 2307.09702, Jul 2023 (the Outlines paper).
- Tried: compile a regex or JSON Schema into a deterministic finite automaton, mask invalid tokens at generation time so output is schema-valid by construction.
- Measured: the paper's contribution is the mechanism and its efficiency vs naive masking; the latency numbers practitioners cite are that early Outlines deployments added 10 percent to 30 percent latency from FSM state tracking, and compiling a complex schema could take 40 seconds to over 10 minutes on first use (zylos.ai review, SECONDARY).
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. Our act step already emits a small JSON action envelope, and a parse failure costs a full extra think cycle (14.9s mean). Constrained decoding trades a small per-token overhead for zero parse failures. But our executor is remote Codex, so we only get constrained decoding if the Codex CLI or API exposes it (see 11.3). If it does, use it for the action envelope only, with a flat schema (see 11.2: depth-1 JSON is about 15 percent faster than depth-4).

### 11.2 XGrammar (Dong et al., arXiv 2411.15100, Nov 2024)

- Source: XGrammar team (CMU/MLC), "XGrammar: Flexible and Efficient Structured Generation Engine for Large Language Models", arXiv 2411.15100, Nov 2024. Numbers via the microscale.academy lesson (SECONDARY).
- Tried: precompute the allowed-token set per FSM state (context-independent tokens cover about 99 percent of the vocabulary), so the per-token mask is nearly free; overlap CPU mask computation with the GPU forward pass.
- Measured: about 1 percent time-per-output-token overhead (6.2ms to 6.3ms at batch 1, 9.0ms to 9.1ms at batch 16 on Llama-3.1-8B on one H100). Up to 3x speedup over prior structured-output stacks on JSON Schema, up to 100x on nested CFG workloads. Mask generation under 40 microseconds per token.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY directly (needs a self-hosted vLLM or SGLang server), but it proves constrained decoding is effectively free on a modern stack, so the cost argument against it is gone. If a local model path is built (section 13), use XGrammar or SGLang, not the old Outlines path.

### 11.3 Provider structured outputs (OpenAI, Aug 2024; llguidance migration, May 2025)

- Source: OpenAI structured outputs launch, Aug 2024; migration to llguidance (Microsoft Rust library) reported May 2025. Latency figures from the braiviq.com production guide (SECONDARY).
- Tried: native structured outputs with strict JSON Schema enforcement, backed by constrained decoding on the provider side.
- Measured: prompt-only JSON fails 5 percent to 10 percent of the time; JSON mode 2 percent to 5 percent; provider structured outputs under 0.1 percent; FSM-constrained decoding 100 percent compliance. Provider structured outputs add about 100ms per call vs 200ms to 500ms per retry for repair-based approaches. SGLang with jump-forward decoding measured on an RTX 4090, Llama-3.1-8B, 6-field JSON, batch 1: 490ms P50 and 740ms P99 with 0 percent parse failures, vs free-form plus regex 810ms P50 and 1,420ms P99 with 3.2 percent failures, and free-form plus retry 920ms P50 and 1,980ms P99 with 0.8 percent failures.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. The 100ms cost vs a 14.9s think step is noise, and it kills the parse-failure retry loop outright. OPEN CHECK: does the Codex CLI expose a structured-output or JSON-schema option for the action envelope, or does the broker already validate the envelope? If yes, turn it on for act steps. If no, do not build a local constrained-decoding proxy; the win is one retry per few tasks, not a think-time cut.

### 11.4 CRANE: constrained decoding can hurt reasoning (Feb 2025, ICML 2025) - NEGATIVE

- Source: CRANE paper, Feb 2025, ICML 2025, via the zylos.ai structured-output review (SECONDARY).
- Tried: alternate between constrained and unconstrained decoding windows instead of constraining the whole generation.
- Measured: grammar constraints can force the model away from high-probability token sequences and degrade reasoning quality; alternating windows recovers up to 10 percentage points on symbolic reasoning benchmarks.
- Applies: YES, as a NEGATIVE guardrail. Constrain ONLY the final action JSON, never the reasoning trace. If the model must reason in the same call, let it reason unconstrained and constrain only the output envelope. Do not put the thinking itself inside a schema.

## 12. Parallel and asynchronous function calling

### 12.1 LLMCompiler (Kim et al., arXiv 2312.04511, Dec 2023, ICML 2024)

- See section 7.6 for the full merged entry. The latency survey's version adds: vs ReAct, latency speedup 1.80x on HotpotQA and 3.74x on Movie Recommendation, cost down 3.37x and 6.73x, accuracy up about 9 percent on the dependency-pattern tasks (up to 2.27x speedup, 4.65x cost cut). Up to 35 percent faster than OpenAI's native parallel function calling on the same benchmarks. On the Game of 24 task, up to 101.7x speedup and 25.7 percent better success rate vs baselines (an extreme case, not representative).
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. The win comes from replacing N sequential model calls with 1 planning call plus parallel execution. Our loop already batches actions into one act request (the v3 discipline), so the easy part of this win is already banked. The remaining idea is to let the model plan MULTIPLE steps ahead in one call instead of one call per step.

### 12.2 AsyncLM: asynchronous function calling (Gim, Lee, Zhong, Yale, arXiv 2412.07017, Dec 2024)

- Source: In Gim, Seung-seob Lee, Lin Zhong, "Asynchronous LLM Function Calling", arXiv 2412.07017, Dec 2024.
- Tried: let the LLM generate and execute function calls concurrently, with an interrupt mechanism that notifies the model in flight when a call returns. Implemented on Llama 3 (fine-tuned) and emulated on GPT-4o with in-context prompting.
- Measured: end-to-end task completion latency down 1.6x to 5.4x vs synchronous function calling on Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) tasks; up to 2.1x vs synchronous parallel calling. Accuracy maintained on fine-tuned Llama models. Function execution times in the benchmark averaged 110ms (range 30ms to 500ms).
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY as published (needs fine-tuning for the interrupt semantics, or a co-designed inference process; the GPT-4o numbers are emulated from a 5ms-per-token assumption, not measured end to end). The IDEA applies in a weaker form: our broker service is only 0.16s to 1.09s per request, so overlapping generation with execution saves at most about 1s per task. Do not chase AsyncLM; the overlap win is capped by our tiny service times.

### 12.3 AsyncFC: future-based async function calling, no retraining (Feng et al., arXiv 2605.15077, May 2026)

- Source: Feng, Mao, Dutta, Gonzalez, "AsyncFC", arXiv 2605.15077, May 2026, via the agentpatterns.ai summary (SECONDARY).
- Tried: keep the standard synchronous schema, but let the model issue futures for slow calls and continue generating while they run. Stock models, no retraining.
- Measured: speedup 1.26x on BFCL v3 sequential function calling, 1.26x on BFCL v4 web search, 1.44x on SWE-bench Lite, 1.24x on HotpotQA, 1.17x cross-model on Gemini 3.1 Pro. Gains come from decode-execution overlap and inter-function parallelism; SWE-bench shows the largest gain because file reads and test runs take seconds.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the current loop for the same reason as 12.2: our tool calls take 0.16s to 1.09s, not seconds, so overlap saves little. It would matter only if an act step ever blocks on a slow command (a long build, a download). Note as a conditional: if a future task involves slow shell commands, issue them as background futures and let the model do the next think while they run.

## 13. Speculative decoding and local serving (only helps a local model, not the remote executor)

### 13.1 Speculative decoding, original paper (Leviathan, Kalman, Matias, Google, arXiv 2211.17192, Nov 2022)

- Source: Yaniv Leviathan, Matan Kalman, Yossi Matias, "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2211.17192, Nov 2022.
- Tried: a small draft model proposes several tokens, the large target model verifies them all in one forward pass, keeping only the accepted prefix. Output distribution is provably identical to the target model alone.
- Measured: 2x to 3x speedup on T5-XXL; 2x to 2.5x on Chinchilla 70B (reported in the morphllm.com review of the paper).
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the remote Codex executor (we do not control its decoding). It applies ONLY to a local model, and only at batch size 1, which is our case. See 13.4 for the local-model verdict.

### 13.2 Medusa (Cai et al., 2024) and EAGLE / EAGLE-3 (Li et al., 2024-2025)

- Source: Cai et al., "Medusa: Simple LLM Inference Acceleration Framework with Multiple Decoding Heads", 2024. Li et al., "EAGLE: Speculative Sampling Requires Rethinking Feature Uncertainty" and "EAGLE-3: Scaling up Inference Acceleration of Large Language Models via Training-Time Test", arXiv 2503.01840, Mar 2025. Numbers via the agentnative.dev and morphllm.com comparisons (SECONDARY).
- Tried: Medusa adds extra decoding heads to the target model to predict several future tokens at once. EAGLE predicts from the target model's own feature layer with a one-layer draft head; EAGLE-3 adds multi-layer fusion and direct token prediction.
- Measured: Medusa-1 over 2.2x, Medusa-2 2.3x to 3.6x. EAGLE 2.7x to 3.5x on LLaMA2-Chat 70B; EAGLE-2 3.05x to 4.26x; EAGLE-3 up to 6.5x vs vanilla decoding, acceptance length up to about 7.5 tokens on HumanEval, about 1.4x better than EAGLE-2. Independent 500-prompt production benchmark (youngju.dev, 2026): EAGLE-3 2.89x, EAGLE-1 2.52x, Medusa-2 2.21x, vanilla draft model 1.95x, prompt-lookup 1.38x. Red Hat vLLM testing: EAGLE-3 up to 2.5x, per-request latency down 1.8x (8B) and 1.6x (70B) at low request rates.
- Applies: DOES NOT APPLY to the remote executor. For a local model it is the largest lossless speedup available, but it needs a trained draft head per target model (EAGLE training takes 4 to 8 hours on 4 GPUs for a custom target) and it is only validated for the models that ship heads. Do not build this before the local-model question (13.4) is answered.

### 13.3 Speculative decoding can be SLOWER than baseline - NEGATIVE (vLLM AMD benchmarks, 2026)

- Source: vLLM post on AMD GPUs, 2026, via the dev.to summary (SECONDARY).
- Tried: EAGLE-3 and DFlash speculative decoding on gemma-4-26B and Qwen3-8B, MATH500.
- Measured: on gemma-4-26B, DFlash reached 2.87x throughput and EAGLE-3 2.11x to 2.27x. On Qwen3-8B, EAGLE-3 with one drafted token ran at 0.44x of baseline on MATH500, SLOWER than no speculation. The serving-latency study arXiv 2406.14066 found gains eroding past about 12 requests per second and reversing past about 16. The vLLM docs for v0.8.2 state speculative decoding there "is not yet optimized and does not usually yield inter-token latency reductions" for all prompt datasets or sampling parameters. Independent benchmarking measured 1.96x at batch size 1 falling to 1.21x at batch 128.
- Applies: YES, as a NEGATIVE guardrail. Any speculative-decoding prototype must measure acceptance rate and end-to-end throughput on our own workload before adopting; a headline 6.5x can be 0.44x on the wrong model and settings. Do not adopt on paper numbers.

### 13.4 Small local models on the RTX 4050 6GB (practitioner benchmarks, 2026)

- Source: practitioner benchmarks, 2026 (SECONDARY): gsandip.com.np sub-12B agent-model roundup; dev.to local-LLM guide; freesoftlabs.com 5-runner benchmark on an Intel N100 mini PC.
- Tried: run Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, Phi-4-mini 3.8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, SmolLM2 1.7B, Llama 3.2 3B via Ollama or llama.cpp at Q4_K_M GGUF quantization on consumer hardware.
- Measured: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B at Q4_K_M is about 4.7GB on disk and about 5.2GB VRAM, 32k context (up to 128k). Reported 40 to 90 tokens per second across the sub-12B set on consumer GPUs; 20 to 40 tok/s is the cited range for 6 to 8GB VRAM running a 7B at Q4. A 7B at Q4 on CPU-only (Intel i7-13700H) does about 15 tok/s; on an N100 mini PC, llama.cpp does 4.2 tok/s for a 7B and 8.7 tok/s for a 3B. The memory-bandwidth ceiling is the binding constraint: one token requires reading every weight once, so tokens per second is roughly bandwidth divided by model size in memory.
- Applies: APPLIES WITH CONDITIONS. A 7B Q4 model FITS in 6GB VRAM (about 5.2GB) but leaves almost no room for KV cache, so long histories will spill or evict. That makes a local 7B viable ONLY for short, structured steps: grounding coordinates, classifying a step as easy or hard, verifying an exact string, emitting a single JSON action. It is NOT viable as a drop-in replacement for the full think step. Test fit first (load Qwen2.5-Coder-7B Q4_K_M, measure tok/s and KV-cache headroom with a 2k-token context), then test it on ONE step type (verify, the cheapest) before anything else. Note the conflict resolved in the synthesis log: UI-TARS-1.5-2B fits and is the first grounding candidate, 7B is borderline, Mage-VL-Cowork 4B does not fit unquantized at 9.48GB. This merged survey re-ranks the grounding candidates: Ferret-UI Lite 3B first, UI-TARS-1.5-2B second (section 4.4, 4.9).

## 14. Measurement: token and latency instrumentation

### 14.1 What to log per step (synthesis of 2.9, 14.2, 14.3)

- Source: this survey's synthesis, grounded in the sources below. No new paper.
- Tried (recommended): log per request, not per task: step type (windows, observe, act, verify), think gap (previous result to DECIDE line), service time, input tokens, output tokens, reasoning tokens (output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens on OpenAI-family APIs), cached tokens (usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens), screenshot bytes and pixel size, prompt token count, and the step's outcome (success, refusal, parse failure).
- Measured (from our own profile): think is only measurable as the gap to the DECIDE line today, which lumps model reasoning, vision reading, and tool-call latency together. The split between those three is not measurable from the current records. Adding the token fields above is what makes the split measurable: if reasoning tokens fall but the gap does not, the cost is vision reading or network, not reasoning.
- Applies: YES, immediate and cheap. This is the highest-value measurement change: it costs zero model calls and it is what lets every prototype in section 17 prove a think-time saving instead of claiming one. Do it before prototyping.

### 14.2 cua-speedrun timing split: agent time vs environment time (Aggarwal et al., Sep 2026)

- Source: same as 3.5, arXiv 2609.40284.
- Tried: start the clock at the instruction, stop it at termination, split each task into agent time and environment time, exclude setup and verification.
- Measured: see 3.5. The split is what exposed the negative result: environment time fell 8.72s while agent time rose 18.20s.
- Applies: YES. Adopt the split as our standard reporting shape: think time, broker service time, and wall time, per step and per task. Never report wall time alone; a wall-time win that comes from a faster environment can hide an agent-time loss.

### 14.3 Prompt Compression in the Wild profiler: predict the break-even (Kummer et al., Apr 2026)

- Source: same as 2.5, arXiv 2604.02985.
- Tried: an open-source profiler that predicts, for any model and GPU pair, the prompt length at which compression yields a net benefit.
- Measured: see 2.5 (up to 18 percent end-to-end speedup, only above about 5k tokens).
- Applies: YES as a method, not just a result. The discipline transfers to every prototype: predeclare the break-even (prompt length, escalation rate, acceptance rate) and measure against it on the held-out tasks, instead of adopting on a headline number.

### 14.4 What to measure next (so the next worker does not guess)

1. Gap split: instrument the executor loop with timestamps for image receipt, vision consumption, and decision (GROUND measurement 2). Until this exists, no observation-compression claim can be stated as a think-time saving.
2. Local grounder A/B on held-out tasks: Ferret-UI Lite-3B vs UI-TARS-1.5-2B vs Luna-only grounding, on the same 4 held-out tasks, reporting think-before-act time, misclicks, steps, and end-to-end wall. Include Vietnamese dialog text cases; the benchmarks are English-only and will flatter both models.
3. Crop gating A/B: full-frame grounding vs predict-then-verify-crop vs crop-only-on-small-targets, on small-target cases (Pinta toolbar, VS Code menus, GTK dialogs), reporting attempts per control and think time.
4. Read-mode A/B: verification steps served by OCR/a11y text vs by full screenshot, reporting think time and verification errors (missed state changes).
5. A11y coverage matrix per suite app (GROUND measurement 5): which controls expose pressed/checked/selected and native click, and whether get_extents returns sane boxes, per app (VS Code, Pinta, Nautilus, GTK file chooser, Zen, Omawrite, Omacalc, Kitty).
6. Step-count accounting on every prototype: report steps, think time, and wall together, with the step cap stated. A win that costs more steps is not a win.
7. BiGrounder verification: independently reproduce its 72.4 AndroidWorld / 42.8 OSWorld grounder-plus-GPT-4o numbers before re-ranking on them. If reproduced, it becomes the rank 3 candidate 1.
8. Reasoning-effort open questions: per-call vs session-level override in the Codex CLI; structured-output support on the Codex path; cached-token fields in one Codex usage record. All three are read-only checks, no build.

## 15. Consolidated do-not-redo list (proven failures from all three surveys)

One list, deduplicated. Every item has measured evidence in the cited entry. Prototype workers must not pick any of these.

1. Faster screenshots without a redraw guard make the agent slower (3.5, cua-speedrun, Sep 2026). Environment time down 8.72s, agent time up 18.20s, task time up 9.48s. Any observation speedup needs a settle or redraw check.
2. Standalone prompt compression on short prompts loses to its own overhead (2.5, Kummer et al., Apr 2026). Up to 18 percent end-to-end speedup only above about 5k tokens with matched hardware; outside that window the compressor dominates and cancels the gain. Our prompts are short. Measure prompt tokens first; do not build a compressor first.
3. Speculative decoding can run slower than baseline on the wrong model and settings (13.3, 2026). EAGLE-3 at 0.44x on Qwen3-8B MATH500. Measure acceptance rate on our workload first. Through the Codex API it is not available at all: no draft model, no logits, no inference control (7.3).
4. More thinking eventually hurts accuracy, not just latency (1.4, Zhou et al., Apr 2026; 1.5, Guan et al., Oct 2026). Past about 7,000 reasoning tokens the model flips more right answers to wrong than the reverse; 64.3 percent of thinking tokens come after accuracy already peaked. Uncapped max reasoning is not a safe default.
5. LLM summarization of history is no better than simple observation masking, and it costs an extra model call (3.1, Lindenbauer, Aug 2025). Masking halves cost and matches or beats summarization on solve rate. Do not build a summarizer before testing masking.
6. Cascades pay the sum of both models on escalation (5.1, FrugalGPT reviews). If the escalation rate is high, a cascade is slower than calling the strong model directly. Measure the escalation rate; if it exceeds about 30 percent, drop the cascade.
7. Constrained decoding over the whole generation degrades reasoning by up to 10 points (11.4, CRANE, Feb 2025). Constrain only the final action JSON, never the reasoning trace.
8. Long context costs more per generated token even with perfect KV-cache reuse (2.12, NVIDIA 2026). Context growth from 16K to 256K drives cost per token up 3.6x to 7x. History growth is a latency cost, not just a token cost.
9. Over-reasoning degrades the model's own confidence signal (1.7, arXiv 2508.15050). Do not route or accept on self-reported confidence after a max-effort think; use external checks.
10. Parallel and async function calling save little when tool calls are fast (12.2, 12.3). Our broker service is 0.16s to 1.09s per request; overlap saves at most about 1s per task. Do not chase AsyncLM.
11. Full a11y-tree dumps as observation (3.10, 3.11). Measured: 5,000 to 15,000 tokens per perception vs 1,200 to 5,000 for a screenshot. OSWorld-Human: adding a11y trees increases per-task latency. Mage-VL: adding a11y data increased mean steps 25.0 to 35.33. Locally rejected twice. Only skeleton or query-scoped reads are admissible.
12. A11y-only grounding, no images (3.10). Fails by construction: a11y.py has no coordinate path, GTK popups are not exposed, VS Code editor surface is uninformative. VisualWebArena text-only is 7.3 percent vs 15.1 with a screenshot. Rejected locally, confirmed by the literature.
13. Fully blind execution: predict coordinates, click, no fresh image (grounding survey). Rejected twice locally. No paper supports it; every zoom and grounding paper verifies against a fresh crop. The tc01 4/4 misclick history is the local proof.
14. Observation and pixel caching: reuse delivered images as ground truth (3.8). Rejected in the 10x synthesis and by GROUND rank 7. No counter-evidence found. Diff-based WHERE-to-look is fine; reused pixels as ground truth is not.
15. Token merging or pooling of visual tokens before grounding (4.11, ShowUI ablation). 70.8 to 42.3 percent. Token selection (which keeps positional encodings) is almost lossless at 70.8 to 70.4 percent. Never pool; select.
16. CogAgent on this laptop (4.5). 29GB VRAM minimum at BF16; INT4 loses significant accuracy per the project README. Hard hardware reject, not a judgment on the model.
17. 7B grounders as the default local model (4.9). Borderline on 6GB VRAM with KV cache and vision encoder, and beaten on Pro by 3B Ferret-UI Lite (53.3 vs UI-TARS-1.5-7B 49.6, Jedi-7B 39.5, UGround-v1-7B about 32).
18. Higher resolution is not always better (3.6, FastFocus "semantic fragmentation"). Excessive resolution disperses attention and causes coordinate hallucination. A low-res first pass can beat a native-res first pass. Do not raise max-width as a grounding fix without an A/B on misclicks.
19. More steps is not a free accuracy lever (4.15). OSWorld-Verified: one model swings 9.1 to 23.0 percent on step budget alone. A prototype that "wins" by taking more steps has not won on think time; always report steps and think time together.
20. Unverified vendor claims treated as results (section 16). DirectShell "85 percent task success using only a11y text", MAI-UI "76.7 percent AndroidWorld", InnvoLabs "94.8 percent task completion", Mobilerun "100 percent AndroidWorld", and the BiGrounder 72.4/42.8 end-to-end numbers are all single-source, self-reported, or very recent without independent reproduction. Use them as leads, never as ranking evidence.
21. Rust rewrite of the harness (grounding survey, Track 1 verdict 2026-11-18, STAY PYTHON). Pure Python CPU is only 1.0 to 1.5s of a 101s task; the Rust observation-store port is measurably SLOWER than Python. Do not redo.
22. Downscaling as a think-time fix, unmeasured (3.6). The byte saving is proven locally (10 to 32x for crops); the think-time saving is not, because the gap split (vision vs reasoning inside the think gap) is still unmeasured. Do not claim a downscale win until that split is instrumented.
23. Tree of Thoughts per step (7.12). Yao 2023, 5x to 100x tokens; Stechly 2024, CoT plus self-consistency frequently wins at equal budget. More branches cost more think calls; same budget spent on independent samples does better.
24. Online MCTS / Agent Q search (7.11). Agent Q needs resettable env; R-MCTS costs 7.3x to 10.1x tokens. Live desktop actions are irreversible; no rollout environment; token cost is the opposite of the goal.
25. GTA1 K-way sampling per step (7.10). Token cost scales linearly with K. Buys success, not speed; judge adds a call per step.
26. Parallel rollouts, BJudge style (9.5). 891s average per task, single rollout. 24x his clean-task mean before judging.
27. Per-step judge / agentic verification (9.4). VeriCUA 10x tokens, 97s per episode, worse OSWorld recall. Verification loop costs more than the task it checks.
28. Per-step reflection (9.1). Reflexion worst case about 7x a single attempt; reflection without a grounded test scores worse than nothing (0.52 vs 0.60). Reflection is a failure response, not a habit.
29. Semantic cache as a correctness mechanism (6.7). Similarity is not proof; filename, number, or negation changes flip the answer. Fast wrong answer is worse than slow right answer.
30. AWM gains on newer models (6.2). ReasoningBank re-run: minus 0.9 points on Claude 3.7 Sonnet. A 2024 GPT-4 win does not transfer to a 2026 strong model.
31. More retrieved memories (6.4). ReasoningBank k=4, 44.4 percent vs k=1, 49.7 percent. More context degrades the decision and costs tokens.
32. Wholescale small-model swap (planning survey). Small models plan poorly (Ferret-UI Lite navigation 19.8 percent OSWorld); small model as planner is not a speed win if it fails and retries. Small by default with big-model escalation is the shape; small INSTEAD of big is not.
33. Fine-tune a planner (7.8). Plan-and-Act needs LLaMA-3.3-70B fine-tuning; JEDI-style training needs a GPU cluster. No cluster, no API credit; shape only, not training.
34. Speculative writes on the live seat (7.5). No sandbox, no undo on a live desktop; only speculative READS are safe here.
35. SpecExec agent 2.8x claim (7.2). Cited arXiv ID 2406.02100 is a math paper, not SpecExec. Numbers unverified; do not use until a real primary source is found.

## 17. Ranked shortlist for this setup (think time first)

Merged from the three surveys' rankings (latency section L, grounding section E, planning section 7), re-ranked by expected think-time saving per clean task (36.4s wall, 34.2s think, think-before-act 14.9s). Savings are estimates from the cited measurements, not promises; each needs a held-out run. NOT ADDITIVE: several items attack the same think calls; prototype and measure each one alone first.

1. Reasoning-effort dial per step type (1.1, 1.4, 1.5). Try low or medium for windows, observe, and verify; keep medium or high only for act composition. Basis: max took 30.6s to first text vs 3.0s at low on one streamed test, and 2,517 vs 56 reasoning tokens; accuracy peaks near 1,024 thinking tokens and then reverses. Expected: 10s to 20s per task. Cost to test: one config change, no code. Open questions: per-call vs session-level override, structured-output support.
2. Code execution as the action (8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.5). One program per call, loops, conditions, asserts; code first, GUI only when visual. Basis: OpenGCUA code-alone ablation minus 14.8 percent steps (conservative bound; the 53 percent full-config number mixes batching and code), CoAct-1 10.15 vs 15 steps, CLI 10.7 vs 18.6 steps, CodeAct up to plus 20 percent success. Expected: 10s to 17s per task on code-able subtasks. Guardrail: allowed action set, checked preconditions, no irreversible action without a checkpoint, code runs through the broker, never raw input. Measure think time per task, not just steps.
3. Compiled routine library, executed deterministically (6.1, 6.3, 6.4). A retrieved routine runs with 0 think calls. Basis: local precedent 823s to about 100s (8.23x); Voyager zero-shot transfer 100 percent vs 0 percent baselines; SkillWeaver plus 31.8 percent WebArena. Expected: on a repeat task shape, think falls from 34.2s to near 0s for the routine part; on a new shape, first run pays one supervisor extraction pass, later runs pay 0. Build as code routines, honed by tests, one strategy line plus one failure line per routine, retrieved by exact task shape, k=1 only, quarantined after 2 consecutive checkpoint failures.
4. Observation masking plus text history, cap image history at last 1 to 3 frames (3.1, 3.4, 2.12). Keep only the latest 1 to 2 screenshots in full, mask older observations to one text line, keep reasoning and actions. Basis: masking halves cost and matches summarization on solve rate; observations are about 84 percent of turn tokens; long context raises per-token cost 3.6x to 7x; later steps take up to 3x longer as history grows; screenshot-only history does not help, only text history does. Expected: 5s to 15s per task, mostly in think-before-act and think-before-observe. Cost to test: prompt and history handling change, no new model.
5. Rule router by step type (5.2, 5.4). Cheap tier for windows, observe, and verify (together 19.3s of think); strong model only for act composition. Basis: RouteLLM proved one-call pre-generation routing beats cascades on latency; 65 percent to 80 percent of queries do not need frontier reasoning. Expected: 8s to 13s per task if the cheap tier answers each easy step in about 2s. Open question: what the cheap tier is (smaller Codex model if the CLI offers one, else a local 7B, see 13.4).
6. Speculative multi-action with local validation (7.1, 10). One think call predicts 2 to 4 actions; each is validated against live UI state before execution; stop at first failure. Basis: UFO2 51 percent fewer LLM calls, steps down up to 50 percent on Windows with UIA validation. Expected: 20 to 30 percent fewer think calls on predictable sequences on Wayland, lower than 51 percent because AT-SPI plus OCR validation is weaker than Windows UIA. Never speculative writes without validation; never two writers on one seat.
7. Plan once per subgoal, execute without re-thinking (7.7, 7.8, 7.6). One planner call per subgoal, deterministic workers, solver only at the end. Basis: ReWOO 5x fewer tokens, plus 4 percent HotpotQA; SteP 2.3x fewer tokens per trajectory. Token saving is not wall saving, but fewer think calls IS wall saving on this profile, where each call carries a 14.9s mean think-before-act gap. Replan only on checkpoint failure, never per step. Parallelise independent local reads in one batch; do not parallelise dependent GUI writes.
8. Milestone-only verification (9.3, 9.1, 9.2). Verify at subgoal boundaries with local checks (file bytes plus SHA, window state, OCR tokens); escalate to Luna only on stuck (same action twice, no state change) or milestone failure. Basis: planning plus reflection 75 to 94 percent of latency (3.4); reflection is 25 to 30 percent of reasoning tokens (9.2). Expected: removing one verification think call per task saves one think gap, roughly 5 to 15s; the exact number is UNMEASURED until the prototype runs. Do NOT verify per step, do NOT reflect per step, do NOT run an agentic judge in the loop.
9. Local small grounder, Ferret-UI Lite 3B first, UI-TARS-1.5-2B second (4.4, 4.9, 3.13). A small local model returns coordinates so the big model never has to read the screenshot for grounding. Basis: Ferret-UI Lite-3B Pro 53.3, v2 91.6, OSWorld-G 61.2/55.3; UI-TARS-1.5-7B Pro 49.6 for reference; local forward pass sub-second vs Luna think 5 to 18s per grounding step; OmniParser v2 0.6s to 0.8s per frame on A100/4090. Expected: 3s to 10s per task, more on recovery loops. Cost to test: high (VRAM fit on 6GB, local serving). Gate: load each candidate on the laptop and report actual VRAM and per-call latency first; verify-first designs that add a Luna confirmation call can erase the win, prefer local guess plus automatic execution with post-action verification.
10. Predict-then-verify-crop, gated on small or uncertain targets; stamp failed points on retry (4.13, 3.7). Basis: RegionFocus plus 12 to 14 points Pro; ScreenSeeker 18.9 to 48.1; ZoomClick 61.4 to 73.1; local crop bytes 10 to 32x fewer, local time 1.4x. Expected: medium, fewer recovery loops (each about 90s in P1), not cheaper calls. Gate on predicted element size and on first-guess uncertainty; never crop out discriminating context; stamp the failed point on the next observation (RegionFocus image-as-map 43.2 vs 37.2 for text history).
11. Read-mode observation (OCR or a11y text) for verification steps; scan-mode grayscale low-res for change detection (3.9, 3.10). Basis: FSP projection 10 to 13x fewer observation tokens (projection, not measured, see section 16); DirectShell 50 to 200 tokens per text perception; VisualWebArena text-only 7.3 vs screenshot 15.1 (so read mode is for verification, not discovery). Expected: medium for verification-heavy tasks; near-zero for grounding-heavy tasks. Wrong mode choice costs a second observation; default read only where the expected state is textual.
12. Prompt-caching check (2.9). Read one Codex usage record for cached-token fields; fix prefix order so the stable part comes first. Basis: up to 80 percent latency cut on long prompts, free if already active. Expected: 0s if already hitting, small if the prefix is unstable today. Cost to test: near zero, do it first.
13. Structured output for the action envelope only (11.3). Turn on provider structured outputs if the Codex CLI exposes them. Basis: about 100ms per call, failures under 0.1 percent vs 5 percent to 10 percent prompt-only. Expected: under 1s per task on average, but it kills the occasional full retry cycle (14.9s). Cost to test: near zero if exposed, do not build a proxy if not.
14. SoM numbered overlay from a local detector, MIT YOLO only (3.12, 3.13). Basis: SoM lifts VisualWebArena 15.1 to 16.4; OmniParser lifts GPT-4V desktop grounding about 41 to 75 percent; v2 latency 0.6s A100, 0.8s 4090, 0.5 to 0.8s on RTX 3080. Expected: medium, IF Luna reasons faster over discrete marks than over coordinates; unmeasured for Luna. One prototype arm, gated on a misclick and think-before-act A/B.
15. AT-SPI extents for native dialogs, plus DOM-first browser fast path (3.11, 3.10). Basis: WAA 12.5 to 19.5 percent with UIA tree; local a11y.py currently has no coordinate path at all. Expected: small to medium, only where the tree is complete (native GTK dialogs, Nautilus); zero where it is not (popups, VS Code editor). Needs new code (get_extents) plus a per-app coverage matrix before trusting it.
16. Diff-based where-to-look, hash tiles, fresh crop on changed regions only (3.8). Basis: GUIPruner 3.4x fewer FLOPs, 3.3x faster vision encoding, over 94 percent performance retained (Qwen2-VL-2B); InnvoLabs 85 percent token saving (vendor, unverified). Expected: small to medium on vision-encoding time; zero on Luna think unless the token saving shortens Luna's read. Staleness hazard if misused as pixel reuse; keep the fresh-observation contract. Only after the gap split is measured.
17. Prompt scoping per call (6.5, 6.6, 3.4). One routine skill file loaded at a time, small context per supervisor call, history pruned to the compact state block (goal, completed steps, open facts with values), never the full trace. Basis: SteP context 22.7K vs 52K tokens; OSWorld-Human later steps up to 3x slower as history grows. The think-time effect of scoping alone is UNMEASURED; it is a multiplier on items 1 to 8, not a standalone prototype.

Do NOT prototype for think time: standalone prompt compression (2.5), speculative decoding (13.3), AsyncLM (12.2), latent compression training (2.7), LLM summarization of history (3.1), Tree of Thoughts (7.12), online MCTS (7.11), K-way sampling with a judge (7.10), parallel rollouts (9.5), per-step judge or reflection (9.1, 9.4), semantic cache as correctness (6.7), speculative decoding through the Codex API (7.3), fine-tuning a planner (7.8). Each has a measured cost in section 15 that points the wrong way.

## Caveats and confidence

- Confidence is highest on: the OSWorld-Human latency split (primary paper, reproduced by this program's own profile), the zoom/crop accuracy gains (five independent methods, same direction), the ShowUI token-merging ablation (direct measurement), the VRAM rejects (vendor-stated requirements), the reasoning-effort dial numbers (measured per effort level), and the benchmark saturation facts (leaderboard dates given).
- Confidence is medium on: exact per-model ScreenSpot numbers, because different tables report different splits and versions of the same model (e.g. Ferret-UI Lite OSWorld-G 61.2 vs 55.3 inside one paper; UGround-v1 Pro 31.1 vs 32.5 across tables). Where two numbers conflicted, both are shown and the entry says so.
- Confidence is low on: vendor and self-reported claims (DirectShell, MAI-UI, Mobilerun, InnvoLabs, BiGrounder end-to-end numbers), FSP's projected 10 to 13x token reduction (a projection, not a measurement), and any latency figure quoted on A100/4090 hardware (the RTX 4050 is far slower; local measurement is mandatory before a prototype assumes sub-second).
- No local model was run in any survey (read-only tasks). Every "fits in 6GB VRAM" claim for Ferret-UI Lite-3B and UI-TARS-1.5-2B is a size-based judgment (3B and 2B weights plus vision encoder fit in 6GB at 4-bit or 8-bit with room for KV cache), not a measured load test. The prototype worker must load each candidate on the laptop and report actual VRAM and per-call latency before it is used in the loop.
- Two numbers in the planning survey rest on secondary summaries and are labelled as such: the Stechly equal-budget finding and the VeriCUA cost numbers. Everything else with a number traces to an arXiv abstract, paper text, or repo measurement.
- Cross-paper comparisons (for example, UFO2's 51 percent on Windows vs OpenGCUA's 53 percent on OSWorld-v2) compare different benchmarks, models, and setups, and must not be read as a ranking of the methods against each other. The ranking in section 17 is a judgment for THIS setup, not a league table of the papers.
- No em dash or en dash used in this file (hyphens only), per the standing rule.
