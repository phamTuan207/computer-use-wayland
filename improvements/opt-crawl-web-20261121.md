## (a) Session and guardian startup failures in agent harnesses

### S1. GoAkt supervision docs
- URL: https://docs.goakt.dev/actor/supervision
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: actor supervision with explicit strategies (OneForOne: restart only the failing child; OneForAll: restart all siblings), directives (Resume, Restart, Stop, Escalate), a restart budget (max consecutive restarts inside a time window, then suspend instead of restart), and exponential backoff (nth restart waits min(initialDelay shifted by n-1, maxDelay)). Example given: restarts at 200ms, 400ms, 800ms, 1.6s, 3.2s, then the 6th consecutive fault suspends the child; the counter resets after 30s fault-free.
- Evidence shown: design doc with worked backoff sequence, no measured failure-rate numbers. The numbers are the configured parameters, not benchmark results.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The pattern maps directly onto our guardian: OneForOne restart of the driver only (not the whole runner), a restart budget (our kill rules need exactly this: a number, not "retry forever"), exponential backoff from 200ms. But GoAkt is a Go actor framework, not a Python browser driver, so the code does not transfer, only the policy shape. Missing piece for us: GoAkt assumes the supervisor itself never dies; our guardian needs the Azure-style split (see S2) where a second watcher watches the watcher.

### S2. Azure Scheduler Agent Supervisor pattern
- URL: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/scheduler-agent-supervisor
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: three logical actors. The Scheduler records every step state (not started, running, completed) plus a complete-by deadline in a durable state store. The Agent wraps one remote call with its own retry logic inside the complete-by time and passes a stable id across retries for dedup. The Supervisor runs on a schedule, scans the store for steps stuck in running past complete-by, and arranges retry or a compensating transaction. Steps must be idempotent because a retry can run twice. Explicit warning: retry limits at every layer multiply, so one durable end-to-end retry budget must own the total, and retries elsewhere must be disabled or capped inside it.
- Evidence shown: pattern doc, no measured numbers. The durable-store plus complete-by design is the evidence: a step that times out is indistinguishable from a step that failed, and only the store scan finds it.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the closest published shape to our guardian problem. Our guardian should be the Supervisor (periodic scan of a durable step log with complete-by times), not a process that only reacts to crashes. Direct consequences for us: (1) every driver step writes state plus a deadline to a file before it runs, so a dead driver is visible as an expired running step; (2) one retry budget owned by the guardian, driver-level retries disabled or counted inside it, because today our retries can stack; (3) the Supervisor must not restart processes itself (Azure: that belongs to hosting), which means our guardian needs a separate watchdog that restarts the guardian, matching the repo's existing watchdog cron.

### S3. DCC-MCP-Core gateway guardian
- URL: https://dcc-mcp.github.io/dcc-mcp-core/guide/gateway
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: a three-layer daemon setup on one workstation. On startup, a single-flight lock file (gateway-launch.lock) guarantees N concurrent launches spawn at most one gateway process. A guardian watchdog in each backend probes /health every 5s (probe timeout 0.5s); after 2 consecutive misses it re-runs the daemon ensure path under the same lock and restarts the daemon. A stale lock is reclaimable after 30s. A terminal host-died envelope triggers immediate deregistration, no waiting for heartbeat.
- Evidence shown: concrete parameters (5s probe, 0.5s timeout, 2 misses, 30s stale lock, 300s idle timeout for auto-ensure), no measured uptime numbers.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the most directly copyable guardian design of the three: probe interval, miss count, single-flight lock, stale-lock reclaim. Our Marionette/BiDi guardian can copy the numbers as a starting point (probe every 5s, restart after 2 missed health checks, lock file so two runners never spawn two drivers). Note the lock solves a failure mode we have: two live runners racing to start one driver. The 0.5s probe timeout is a warning too: our Marionette socket can stall longer than 0.5s under load, so a too-tight timeout would restart a healthy driver.

## (b) Slow first action and think-observe loop costs

### S4. OSWorld-Human: Benchmarking the Efficiency of Computer-Use Agents (Abhyankar et al.)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16042v1
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: first systematic latency study of computer-use agents on OSWorld (369 tasks). Broke down Agent S2 and GTA1 trajectories on 37 tasks into retrieval, planning, grounding, action, screenshot, reflection. Built OSWorld-Human, human gold trajectories for all 369 tasks, plus a Weighted Efficiency Score.
- Evidence shown: planning and reflection LLM calls are 75% to 94% of total latency across tasks; judging/reflection alone is 22.5% (GTA1) to 33.6% (S2); each successive step can take 3x longer than early steps as prompt history grows; best agents take 1.4-2.7x more steps than necessary (16 agents evaluated); best agent 42.5% success but only 17.4% on the strictest efficiency metric. Concrete example: changing line spacing of two paragraphs takes an agent 12 minutes, a human under 30 seconds.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the core diagnosis for our think-observe loop. Two fixes follow from their numbers, and both match our standing direction: (1) cut LLM calls per step (group actions into one call, drop per-step reflection/judging, which is up to a third of time); (2) cut steps per task (their 1.4-2.7x over human is our batching lever). Their "later steps 3x slower" also justifies our observation expiry: stale context is not just stale, it is expensive. Caveat: their numbers are GPT-4.1 planner plus UI-TARS grounding on Ubuntu VMs, not our Luna/Codex loop, so the percentages transfer as direction, not as our exact budget.

### S5. Temporal UI State Inconsistency in Desktop GUI Agents (Xu, UCSD, April 2026)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18860
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: measured the observation-to-action gap on real OSWorld workloads (VMware Ubuntu 22.04, Claude Opus 4.6), then built PUSV, Pre-execution UI State Verification: re-verify UI state immediately before dispatch with masked pixel SSIM at the click target, global screenshot diff, and X window snapshot diff.
- Evidence shown: mean gap 6.51s, std 3.59s, min 3.18s, max 13.23s, n=10 tasks. Attack primitive B (window focus manipulation) reaches 100% action-redirection success with zero visual evidence at observation time. PUSV reaches 100% Action Interception Rate across 180 adversarial trials (135 primitive A plus 45 primitive B), zero false positives, under 0.1s overhead. Against DOM injection (primitive C) PUSV is near 0% AIR, a structural blind spot of any visual-only check.
- Verdict: APPLIES, with a split. The 6.51s mean gap is the published number closest to our 120s observation expiry problem: it proves a screenshot ages in seconds, not minutes, so our 120s expiry is far too generous and our first action must be re-grounded, not trusted. PUSV itself is directly usable as a cheap pre-dispatch check: a pixel diff at the target plus a DOM re-read costs under 0.1s and catches focus changes and overlay hijacks. But the 0% AIR on DOM injection is the warning: visual re-verification alone cannot catch a DOM that changed under a correct-looking screenshot, so our pre-dispatch check must include a DOM re-read (element still present, rect unchanged), not only pixels. That matches our stack: DOM rects are exact, vision is 58.6%.

### S6. Chronotope: One Step, Four Clocks
- URL: https://zhanwenchen-chronotope-report.static.hf.space/index.html
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: ran the same seeded GUI tasks under Paused (response stays valid), Replay (same frames, response stays valid), and Live (response can expire). Recorded four event times per decision: E (environment change), O (observation available), D (decision irrevocable), A (action received by app). Proposed late binding: leave one action argument (click coordinate or typed value) unfinished during deliberation and fill it from the newest frame immediately before D.
- Evidence shown: a 27B VLM scores 100/100 Paused and Replay but 0/100 Live at every window shorter than its roughly 5s deliberation. On a drifting target, filling the coordinate from the newest frame raises success from 0/100 to 100/100 with no extra model call; re-running the model also succeeds after a one-time jump but fails under continuous drift. A drifting balloon at 16 px/s: the frozen click lands 84 px away, the late-bound click 4 px away. Audit of 18 benchmarks found none that record observation, commitment, and receipt times while varying only whether the response can expire.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the single most actionable paper for our first-action problem. Late binding is exactly our fix shape: decide WHAT to click from the old observation, but fill WHERE from a fresh DOM rect read milliseconds before dispatch. It costs no extra model call and their experiment shows it fixes continuous drift, which re-decision does not. It also gives us the measurement discipline we lack: record E/O/D/A timestamps per step and score at receipt (A), never at commitment (D). Our log should adopt their four clocks. Caveat: their tasks are synthetic (balloon, notification, trading), not real desktop apps, so the 0/100 to 100/100 swing is a clean-room number; on our real Zen pages the gain will be smaller but the mechanism is the same.

### S7. Laya computer-use (AX tree plus decision model, macOS)
- URL: https://github.com/ChenneyZhuang/laya-computer-use
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: replaced screenshot plus vision LLM with macOS Accessibility tree plus a small local decision model. The model picks an element index from a numbered table; code resolves the index to the real element. No coordinates, no screenshots, no cloud.
- Evidence shown: AX walk median about 110ms (22 actionable of 169 raw elements), one decision about 110ms at up to 20 options, about 230ms at 40 options, total about 220ms per step, $0. Honest negative result: the official Laya checkpoints score 0/12 on control selection (they answer DONE/WAIT without reading the options), while a vision model (OmniJev-4B) scores 9/12 but at about 142s per decision, about 600x slower.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The latency numbers (about 220ms per step vs our multi-second think loop) prove the ceiling of a structured-tree route, and the design (decide on element identity, resolve coordinates at dispatch) is the same late-binding idea as S6. But it is macOS-only (AX API plus cua-driver), and the headline decision model scores 0/12, so there is no usable model to copy. For us the transferable part is the architecture, not the code: our DOM is our AX tree, and our el.click() path already resolves identity at dispatch. The 600x vision-vs-tree latency gap is also a useful number to quote when defending the DOM-first design against vision-first proposals.

## (c) Overlay and pill flicker or visibility failures on Wayland and Hyprland

### S8. Hyprland issue 15738: layer-shell overlay not rendered on first invocation cycles
- URL: https://github.com/hyprwm/hyprland/issues/15738
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: reported, not fixed. A gtk-layer-shell overlay (Handy speech-to-text app, Tauri/GTK) is not rendered by Hyprland during the first several invocation cycles, then appears after about 5 triggers and works persistently until restart. The surface exists and receives events (mic-level events in logs), it is simply not painted. Same class of bug existed on KDE Plasma and was fixed in the app repo, not the compositor.
- Evidence shown: reproduction steps, Hyprland 0.56.1, gtk-layer-shell 0.10.1, about 5 cycles to first paint, reset on restart. No fix, no workaround except HANDY_NO_GTK_LAYER_SHELL=1, which falls back to a regular always-on-top window and loses layer-shell anchoring (overlay centers instead of bottom edge).
- Verdict: NO PUBLISHED FIX. This is the closest public report to our pill visibility failures, and it is an open issue with no compositor fix. What it gives us is a diagnosis pattern and a fallback shape: (1) "surface exists, events flow, compositor does not paint" is a known Hyprland layer-shell failure mode, so our pill going invisible is plausibly compositor-side, not a bug in our overlay code; (2) the working fallback is a regular always-on-top window instead of a layer-shell surface, at the cost of anchoring, which is a fallback we can implement if the pill stays invisible; (3) the "works after about 5 triggers" detail suggests a warm-up or first-commit race, so a fix attempt is to force an early commit or a dummy show/hide cycle at startup. No published numbers on flicker rate or a verified fix exist for this class in the sources found.

## (d) Firefox Marionette browserElement is null and session creation failures

### S9. Bugzilla 1682242: tabBrowser is null in WebDriver:NewSession
- URL: https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1682242
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: Mozilla fixed NewSession failing when a non-browser window (no tab browser, e.g. Thunderbird or a library/downloads window) is open. Root cause: the initial browser collection assumed every chrome window has a tab browser. Fix: a null check, correctly registering chrome windows without a tab browser.
- Evidence shown: fixed in Firefox 86, verified in Nightly, covered by automated tests, low risk. The failure signature matches ours structurally: Marionette throws at GeckoDriver newSession when its window enumeration hits a window it cannot handle.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The upstream fix is already in every modern Firefox, so it does not fix our current Zen failures directly. But it names the mechanism behind our "browserElement is null" on session creation: NewSession enumerates windows and chokes on one that is not a normal browser window. Our mitigation follows: before creating a session, ensure the selected tab is a live browser tab (never a dead or closed tab, never a non-browser window), and if NewSession fails, close or ignore non-browser windows and retry with a fresh live tab focused. This matches our local lesson (open a sacrificial live tab before closing tabs, end the session focused on a live tab).

### S10. Bugzilla 1682062: browsingContext is null for GetWindowHandles with unloaded tabs
- URL: https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1682062
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: reported GetWindowHandles returning 500 with "browser.browsingContext is null" when unloaded tabs are present, on Wayland (sway), about 10 windows and 140 tabs, geckodriver --connect-existing --marionette-port 2828. Fixed in Firefox 90. Key observation from the reporter: closing the tab selected when geckodriver started leaves the driver unable to find any window until restart; it seems to see only one tab.
- Evidence shown: full geckodriver trace logs, reproduction across Firefox 83, 84, 85, 86 beta, fixed in 90. The "only sees one tab, and losing the selected tab blinds it until restart" observation is the useful part.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the closest upstream analogue to our exact failure: connect-existing on port 2828, many tabs, closing the selected tab blinds the driver until restart. Our local rule (never delete the session while focused on a dead tab, always open a live tab first) is the correct mitigation and is now backed by an upstream report. The fix being in Firefox 90 means Zen, as a Firefox fork, should carry it, so if we still see it, the cause is more likely Zen-specific tab handling or our driver deleting a session on a dead tab, not this bug.

### S11. Bugzilla 2081625: connect-existing hangs 60s when another session is active
- URL: https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2081625
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: Mozilla fixed the firefox-devtools-mcp connect path. Marionette allows only one session; a second client gets its socket closed with no error, and geckodriver retries silently for about 60s. Fix: the checkPort probe now waits for the Marionette hello greeting; if the socket closes before the greeting arrives, it fails at once with "Another WebDriver session is already active".
- Evidence shown: measured 60.1s on main, 4-6ms with the patch. Same PR added a 45s timeout (with geckodriver killed) when Firefox never answers the session request, e.g. an unanswered "Allow remote control?" prompt, and an idle-timeout option for the fixed 30-minute idle hold.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This explains a whole class of our "session creation hangs with no error" reports: a stale session is still attached, and our new connect attempt waits 60s in silence. The fix is directly copyable: before creating a session, probe the Marionette socket and wait for the greeting; if the socket closes, report "another session is active" at once instead of retrying for 60s. Also copy the 45s hard timeout with kill: a session request that never answers must die loudly, never hang the runner. This also reinforces our one-live-runner rule with a mechanism: the second runner must fail fast with a clear message.

## (e) Hyprland workspace switching and app-launcher opening by command vs mouse click

### S12. Hyprland discussion 830: get open windows list
- URL: https://github.com/hyprwm/Hyprland/discussions/830?sort=new
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: community pattern for listing and switching windows without a mouse: read `hyprctl -j clients`, pick a window, then `hyprctl dispatch focuswindow address:<addr>`; close with `closewindow address:<addr>`; move with `hyprctl --batch "dispatch focuswindow address:<addr>; dispatch movetoworkspace <ws>"`. Launcher side: pipe the list through wofi/rofi dmenu and exec the dispatch for the chosen line. Maintainer answer: xlsclients is X11-only, use `hyprctl clients`, and `hyprctl dispatch focuswindow <window>`.
- Evidence shown: working scripts (perl and shell), no timing numbers. The pattern is the community standard, not a benchmark.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the command-side answer to our class (e): workspace and window switching by hyprctl is the supported path, and the address-based focus (not title or index) is the robust identifier because titles change and indices shift. For our driver, window switching should be `hyprctl -j clients` to resolve the address, then `focuswindow address:...`, never a mouse click on a workspace pill, and never a title match. The launcher side (rofi/wofi dmenu) is the human path; our agent path should skip the launcher UI entirely and dispatch directly.

### S13. Dotfiles history, July 2026: Hyprland 0.55 Lua dispatch breakage
- URL: https://dotfiles.v1cferr.dev/history/2026/07-july/
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: documented, with live debugging, that Hyprland 0.55 changed `dispatch` into a shortcut for `hl.dispatch(...)`, so the old string form `dispatch workspace 3` builds `hl.dispatch(workspace 3)` and dies in the Lua parser. The click died silently because Quickshell execDetached hides stderr. Correct form: `hl.dsp.focus({ workspace = N })`. Also notes `hl.dsp.workspace(N)` fails ("attempt to call a table value") because it is a table of sub-dispatchers, not a function. Separately: `hyprctl -i 0` finds the Hyprland instance from any shell, fixing SSH reloads that silently never applied because HYPRLAND_INSTANCE_SIGNATURE was missing.
- Evidence shown: dated log entries, exact error strings, the working form verified by the author. No timing numbers.
- Verdict: APPLIES. This independently confirms our local Hyprland 0.57.2 Lua lesson (hl.dsp object form, never the string form) and adds two things we should adopt: (1) any dispatch path must surface stderr, because a silent failure is the failure mode (our driver should capture hyprctl stderr on every call and log it); (2) `hyprctl -i 0` is the correct invocation from SSH or any shell without the instance signature in the environment, which matters for our VM-to-laptop SSH path. The workspace-switch-by-click path (clicking a bar pill) is exactly what broke here, which supports preferring hyprctl dispatch over mouse clicks for workspace moves.

## (f) Blind composite test design for GUI agents

### S14. The Agent Evaluation Crisis (Agentic Frontier)
- URL: https://www.agenticfrontier.dev/articles/agent-evaluation-crisis
- Accessed: 2026-11-21
- What they did: synthesized D'Oro et al. 2026 (NeurIPS, arXiv 2605.08261) and the Berkeley RDI BenchJack work into an audit framework. Core result: a 1MB blind replay script (no model, fixed recorded action sequence) beats frontier models on static benchmarks: 71.1% vs 70.6% on OSWorld, 41.5% vs 32.7% on MobileWorld. Rebuilt as DigiWorld (15 sandboxed apps, data, theme, and initial state varied independently across 3.2M verified configurations), the blind script falls to 6.90% while the real model scores 45.7%. Five failure modes: contamination, determinism exploits, single-seed reporting, success-criterion gaming, cost blindness. Cures: report IQM plus confidence intervals (worked example: a mean tie 50.0% vs 53.5% becomes an 8-point IQM gap), hierarchical bootstrap with Wilson intervals, pass^k (chance all k attempts succeed; retail pass^8 under 25% for leading agents), functional correctness (check the world reached the goal state, not the action sequence), and cost as a first-class metric.
- Evidence shown: replay vs frontier percentages, DigiWorld 6.90% vs 45.7%, GAIA humans 92% vs GPT-4 15%, WebArena GPT-4 14.41% vs humans 78.24%, pass^8 under 25%. Also cites BenchJack: an exploit agent scoring 100% on 8 benchmarks with zero tasks solved (OSWorld 73% via fetching the gold file and abusing eval in the grader).
- Verdict: APPLIES. This is the design authority for our class (f). Direct rules for our blind composite tests: (1) vary the initial state per run (data, theme, starting tab, window layout), or a replay of a recorded run will pass; run a blind replay control and require it to score near zero; (2) executor blind to the test means also blind to the gold answer: the grader must check end state (file exists, text present, setting changed), never the action sequence, and the gold must not be readable from the machine the agent can reach (BenchJack fetched OSWorld gold from a public URL); (3) report IQM plus intervals over multiple seeds, not a single pass rate, and report wall time and cost beside accuracy; (4) use pass^k for reliability, since a single lucky pass hides inconsistency. Our current practice (fresh agent, held-out cases, no baked coordinates) already follows the spirit; this paper supplies the controls that prove it (replay control, state variation, end-state grading).

## Synthesis: one optimal fix method, from these sources

The sources converge on one method, and it is not a single patch. It is a loop with four parts, each backed by a source above:

1. Supervise with a durable state store and a budget (S1, S2, S3, S11). Every step writes state plus a complete-by deadline to a file before it runs. A separate supervisor scans for expired running steps and restarts only the failed child (OneForOne), with exponential backoff from 200ms and a hard restart budget, then suspends and reports. A second watchdog restarts the supervisor itself. Marionette connect probes for the hello greeting and fails fast in milliseconds (S11: 60.1s to 4-6ms) instead of hanging. One retry budget owns the total; driver-level retries count inside it.
2. Decide on identity, bind coordinates late (S5, S6, S7). The model decides WHAT element to act on from the observation; the driver re-reads the DOM rect milliseconds before dispatch and fills the coordinate from the freshest frame (Chronotope late binding: 0/100 to 100/100, no extra model call). Before dispatch, re-verify: DOM element still present with unchanged rect plus a pixel diff at the target (PUSV: 100% interception, under 0.1s). Score at receipt, record the four clocks E/O/D/A per step. Cut LLM calls per step: drop per-step reflection and judging (OSWorld-Human: 75-94% of time, up to a third on reflection alone) and group actions into one call.
3. Prefer command over click for compositor operations (S12, S13). Workspace and window moves go through hyprctl with address resolution (`hyprctl -j clients`, `focuswindow address:...`), using the hl.dsp object form on Hyprland 0.57, with stderr captured on every call and `hyprctl -i 0` from SSH. Never click a workspace pill or launcher to switch. For the pill overlay itself there is no published fix (S8): implement the always-on-top fallback and a startup warm-up, and measure visibility live.
4. Test blind, with a replay control and end-state grading (S14). Every fix is proven on held-out cases by a fresh executor that never saw the test, with varied initial state per run. Run a blind replay control and require it near zero. Grade end state only, keep gold unreadable from the agent machine. Report IQM plus intervals over seeds, wall time, and cost beside accuracy. Generator never runs its own tests; council plus redteam on every fix; commit small and many; log every event with wall-clock time.
