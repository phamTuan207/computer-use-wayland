## Q1. Are the win criteria fair? Verdict: AMEND

### What is sound

- The total-speed bar is correct accounting, not a bias toward A. The design quotes: "SPEED (total): B wins ONLY if B total (deliberation + merge + B execution) < A total (A execution). Otherwise B loses on total speed, whatever B execution alone shows." Deliberation plus merge (about 10m 40s) is real cost paid before B execution starts. Any deployed deliberated system pays it. Requiring B execution to beat A by more than that is the honest test of the research question "does splitting increase SPEED".
- Reporting execution-only speed separately, and calling "execution faster, total slower" a partial result rather than a win, is the right guard against headline theft.
- "No single blended score" and answering "faster?" and "more accurate?" separately is correct. A blended score would hide which question was answered.
- The direction of the accuracy rule (A passes and B fails means B loses on accuracy regardless of speed) is correct.

### What must change

1. Deliberation cost is frozen on a shaky number, and the wait rule is unstated. Deliberation wall is the parallel max 8m 40s, but the three deliberators finished at 4m 32s, 7m 40s, and 8m 40s, and delib-3 alone produced a 58-step plan with zero UNVERIFIED selectors that became the merge skeleton. If a deployed system would take the first usable draft, or a 2-of-3 quorum, the max overstates the cost and biases the total-speed bar toward A. Merge time (about 2 min) is bounded by file mtimes (drafts fetched 18:03:48, plan written 18:05:50), not a stopwatch, so its error is unknown. Replacement text for the design: "Deliberation cost is the parallel max 8m 40s because the deployed rule is wait-for-all-3 deliberators. Merge cost is about 2 min, measured from draft-fetch mtime to plan-write mtime, with an uncertainty of plus or minus 1 min because no stopwatch was running. If the deployed rule ever becomes first-usable-draft or quorum, the deliberation cost must be re-frozen before the comparison, not re-argued after it." If Y8 cannot assert wait-for-all-3 is the deployed rule, the deliberation cost must be re-frozen as time-to-first-usable-draft (4m 32s) with the merge rule stated, before any executor runs.

2. Two wall clocks are recorded but neither is named primary. The design records executor-recorded start/end AND coordinator-observed launch-to-final-report wall, and says "Both stated", but the win comparison ("B total < A total") needs one number per arm. If the clocks disagree (codex spawn overhead, report-writing time, clock skew between executor and coordinator), the winner could be picked post hoc. Replacement: pre-register coordinator-observed launch-to-final-report as the PRIMARY clock for both the total-speed and execution-only comparisons. Executor-recorded start/end is secondary (diagnostic). Define the boundaries now: execution wall starts at executor process launch (first codex spawn) and ends when the coordinator receives the executor final report message. Pre-flight diagnose time and infra start time are excluded from execution wall and recorded separately per arm. B total is deliberation wall (first deliberator launch to last deliberator finish) plus merge wall (first draft fetched to plan written) plus B execution wall on the primary clock.

3. The accuracy formula conflates two things and omits the strongest signal. The design quotes: "ACCURACY: B wins ONLY if (B passes and A fails) OR (both pass AND B has strictly fewer errors + recovery steps than A)." Problems: (a) "errors + recovery steps" reads as a sum, but an error that triggers a recovery then counts twice (once as an error, once as a recovery step). (b) Misclicks and check failures are recorded in the metrics schema but excluded from the formula. A check failure (a read that differed after its one retry) is the strongest accuracy signal in the whole schema, and it is left out. (c) Plan deviations are excluded (see Q5 item 1). Replacement accuracy rule: "Define the accuracy burden per arm as: check failures + errors + misclicks, each counted once with step numbers. Recovery steps are reported separately as cost, not added to the burden. B wins on accuracy ONLY if (B passes and A fails) OR (both pass AND B has strictly fewer burden AND B has zero material deviations, AND the burden difference is at least 2). If A passes and B fails, B loses on accuracy, except when the B failure is traced to a single plan defect (see Q5 item 2). If both fail, see item 4 below." Note the "at least 2" comes from item 5.

4. Tie cases are undefined. The design says B wins ONLY if strictly fewer, which implies equal burden means B does not win, but it never says the word "draw" for that case. And "If both fail, accuracy is a draw at failure" ignores progress: A failing at step 3 and B failing at step 27 are not the same outcome. Replacement tie table, to be added verbatim: "Both pass, equal burden: accuracy draw, B does not win. Both pass, burden difference of 1: accuracy draw (within noise, see item 5), B does not win. Both fail: not a draw. Compare steps completed before the first unrecovered failure and the final verifier state, and state which arm got further. A flat draw label is forbidden for two failures at different depths."

5. No noise margin at n=1. Executor runs are nondeterministic (the design says so itself). A 1-count difference in errors plus recovery is within that noise and cannot decide a win. Replacement: a burden difference of 1 is a draw, not a win. B needs a difference of at least 2 to win on accuracy when both pass. This is a judgment call, but it must be frozen now, not chosen after the counts are known.

6. The structural asymmetry must be stated, or a B win will be over-read. A makes about 28 decisions; B makes about 0 (it transcribes a validated plan). Error counts are not normalized by decision points, so a lower B error count is the predicted effect of offloading decisions, not proof that deliberation is better in general. Worse, B is not just deliberation: it is deliberation plus merge plus coordinator validation (64 JSON commands parse, all 15 element-id references resolve, banner text exact). A gets no validation. A B win could be caused by validation alone. Replacement addition to the confounds section: "B is the deliberated-plan bundle (3 deliberators plus merge plus coordinator validation). A is unvalidated direct execution. A B win, if observed, is a win for the bundle on this task, not proof that deliberation alone caused it. Isolating deliberation would need a control arm (single-agent plan plus the same validation), which is out of scope for this run."

7. Wording: change "Otherwise B loses on total speed, whatever B execution alone shows" to "Otherwise B does not win on total speed, whatever B execution alone shows." "Loses" implies deliberation made things worse; "does not win" states the bar was not cleared. Small, but the headline will be quoted.

## Q2. Is the INFRA retry-once rule right? Verdict: AMEND

### What is sound

- Classifying a pre-flight hidden body as INFRA-BLOCKED, not FAIL, is correct. It is a browser fault, not an executor result.
- Retrying the whole arm once from scratch, logging the partial run, never silently discarding it, is correct.
- "Do not run one arm and compare it against nothing" is the right principle.

### What must change

1. "Report both arms as blocked" is wrong when A already ran cleanly. The design quotes: "If it recurs, report both arms as blocked with the infra evidence and stop; do not run one arm and compare it against nothing." If A completed cleanly and B is then blocked twice, A is measured data, not blocked. Discarding it or relabelling it wastes a valid run and misreports the record. Replacement: "If the FIRST arm is blocked twice, no arm has run: report the comparison blocked with the infra evidence and stop. If A completed cleanly and B is blocked twice, report A as measured, B as blocked, and the comparison as impossible (no verdict). Never relabel a completed clean arm as blocked, and never compare one measured arm against nothing."

2. 5 min is shorter than the incident it is meant to outwait. Y4b records the hidden-body windows at about 7 to 8 min (16:36 to 16:44, and 17:52 to about 17:59). A retry after 5 min can land inside the same window, and a pre-flight that passes at the tail of a window is weak evidence the window cleared. Replacement: "Retry the whole arm once after 10 min, measured from the timestamp of the INFRA-BLOCKED classification, not from arm start. The retry runs a fresh pre-flight from scratch (new session, new tab). The retry pre-flight must be VISIBLE with no intervention (see Q4) or the arm is blocked a second time." During the wait, infra stays up, no tab is driven, no executor is launched.

3. There is no rule for a hidden body hitting mid-run, and the pre-flight tab is not the executor tab. The design diagnoses a throwaway tab, closes it, and the executor opens its own fresh tab afterwards. Y4b shows the fault is per-load and intermittent (about 6 of 6 new tabs hid while a reused tab stayed visible seconds earlier in the same probe). A clean pre-flight does not prove the executor tab will stay visible. If the executor tab goes hidden mid-run, its failed types and clicks will be scored as executor errors, which can flip the accuracy verdict. Replacement, to be added as a new invalid-run rule: "MID-RUN HIDDEN BODY: if the executor tab goes hidden mid-run (the executor reports body hidden on its own tab, or two consecutive type/click steps fail and a diagnose on the executor tab shows hidden), the executor stops, the coordinator diagnoses the executor tab and classifies it, and the arm is INVALID (INFRA), not a FAIL. It is re-run once from scratch under the retry rule. The partial run is logged, never counted." And add to each executor brief (or to the coordinator procedure): the executor's first step after new_tab is a diagnose_body eval on its OWN tab, and it proceeds past step 1 only if that diagnose is VISIBLE. This closes the gap between the pre-flight tab and the tab that counts.

4. Retry budget interaction is undefined. INFRA retry, DRIVER DEAD retry, and crash retry are each "once". An arm could in principle consume three re-runs for three different causes. Replacement: "Retries are per cause, but the total re-runs per arm are capped at 2 regardless of cause. A third invalidating event ends the comparison as blocked (if it hits the first arm) or as impossible (if it hits B after A completed, per item 1)."

5. No max inter-arm gap. With retries and the Y4d wait, A and B could end up hours apart, and Zen state, machine load, and driver warmth will have drifted. Replacement: "Record the inter-arm gap (A end to B start). If the gap exceeds 60 min, re-verify the frozen inputs (spec, fixture, hand_drive.py, plan, both briefs by md5) before B starts and record the gap in the B report. A gap over 60 min does not invalidate the comparison by itself, but it must be disclosed in the verdict."

6. FIXTURE_FAILURE has no rule. Preflight.py exits 3 for it, but the design's invalid-run rules cover only INFRA and DRIVER DEAD. Replacement: "FIXTURE_FAILURE (pre-flight exit 3, or fixture HTTP error, or missing #contacts-count) is neither INFRA-BLOCKED nor a FAIL. It stops the run. The coordinator re-verifies the fixture file and the HTTP server, fixes or restores them, then re-runs pre-flight from scratch. It does not consume the INFRA retry."

7. State what happens to infra during the wait (see item 2): infra stays up, no driving, fresh pre-flight. Do not reuse the blocked pre-flight tab or session for the retry.

## Q3. Is n=1 acceptable? Verdict: AMEND

### What is sound

- The design's scoping is honest: "n=1 per arm: one run each, one task, one model. This is ONE observation, not proof of a general effect. Any verdict is scoped to 'this task, this run'. No claim about deliberation in general follows from n=1." That is the right stance for a first observation, and it is stated before the run, not after.
- Demanding proof from this run would be the wrong ask. n=2 would still be one task and one model, still not proof, and would double live-machine time and tokens for almost no extra generality.

### What must change

1. Task-selection bias is not stated. X's report says test-05 was chosen because it has "more distinct decision points than test-09 (25 steps, mostly linear timer flow), so more room for deliberation to matter." That selection favors B. A B win on this task does not imply a win on a linear task. Replacement addition to the confounds section: "Task selection favors B: test-05 was chosen for its varied decision surface (modal add, edit, star, delete, search, group filter, save) precisely because deliberation has more room to matter there. A B win here is a win on a deliberation-friendly task. Generality would need a second task (for example test-09, linear timer flow) and is out of scope for this run."

2. Do NOT adopt A1/A2/B1/B2 blocks as the question frames them. Two As then two Bs is still fully confounded with order (all As first, all Bs second). Repeating in blocks buys a second observation but does not separate treatment from order, warmth, or drift. Replacement: pre-register a replication trigger instead of a second pair up front. Add verbatim: "Replication trigger: if the first pair yields a close result (total times within 2 min of each other on the primary clock, OR accuracy burden difference of 1 or less, OR either arm fails), run a second pair in swapped order (B first, then A) before claiming any win. If the first pair is decisive (B total beats A total despite the 10m 40s handicap, or either arm fails catastrophically with the cause identified as plan defect or spec misread), report the single observation as scoped and stop. The trigger thresholds are frozen here and must not be re-chosen after the first pair is seen."

3. Deliberation itself is n=1 and that must be stated. B total embeds a single deliberation measurement (8m 40s) and a single merge (about 2 min). The deliberator times already ranged from 4m 32s to 8m 40s, so a re-run deliberation could differ by minutes. Replacement: "B total uses the single measured deliberation and merge from Worker X. Deliberation is not re-run for this comparison. A different deliberation run could change B total by minutes; that variance is unmeasured and is a stated limit."

4. Verdict language must be fenced. Replacement: "No headline may say 'deliberation wins' or 'deliberation loses' outside the scoped sentence: 'On test-05, this run, [B total vs A total on the primary clock; accuracy burden A vs B; PASS/FAIL per arm].' Any wider claim is forbidden from this run."

## Q4. Does the pre-flight recovery ladder contaminate the comparison? Verdict: BLOCK

Yes, as written, and the flaw is a wrong inference, not a missing detail. No executor runs until this is fixed in the design text AND in preflight.py.

### The flaw

The design quotes: "Recovery ladder, 8 s total budget: poll 250 ms up to 2.0 s for the extension's own removal; if V1 persists, remove tz-hidden exactly as removeLoadingScreen does (single eval, test tab only, test origin only, logged as intervention); then re-diagnose. If still hidden, declare INFRA-BLOCKED per the rule above." And: "A pass achieved only after the tz-hidden removal is a FLAGGED pass, counted separately in the summary, never folded silently into the headline pass count."

That ladder is applied to a throwaway pre-flight tab. The design also quotes: "The pre-flight tab is closed after diagnose; each executor opens its own fresh tab afterwards." Preflight.py confirms the sequence: it opens its own session and tab, diagnoses, removes the class on that tab if V1 persists, re-diagnoses, then closes the tab and deletes the session (exit 0 for VISIBLE or FLAGGED). The executor then opens a different tab in a different session.

Removing tz-hidden on tab X proves nothing about tab Y. Y4b establishes that the fault is per-document and per-load: the extension injects at document_start on every navigation, the removal is per-document, and about 6 of 6 new tabs hid while a reused tab stayed visible seconds earlier. A removal-assisted "FLAGGED pass" on the pre-flight tab therefore proves the browser was in an unhealthy window at that moment, and it gives false confidence to start the arm on a new tab in that same window. If the executor tab then goes hidden mid-run, its failed steps will be scored as executor errors, which can flip the accuracy verdict. The FLAGGED label does not cure this: it labels a start that should not have happened.

This is also a transplant error. Y4b's ladder was per-test-tab: removal on the tab the test itself uses, with that test's pass flagged. Y8 lifted the ladder onto a pre-flight tab that the executor never uses, where the removal cannot help the executor at all. The design text even keeps Y4b's "FLAGGED pass" language, but in E1 there is no test pass at pre-flight time: pre-flight is a gate, not a test. A flagged gate is not a pass.

### The replacement (exact, to be applied verbatim in substance)

1. Removal becomes diagnostic only. Replace the ladder with: "Poll 250 ms up to 2.0 s for the extension's own removal. If V1 persists, run the single removal eval once as a DIAGNOSTIC (test tab only, test origin only, logged), then re-diagnose once. If the tab becomes visible after removal, record 'V1 CONFIRMED (manual removal unhid this tab)' and declare INFRA-BLOCKED. Do NOT start the arm. Wait 10 min (see Q2 item 2) and run a fresh pre-flight from scratch."

2. Redefine FLAGGED narrowly. "FLAGGED (proceed) applies ONLY when the tab cleared by the extension's own removal during the poll, with no manual intervention: initial diagnose hidden, then VISIBLE within the poll window. That arm may start, and the arm result carries the flag 'pre-flight cleared spontaneously during poll'. A manual-removal-assisted visibility NEVER authorizes a start."

3. Preflight.py must change to match. Currently it returns exit 0 for both VISIBLE and FLAGGED, so a removal-assisted pass authorizes the arm. Change it so: spontaneous clear during poll returns verdict FLAGGED, exit 0; manual-removal-then-visible returns verdict INFRA-BLOCKED with reason V1-CONFIRMED, exit 2. The design text and preflight.py must be updated together, committed, and the diff read by the coordinator before any executor runs. The first real pre-flight run is the live confirmation: it must return VISIBLE with no intervention, or the arm does not start.

4. Close the pre-flight-tab versus executor-tab gap (also Q2 item 3): the executor's first step after new_tab is a diagnose_body eval on its OWN tab, and it proceeds only if that diagnose is VISIBLE. If the executor tab is hidden at that point, the arm is INVALID (INFRA) under the mid-run rule, not a FAIL.

5. Enforce the time budget in code. The design says "8 s total budget" but preflight.py has no deadline check: its poll loop runs 8 iterations of 0.25 s sleep plus eval latency, and step.sh can take up to 30 s per call, so the poll phase can exceed 2.0 s and the total can exceed 8 s. Add a wall-clock deadline (start plus 8 s) and break out of the poll when it is exceeded.

6. Fix classify() in preflight.py: it calls a tab VISIBLE when body visibility is visible and opacity is not 0, but it never checks body display. A body with display none would be called VISIBLE while nothing renders. Require display not equal to "none" for VISIBLE. This is small, but it belongs in the same pre-flight correction.

### What is NOT the problem

The removal itself is safe as an operation (test tab only, test origin only, logged, and the code also removes the --zen-logo-path inline property, which matches removeLoadingScreen). The problem is what the removal is allowed to authorize, not the eval itself. And the diagnose payload in the code is thorough (full htmlClass string, htmlClassList array, bodyClass, htmlInline, bodyInline, transition, rect, innerLen, hasFocus, visState, countText). The code is better than the design text, which undersells it; the design text must be corrected to match the code (see the pre-flight section below).

## Q5. What else could produce a false win for B (or for A)? Verdict: AMEND

The design's "Known confounds" section lists order effect, n=1, executor nondeterminism, and sunk deliberation cost. That is honest but incomplete. Each item below is a concrete false-win path the design does not catch, with the guard to add. Items 1, 2, 6, and 7 are must-fix before the run; the rest are must-fix or must-record before the run (see the checklist).

1. False B win by improvisation (deviation gating). The design logs B deviations but does not gate the win on them. If B passes only by deviating (the executor re-decides a step the plan got wrong, uses a different selector, or reorders steps), the win belongs to the executor, not to the plan. Replacement addition to the accuracy rule: "B wins on accuracy ONLY if it passes with zero material deviations. A material deviation is any step where the executor used a different selector, order, or value than the plan, except for retries and fallbacks the plan itself allows. Re-finding an element after a re-render, when the plan instructs it, is not a deviation. If B passes with a material deviation, the accuracy verdict is 'B passed by improvisation, not by the plan; no deliberation win', and the deviation is quoted with its plan step number."

2. False A win by plan defect. The design says "If A passes and B fails, B loses on accuracy regardless of speed." That is too blunt. If B fails solely because of a plan defect (a step impossible as written after the allowed retry), that is a plan failure, not evidence that direct execution is better. A single merge typo would then be scored as deliberation losing. Replacement: "If B fails, the coordinator reads the B report against the plan file, identifies the first failing plan step, and classifies it with a quoted reason: PLAN DEFECT (step impossible as written after the allowed retry) or EXECUTOR ERROR (executor misread, skipped, or mistyped a plan step). If the failure traces to a single plan defect, the accuracy verdict is 'plan defect, not a deliberation loss; comparison inconclusive on accuracy', and the defect is recorded with its plan step number. If it traces to executor error, B loses on accuracy."

3. Bundle confound (deliberation plus validation). B is deliberation plus merge plus coordinator validation (64 JSON commands parse, all 15 element-id references resolve, banner text exact). A gets no validation. A B win could be caused by validation alone. Guard: state the scope limit from Q1 item 6 in the verdict language. Do not write "deliberation won"; write "the deliberated-plan bundle won on this task". A follow-up control (single-agent plan plus the same validation) would be needed to isolate deliberation, and it is out of scope for this run.

4. Order and warm state favoring B. The design notes Zen and driver warmth could favor B but has no mitigation or measurement. Guard: before A, run a no-op driver round-trip (new_session, new_tab to the fixture, one eval, close_tab, delete_session) to warm the driver, and record it as warm-up, not as an arm. Record per arm: Zen uptime, time since the last Marionette session, and whether infra was restarted before that arm. If infra is restarted for one arm only (for example after DRIVER DEAD), restart it for the other arm too before it runs, or record the asymmetry as a confound in the verdict.

5. Brief asymmetry (verified from the briefs). The A brief contains task-specific click advice: "A click on a table row fails; click the cell inside instead. The same interactability rule applies to small icons: prefer click_js for the star icon if a real click fails." The B brief has no equivalent sentence; B gets the star click_js from the plan. The runtime judgment (a real Marionette click may not be interactable on the star) is not derivable from static fixture HTML; the plan itself says at line 28 that "static HTML cannot establish whether a real Marionette click will be interactable at runtime." So the A brief hands A a deliberated finding as a hint, which slightly favors A. Guard: remove task-specific click advice from the A brief. Generic interactability guidance belongs in the driver reference docstring (which both arms read), not in one arm's brief. Re-freeze both briefs and record their md5s before the run. Two related checks, verified: the localStorage asymmetry does NOT exist (A brief step 3 clears localStorage, and plan step 3 does the same for B), so no change is needed there; record this check in the design so it is not re-litigated. And the B brief makes `__agyVerify` conditional ("including the eval call to window.__agyVerify() if the plan includes it"). Plan step 56 does include it, so this is satisfied in fact, but make the B brief unconditional to match the design rule that PASS/FAIL is decided from DOM reads plus `__agyVerify`.

6. Shared out.log counting flaw (verified from step.sh and the start script). The metrics schema counts "Driver commands run (counted from the executor report + /tmp/e1/out.log lines)", but out.log is one shared file. It accumulates driver startup lines, pre-flight commands for both arms, and executor commands for both arms, unless infra is restarted (which truncates it) between arms. The design does not state an infra restart between arms or per-arm log files, so a count taken from raw out.log lines will overcount. Guard: the executor report (one line per action, already required by both briefs) is the PRIMARY count. Before each arm, archive out.log to out-preflight-<arm>.log and out-<arm>.log boundaries with timestamps, or restart infra between arms and archive out.log per arm. State which one in the frozen design. Also require per-arm codex event files (events-<arm>.jsonl) and count tool calls from that file only, never from a shared stream.

7. Input drift between verification and run. Md5s were verified once at 18:24 (spec, fixture, hand_drive.py, plan), but the arms run later, after Y4d completes. If any input changes in between, the arms could run against different inputs. Guard: re-verify md5s (spec, fixture, hand_drive.py, plan, both briefs) immediately before EACH arm and record them in the arm report. If any md5 differs from the frozen values, stop and re-freeze. Do not run on drifted inputs.

8. Coordinator non-blinding. The coordinator authored the merged plan and will judge B deviations and count errors, knowing which arm is which. Full blinding is impractical with one coordinator, so the mitigation is an audit trail. Guard: the arm reports must be committed unedited before the coordinator writes the verdict, and every deviation, defect, and error in the verdict must cite the report line and plan step number it rests on, so a second reader can audit the call.

9. Token-cost blindness. Speed uses wall time only. Deliberation cost about 463,124 tokens in and 67,995 out (X report) versus A's unknown tokens. A B total-speed win that costs many times the tokens is a different trade from a cheap win. Guard: record tokens in and out per executor (from the codex event stream or final message) and report them alongside wall time. State in the verdict that a speed win does not imply a cost win.

10. Step-granularity speed bias against B. The plan has 58 steps versus the spec's 28. The B brief says "Where the plan gives an exact command, run exactly that command" step by step in order, which reads as one driver call per plan step, while the A brief lets A decide and batch freely. B could pay 58 sequential round-trips where A pays fewer. Guard: clarify in the B brief that consecutive plan commands may be batched into one driver invocation ONLY where the plan's order and check steps are preserved, and never across a check step. Or, alternatively, state explicitly that B must run one command per plan step and accept the granularity cost as part of the treatment, with driver-command counts recorded so the granularity effect is visible. Either is acceptable, but it must be stated, not left ambiguous.

11. Both-fail progress (also Q1 item 4). If both arms fail, "draw at failure" hides which arm got further. Guard: record steps completed before the first unrecovered failure and the final verifier state for both failed arms, and state which arm got further. No flat draw label for failures at different depths.

### Infra appendix and scripts (start/stop, commit c98d6c3)

- I1 (must-fix, sed not verified, ports not fail-closed). The start script runs sed replacing WD_PORT 4450 with 4451 in the generated driver file but never verifies the replacement took. If the pattern does not match (spacing drift), the driver starts on the wrong port, and the stop script (which looks for --port 4451) will miss its geckodriver, leaving it orphaned. The script also prints ss output and continues to INFRA_UP even if port 4451 is not listening. Fix: grep the generated file for WD_PORT = 4451 and exit non-zero if absent; after the 3 s sleep, verify BOTH ports listening and exit non-zero if either is missing.
- I2 (must-fix, start does not fail closed on stale infra). The start script removes PID files without checking whether the old processes are still alive. A start over a still-running old driver orphans the old driver (its PID file is gone). Fix: fail closed if ports 4451/8767 are already bound or if the PID files reference live processes. Require stop-infra.sh to have run clean first.
- I3 (must-fix, kill_pidfile does not verify identity). The stop script's kill_pidfile kills by PID from the pidfile without checking /proc/<pid>/cmdline first. PID reuse could kill the wrong process. Fix: check that the cmdline contains the expected string (hand_drive_e1.py for the driver, http.server 8767 for the server, the holder marker for the holder) before sending TERM. The leftover scan in the same script already does cmdline checks; extend the same check to kill_pidfile.
- I4 (must-fix, holder kill ordering is assumed, not verified). The stop script kills the holder, sleeps 3 s, then kills the driver, on the theory that closing the FIFO holder first lets the driver's finally block delete the session and terminate geckodriver cleanly (SIGTERM would skip finally). But the holder is a bash subshell waiting on sleep 7200, and a non-interactive bash waiting on a child may not exit promptly on TERM, so fd 9 may still be open after 3 s and the driver may still be SIGTERMed with its finally block skipped. Fix: after the holder kill plus sleep, check kill -0 on the holder PID; only kill the driver after the holder is confirmed dead. If the holder is still alive, kill its sleep child or wait longer, and record it. Do not assume 3 s is enough.
- I5 (record). The design appendix says geckodriver PID is "recorded at start via the driver's child table", but the start script records only holder, driver, and http. Geckodriver does not exist until the first new_session, and the stop script finds it at stop time via pgrep -x plus a cmdline port check. Fix the appendix text: geckodriver is found at stop time by pgrep plus cmdline, not recorded at start.
- I6 (record). The design text says the Y8 scripts are "new files in /tmp/e1/". They are committed in the repo under improvements/ (c98d6c3) and transferred to /tmp/e1/. Fix the text to point at the repo copies as authoritative and /tmp/e1/ as the deployed copies.
- I7 (correct, keep). No pkill -f anywhere in the Y8 scripts: verified. The leftover scan uses /proc cmdline plus kill by exact PID, and geckodriver uses pgrep -x plus a cmdline port check plus kill by PID. That is the correct pattern, and it correctly avoids X's pkill -f violation. Credit where due.
- I8 (minor). The stop script's final `pgrep -x geckodriver` will list a Y4 geckodriver if one is running on another port. It does not kill it (correct), but the output could be misread as an E1 leftover. Minor: filter the final check message by port 4451, or note that a listed geckodriver on another port is not E1's.
