## INTERNAL baseline (our own runs, not crawled)

- Hand-driven test: about 7 min over 12 tool calls vs 2,678 ms machine time for the same scripted test. Ratio about 157x. The machine work is negligible; the loop is the cost.
- 10x review finding: the think-observe-think loop is 87.8% of task time.
- Implication: shaving model inference or machine execution barely moves the total. The levers that matter are fewer loop iterations, cheaper observations per iteration, and batched actions per iteration.

## PART A: The think-observe-think loop (priority)

### A1. Observation cost: screenshots are the expensive observation, DOM/ARIA trees are the cheap one

Source 7 (SyndiPath, 2026-11-08, within window) claims a pruned ARIA snapshot is about 320 tokens vs a full-HD PNG vision loop at about 2,400+ tokens, a 7.5x lower token cost, and 0.4-0.9 s per action vs 2.8-5.5 s for vision, about 4x faster end to end, with 99.4% selector precision for ref-based clicks. Backing: MEASUREMENT CLAIM, but vendor blog, no independent replication found today. Treat the direction as solid (it matches every other source below) and the exact multipliers as UNVERIFIED until we measure them on our own stack.

Source 9 (PureTensor bake-off, 2026-09-26, OLDER, 56 days) measured the same model in two harnesses: Qwen3.8-27B scored 37/39 at median 38.2 s inside browser-use with vision on, vs 38/39 at median 12.7 s in a lean screenshot-only loop. Same weights, 3.0x slower at the median in the heavier harness. The DOM-only incumbent (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in browser-use) scored 39/39 but needed four GPUs across two nodes. Also: a single viewport constant moved Holo from 17/26 to 23/26, because headless Chrome at 1280x800 reports a 1280x713 page and a harness assuming 800 places every click about 12% too low. Backing: MEASURED, live tasks, verified end state, no LLM judge. Small sample (39 tasks, one run each), so the top four setups (39, 38, 37, 37) cannot be separated, but the 3.0x same-model harness gap is the load-bearing finding: the loop around the model matters as much as the model.

Source 10 (UI-TARS Desktop writeup, about 2026-10-18, OLDER) puts pure GUI automation at 800-2,500 ms per step for capture plus image tokens plus coordinate prediction, vs under 50 ms for scripted selectors and about 1,500 ms for accessibility-tree LLMs. OSWorld table in the same piece: Anthropic Computer Use (screenshot) 14.9% at about 2,200 ms/step; Axtree (accessibility tree) 12.4% at about 1,500 ms/step. Backing: MEASUREMENT CLAIM, secondhand table, numbers not independently verified today. Direction only.

Source 8 (agentnative.dev, about 2026-08, OLDER) survey: hybrid DOM plus vision leads on the web (Browser Use 89.1% on WebVoyager vs 73.1% for accessibility-tree-only), while a screenshot costs more than 15,000 tokens per shot in their accounting. An accessibility-only agent scored 90.7% on a static search page but 27.3% on a dynamic booking site. OSWorld: leading agents about 12%, best reported 20.58%, humans 72.36%. Backing: SURVEY of benchmarks, mixed measurement quality. The practical rule it supports: tree for action, screenshot for verification and recovery only.

What this means for us: our loop currently observes by screenshot every iteration. That is the most expensive observation available. The cheapest observation that still grounds correctly is a structured tree (DOM/ARIA on web, accessibility tree on desktop). Every iteration that can run on tree-only observation instead of a screenshot saves both tokens and seconds, and the PureTensor result says the harness around the model can cost 3x by itself.

### A2. Think cost: parallel/batched tool calls collapse round trips, 1.8x-3.7x measured

Source 6 (Zylos, 2026-04-26, OLDER) states the sequential bottleneck plainly: five 2-second tool calls serial cost 10 s minimum; parallel collapses to about 2 s plus aggregation. Measured speedups from LLMCompiler-style DAG scheduling: 1.8x latency reduction on HotpotQA, up to 3.7x on parallelizable workflows, up to 6x cost reduction from fewer LLM inference calls due to batching. Production multi-agent pipelines: 36-50% wall-clock reduction from fan-out/fan-in. Backing: MEASURED (cited ICML 2024 LLMCompiler numbers) plus vendor synthesis. The 1.8x/3.7x figures are from the original paper, not re-measured in 2026.

Source 5 (kusuma.dev / ai-tldr.dev / theneuralbase.com, crawled 2026-11-21, dates unclear): parallel tool calling is supported natively by OpenAI (tool_calls array) and Anthropic (multiple tool_use blocks in one turn, default on Claude 4+). Each extra sequential turn re-sends the growing conversation history, so collapsing turns also trims repeated input tokens. One source claims 70-90% latency reduction on parallelizable I/O. Backing: OPINION plus API documentation, no controlled measurement in these posts. The mechanism claim (re-sent history per turn) is consistent with Source 12 below.

What this means for us: one observation should yield as many actions as possible. Our 12 tool calls over 7 minutes were sequential think-act-observe cycles. Batching independent actions into one turn and emitting multiple tool calls per response attacks both the think count and the re-sent-history tax at once. This is the single highest-leverage loop change in the crawl.

### A3. Grounding speed: coordinate accuracy is not the bottleneck, the loop is

Source 9 (PureTensor, 2026-09-26, OLDER): click accuracy separated the top three vision models by only 3 targets out of 72 (UI-Venus-2-9B 95.8%, Holo3.1-35B-A3B 93.1%, Qwen3.8-27B 91.7%), yet on live tasks Holo finished last at 33/39, 4-5 tasks behind. Most Holo failures were errors of judgement and recovery, not missed pixels. Backing: MEASURED. Lesson: chasing grounding accuracy past about 92% buys little; recovery behavior and loop design decide live-task success.

Source 11 (MobiBench via blolabel.ai, 2026-10-21, within window; paper arXiv:2512.12634, v2 2026-01-08): GPT-4.1 scores 27.17% task success reading an Android accessibility tree as text, 4.72% reading a raw screenshot only, 31.50% hybrid (both). GPT-4.1 mini: 5.91% tree only vs 15.94% hybrid (2.7x). Dataset: 508 tasks, 66 apps, 4,173 screenshots, 12,339 annotated actions. Backing: MEASURED, peer-reviewed style benchmark. Caveat: this measures which input helps the agent choose its next action, not verification. It supports hybrid input (tree primary, screenshot as supplement) rather than screenshot-only.

### A4. Context rot: long loops degrade the thinker, not just the bill

Source 13 (Xia et al., "Diagnosing and Mitigating Context Rot in Long-horizon Search", 2026-10-27, within window): under extensive context, models give up or give uncertain incorrect answers long before exhausting the window. Context management methods work by decreasing peak context length, redirecting models from inconclusive failures toward further exploration. Parallel sampling with behavior-aware filtering gained 2.6%-4.9% across three aggregation methods. Backing: MEASURED, four flagship models, three benchmarks.

Source 14 (Martin and Roger, "Classifier Context Rot", arXiv:2605.12366, submitted 2026-05-12, OLDER): Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1 miss dangerous actions 2x-30x more often after 800K tokens of benign activity than on their own. Opus 4.6 recall fell from 98.6% to 88% on MonitorBench with 800K benign tokens prepended, and from 99.7% to 69% for a blatant attack inserted in an 800K transcript vs 100K. Periodic reminders partially mitigate. Backing: MEASURED.

Source 15 (Mittal, "How Fast Do Agents Rot?", about 2026-09, OLDER): task success follows a geometric law in per-step reliability; every model tested, including deployed proprietary systems, falls from near-perfect to near zero within 16 steps on the agentic task (n=10,664 trajectories). Degradation is driven by step count, not context length: bounding the context window steepened decay (logit slope -0.69 vs -0.44), contradicting a lost-in-the-middle explanation. Backing: MEASURED, nine models, controlled study.

What this means for us: a 12-call loop is already in the degradation zone. Shortening the loop (fewer steps, batched actions) is not only faster, it is more reliable per step. And bounding context by compaction can make things worse if it drops state, which connects directly to Part B handoff loss.

### A5. Code-exec instead of step-by-step GUI: the scripted baseline is 157x faster (INTERNAL)

Our own INTERNAL measurement: the same test took 2,678 ms scripted vs about 7 min hand-driven. No crawled source today gives a directly comparable scripted-vs-GUI-agent number, so this ratio is OURS, not a literature claim. The nearest crawled support is Source 10 (scripted selectors under 50 ms/step vs 800-2,500 ms/step for vision GUI agents, a 16x-50x per-step gap) and Source 7 (0.4-0.9 s vs 2.8-5.5 s per action). Both point the same way: whenever a step can be done by code or CLI instead of pixels, do it by code. The priority ladder in our own standing rules (CLI first, then code-exec, then vision plus mouse) is consistent with every measured source found today.

## PART B: Other bottlenecks (existing brief)

### B1. Verification/QC: cheap to produce, expensive to verify, and humans overrate the check

Source 1 (AWS Builder, "When Human-in-the-Loop Becomes Theater", 2026-05-29, OLDER): introduces Verification Complexity Ratio, VCR = time to verify / time to produce. VCR under 0.5: HITL fine. 0.5-1.0: on the edge. At or above 1.0: HITL is theater. Enterprise deployments observed: 8-40 seconds to review a change the AI produced in about the same time, where the same artifact would take a human 4-30 minutes to build. A 200-line change produced in 15 seconds gets approved in 20 seconds, and nothing real was verified. Backing: ANECDOTAL field observation (author says so explicitly, "not a survey"), plus cited Bowman et al. 2022 on scalable oversight. The VCR framing is useful; the thresholds are pragmatic cutoffs from three deployments, not theory.

Source 2 (qa.tech, "The Full AI Cost of Shipping a Feature", figures checked 2026-11-16, within window): the cost of shipping a feature is four stages, generate, review, verify in the running product, watch in production, and token spend on generation is the only stage with a clear invoice line. No public benchmark exists for what the other three should cost. Claude Code enterprise average about $13 per developer per active day, $150-250 per developer per month, 90% under $30 per active day. Pragmatic Engineer October survey of 27 workplaces: a two-person startup spends $3,000 per developer per month while a 1,500-engineer public company caps at $150. LeadDev: 58% of orgs measure AI impact by token usage, 57% say that is ineffective at gauging real value. Backing: MIXED, vendor figures plus surveys, no controlled measurement of verification cost itself. The key point stands: verification cost is real, unmeasured, and invisible in the token bill.

Source 3 (ErgoStrat, ROI of one AI agent task, crawled 2026-11-21): cost per case must count review minutes, fixes, hand-backs, and errors that reach the customer, divided by completed cases including every failed attempt. METR RCT cited: 16 developers, 246 real issues, tasks took 19% longer with AI. Backing: SECONDHAND citation of METR, method guidance rather than new measurement.

### B2. Are agents actually faster? Measured: often no, and people feel faster anyway

Source 4 (METR RCT, Becker et al., July 2025, OLDER; re-analysis arXiv Oct 2026, within window; CACM summary 2026-11-09, within window): 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real issues, Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7. Tasks took 19% longer with AI (95% CI +2% to +39%). Developers predicted a 24% speedup beforehand and still believed they were 20% faster afterward. Re-analysis of the same public data: slowdown of 19% or 24% depending on estimand (34% unadjusted), and AI helped on somewhere between 3% and 82% of issues. METR February 2026 follow-up (57 developers, 143 repos, 800+ tasks): estimated 18% speedup for returning developers (CI 38% faster to 9% slower) and 4% for new ones, but METR itself calls the signal unreliable due to selection bias (30-50% avoided submitting tasks they expected AI to speed up, pay dropped $150 to $50/hr). Backing: MEASURED, randomized controlled trial, the strongest evidence in this crawl. Scope: experienced maintainers in their own large codebases, early-2025 tools. Do not generalize to all developers or current tools, but do not dismiss it either: perception vs measurement gap of about 40 points is the finding that matters for how we measure our own loop.

### B3. Handoffs lose information, mostly through repeated compression, not through changing agents

Source 16 (zicq.com summary of handoff paper, about 2026-11-17, within window): 56 interactive worlds with hidden rules, single agent keeping full history across eight lives vs a chain of eight agents inheriting a 300-character note. Across six models, the chain reaches only the learning level the persistent agent reaches after about four lives. Controlled variants: changing the agent causes little or no loss, a single compression causes a smaller loss, repeated compression causes the largest loss. Rewriting each note from the complete record instead of from the previous note recovers 70-82% of the lost learning at the same 300-character budget. Backing: MEASURED experiment, secondhand summary; the underlying paper was not read in full today, so treat the 70-82% figure as reported, not independently verified.

Source 17 (Anchan and Sen, "Attention Tax, Handoff Tax", arXiv:2610.06069, Oct 2026, within window): stylised reliability model with two tradeoffs. Decomposition becomes preferable once the attention cost avoided by resetting context exceeds the handoff cost; parallel sampling wins when its shared-failure floor lies below one agent thinking longer. On a ledger-reconciliation task they measure the context-degradation curve and handoff tax, place the crossover at depth 10, predict decomposition wins at depths 20, 50, 100, and it does, with predicted success landing within 9 percentage points of actual at every depth. Backing: MEASURED on one task plus theory. The crossover-at-depth-10 number is task-specific, not universal.

Source 18 (Madani Agency field study, 14 deployments, Jan 2025-Apr 2026, OLDER): 12 of 14 multi-agent production deployments rolled back or abandoned, an 86% failure or rollback rate; 2 remain production-stable, 7 rolled back to single-agent, 5 abandoned. Context dilution dominant in 11 of 14. Engineers responding by adding more structure to handoffs (JSON schemas, validations) compounded the problem, increasing total token spend 1.4x-2.8x over the single-thread baseline. Backing: FIELD STUDY, structured interviews plus audit root-cause analysis, 14 deployments across 11 EU companies. Small sample, self-selected, and the paper's framing is openly contrarian (steel-manning Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents"). Treat 86% as a warning flag from one field study, not a base rate.

Source 19 (McEntire, "Hop Scaling", Mar 2026, OLDER): chain degradation saturates rather than compounding exponentially: coordination error rises from 0.503 at hop 1 to about 0.60 by hop 5, then plateaus through hop 10. Fencing at every hop vs only the terminal hop is identical (0.598 vs 0.597 at 10 hops), so intermediate fences are pure overhead. Backing: MEASURED, but on Qwen 2.5-7B with a synthetic coordination metric, narrow scope.

Net: more agents help only past a depth crossover (about depth 10 on one measured task), and every handoff should be written from full history, not from the previous summary. Recursive summarization is the loss mechanism.

### B4. Token burn: input dominates, re-sent context is most of the bill

Source 12 (tokenade.net, Claude Code token usage statistics 2026, about 2026-10-16, within window): a typical 50-turn session burns about 1,000,000 input tokens and 40,000 output tokens, a 25:1 ratio. Input drives about 85% of session cost because the agent re-reads its whole context on every tool call. Re-sent context is about 62% of the bill at production teams audited in 2026. Prompt-cache reads cost 5% of fresh input on Opus 5.5 and 10% on Sonnet/Haiku. Uber burned its entire 2026 AI budget in four months after Claude Code adoption reached 84% across 5,000 engineers, at $500-2,000 per heavy user per month. Backing: MIXED, compiled from primary sources with citations, but the Uber figure and the 62% figure are secondhand and UNVERIFIED here. The 25:1 ratio and the re-read mechanism are consistent with Source 6 and with our own loop behavior.

Source 20 (last30days engine, 2026-11-21): HN thread 2026-10-29 (591 points, 236 comments) reports Claude Code regularly sends each background agent transcript to its model, burning tokens for no good reason; one commenter on Max 20x reports quota at 98%. Backing: COMMUNITY REPORT, gist plus comments, not independently verified. Direction matches Source 12: background agents multiply re-sent context.

### B5. Stall detection: liveness is not progress, the watchdog must be a separate process

Source 21 (baeseokjae.github.io, "Agent Walking-Dead State Detection", about 2026-09-23, OLDER): a walking-dead agent keeps running, consumes tokens, passes every liveness check, while producing no durable progress. July 2026 preprint "When Agents Do Not Stop" scanned 6,549 public LLM-agent repos and manually confirmed 68 infinite-loop failures across 47 projects. Detection requires a progress signal (lastProgressAt, monotonic step counter, semantic checkpoints), not a heartbeat. A work-loop heartbeat emitted from inside the main loop only when it advances; a process-level heartbeat on a background thread returns 200 even after the main loop stalls. The watchdog must run as a separate process, not inside the agent. Backing: MIXED, practitioner guide citing a real preprint scan; the 68-failure count is secondhand.

Source 22 (bistecglobal/blog issue 2, "Heartbeat Watchdog Pattern", 2026-06-20, OLDER): three stall patterns, tool-incomplete (tool_use with no matching tool_result), question-unanswered (assistant ends with "?" and no user reply), process-crashed. Detection from the .jsonl transcript, last 200 entries. Guard: transcript modified within the last 30 seconds means still active, skip analysis. Stale threshold default 60 minutes, stuck threshold base 5 minutes at process level. Backing: PRODUCTION IMPLEMENTATION report, one Discord bot fleet, not a controlled study.

Source 23 (zemna.net, "Your AI Agent Pipeline Has No Zombie Detection", 2026-06-24, OLDER): four zombie patterns, infinite wait, compaction loop, subagent black hole, rate-limit sleep. Braintrust traced one production loop costing 274 LLM calls, 91,547 tokens, $1.38 before anyone noticed. Fix: hard wall-clock timeout per task (p95 completion plus margin), heartbeat interval, artifact freshness checks. Backing: PRACTITIONER report, one traced loop cited secondhand.

Source 24 (neuramesh-oss docs, stall watchdog, built 2026-07-08, OLDER): watchdog pass every 5 minutes, full pass every 15 minutes. Stall classes with absolute ages against now, never since-last-sweep: feedback_unactioned after 10 min, stalled_active when no run steps/messages/beats/transitions. A row still reading "running" while writing nothing is flagged explicitly. Backing: PRODUCTION IMPLEMENTATION, one system, code-enforced detection.

Net: our own standing rule (dedicated watcher, poll every 5 min, poke and restart after 15 silent min, watchdog reports to Muse not to the coordinator) matches all four sources. The refinement they add: watch progress markers (steps, artifacts, beats), not process liveness; and the 5-minute poll / 15-minute action cadence is the same as neuramesh's.

## What actually shrinks the 87.8% (ranked by evidence strength)

1. Batch independent actions per turn and run tool calls in parallel. Measured 1.8x-3.7x on parallelizable work (Source 6), plus it cuts the re-sent-history tax (Sources 5, 12). One observation should yield many actions. This is the top lever.
2. Observe with the tree, verify with the screenshot. ARIA/DOM observation is claimed 4x faster and 7.5x cheaper per step than screenshots (Source 7), and the same-model harness gap was 3.0x (Source 9). Reserve screenshots for verification and recovery, where pixels are the only truth.
3. Replace GUI steps with code-exec or CLI wherever possible. Scripted selectors run under 50 ms/step vs 800-2,500 ms/step for vision GUI (Source 10); our own INTERNAL ratio is 157x for the full test. CLI first, code-exec second, vision last.
4. Shorten the loop, not just speed it up. Reliability collapses within about 16 steps (Source 15) and context rot grows with length (Sources 13, 14). Fewer steps is a reliability fix as well as a speed fix.
5. Write handoffs from full history, never from the previous summary. Recursive compression is the main loss mechanism; rewriting from the full record recovers 70-82% of lost learning (Source 16). Do not add more agents below the depth crossover (about depth 10 on the one measured task, Source 17); 12 of 14 production multi-agent deployments in one field study were rolled back or abandoned (Source 18).
6. Measure wall time and perception separately. METR shows people feel 20% faster while measuring 19% slower (Source 4). Our loop metric must be total wall time per task, timed and logged, never self-reported speed.
7. Detect stalls by progress, not liveness. Separate watchdog process, 5-minute poll, 15-minute action, progress markers (steps, artifacts, beats), hard wall-clock timeout at p95 plus margin (Sources 21-24). This matches our standing rule; no change needed, only enforcement.
8. Treat verification as a budgeted stage, not a free check. VCR at or above 1.0 means human review is theater (Source 1). Use independent QC with its own measurements, and count review minutes in cost per task (Sources 2, 3).
